TYPE THE GODS — CORRECTLY.

The PuniCodex app puts a scholarly Unicode keyboard in your pocket: 980 mythological names restored from the sources, 7,728 symbols from twenty-five writing systems, and a living temple behind every name. Write apóllōn.com in any app on your phone — diacritics and macrons intact.

The Scholarly Unicode Keyboard

A real input method — usable in any app on your phone. Type in the restored spellings with long-press accents, browse curated palettes of scholarly scripts, and let the suggestion strip offer the correct restoration as you type. This is the feature the whole site exists for, finally where it belongs: under your thumbs.

Compose, Then Convert

Write freely in any language. The app spots mythological names in what you have written and offers one-tap conversion to their Unicode restoration — no hunting through palettes when you already know the story.

The Lexicon, Offline

Every restoration — breakdown, original script, pronunciation, etymology, its temple — lives on the device from first launch. A mountain hut, a long flight, a dead zone: the pantheon answers anyway.

The Shield, In Your Pocket

The same Authenticity engine that guards the browser extension rides in the app. Paste a name or a link — or hand it one from any app's share sheet — and the verdict arrives with its evidence attached: which code points lie, and why, never a bare color.

WHERE IT STANDS

  1. Today: the Android app has graduated to production on Google Play — the native keyboard, compose mode, the offline lexicon, and the Shield, wrapped in a Capacitor shell with its own tuned dark interface.
  2. Next: open release, then the iOS shell — the same codebase, the same keyboard, Apple's review willing.
  3. Growing: the AR lens in the web app points the way — camera-based recognition of inscriptions is the long game, and the web build already carries its scaffold.
