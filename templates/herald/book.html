Editor's Note

Why the first edition is different

Future issues of The Unicode Herald will be shaped by the sponsors and patrons who appear inside individual temples — short modern stories that connect a brand's work to a deity's lore, industry patterns that show where ancient names still resonate, and creative commissions produced by university collaborators. This first edition is different because those pages are still blank.

What you hold instead is a record of construction: the three months between the first domain registration on 25 May 2026 and the release of this issue on 1 September 2026. It traces the growth of the pantheon from just over 200 flagship temples to {{SPREAD_COUNT}}, the addition of Aboriginal, Baltic, Hittite, Korean, Mapuche, and Slavic flagships alongside the older traditions, and the design decisions that keep the lore free of intrusive advertising.

The gazetteer that follows devotes two pages to every built flagship. Each spread carries the restored Unicode name, original script or scholarly transliteration, mascot, logomark, wordmark, pronunciation, meaning, and a short scholarly note. It is the first printed map of the entire fleet.
