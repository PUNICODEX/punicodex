01

Mythology

Stories of Tantra

Tantra’s mythology is its own theology of secrecy and speed: the god teaching the Goddess, the guru initiating the disciple, and the householder’s body becoming the ritual ground. Its stories are the scriptures’ frames — Śiva on Mount Kailāsa answering Pārvatī’s midnight questions, the siddhas flying on mantra-power, the kings protected by Śaiva rites.

Tantric Frame

The God Teaches the Goddess

Nearly every tantra opens on a mountain: Śiva, asked by the Goddess how liberation may be quick for the poor in merit and short in life, answers with the system. The gender of the exchange is the doctrine — the god is knowledge, the Goddess is energy, and the tantra is their union written down for the age of decline.

Kaula & Śaiva Cycles

The Clan-Traditions

The early Śaiva tantras organised themselves as kulas, clans — initiatory lineages bound to a deity, a mantra, and a guru. The clans’ mythology made heroes of the siddhas, the perfected ones who flew, stayed young, and turned the elements — the tantras’ advertisements and their cautionary tales.

Vajrayāna Cycle

The Thunderbolt Vehicle

Buddhism absorbed the tantras wholesale: the Vajrayāna made the Buddha speak as Vajradhara, the thunderbolt-holder, and taught that buddhahood could be won in a single lifetime. The Tibetan canons — Kanjur and Tanjur — are the largest single body of tantric scripture preserved anywhere.

Royal Protections

The State of the Rites

Medieval kings ruled by tantra: the Śaiva rites of protection (rakṣā) and the pacificatory rites (śānti) were statecraft. The inscriptions of the Cōḻas and the Pālas record land-grants to tantric monasteries that anchored their realms — the esoteric schools as the polity’s immune system.

The Loom of Esoteric Doctrine

The Extended System, Scripture and Practice

Tantra is the name of a scripture-class and the ritual systems those scriptures teach: works cast as dialogues between Śiva and the Goddess, embracing mantra, mandala, initiation, and the disciplined transgression of ordinary purity-rules. From the sixth-century Śaiva scriptures to Tibetan Vajrayāna, the tantras are the Indian religions’ most ambitious technology for accelerated transformation.

The Scripture-Dialogue

The tantra is cast as Śiva’s answer to the Goddess’s questions — god and power, doctrine and energy, in conversation.

The Maṇḍala

The deity’s palace-diagram, constructed from coloured sands or drawn, entered by initiation through the four gates.

Mantra and Mudrā

Sound and gesture: the mantra pronounced, the hand-seal struck — the body made instrument of the doctrine.

Transgression as Method

The pañcamakāra — the five M’s — consumed in ritual under vow, inverting purity to burn through it.

04

The Name

From the original script to the living URL

Original Script

तन्त्र

The name in its original Sanskrit form. Tantra (तन्त्र) is attested in the source tradition — “A class of works teaching magical and mystical formularies, mostly cast as dialogues between Śiva and Durgā; hence the esoteric systems of doctrine and ritual founded upon them.”. Its original diacritics and script distinctions carry the full phonetic and orthographic weight of the source tradition.

ASCII Constraint

tantra

The plain tantra form shares the canonical spelling. Because this name is already written in Latin letters, no recovered diacritics are required; capitalization follows the proper-noun convention.

Canonical Restoration

Tantra

The canonical spelling of Tantra needs no recovered marks. Its value is consistent citation, transparent provenance, and a stable digital home for the scholarly record. The address is readable as-is to both DNS and humanity.

ASCII-Clean Name
Tantra.com

This restoration is written entirely in ASCII letters — no special domain encoding stands between Tantra and the DNS. The domain reads identically to machines and to human eyes.

Pronunciation

How Tantra was spoken

/ˈtʌntrə/ Sanskrit (IAST: Tantra)
Tan- From √tan, “to stretch, extend, weave” — the same root as “tan” and “tend”.
-tra The instrumental suffix: the tantra is the “loom” or “warp” — the extended doctrine.
The word names both the scripture-class and the ritual systems those scriptures teach.

Modern Approximation

“TUN-truh”

The full word family — cognates, derivatives, and variant spellings — is traced in Extended Lore → Etymology.

The Accent / Script Rule

The word is a weaving-metaphor: the tantra is the warp into which the practice’s threads are stretched.
