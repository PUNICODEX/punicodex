Pronunciation

How Rꜥ was spoken

/'raːʕ/ Egyptian Reconstruction
R- Voiced alveolar flap or trill — the r of Egyptian, lighter than the rolled r of Arabic, sharper than the English r. It is the sound of the sun's disk touching the horizon — a quick, bright burst before the full roar.
-ꜥ Pharyngeal fricative — the ayin, a voiced sound produced deep in the throat, where the root of the tongue presses against the pharynx. No modern European language has this sound. It is the hum of creation, the vibration of the primordial waters, the voice of the god who spoke the world into being. To say it correctly is to feel the throat open like a flower toward the sun.

Modern Approximation

"RAH-ah" — with the second syllable produced deep in the throat, almost a growl, a vibration that you feel rather than hear clearly. The r is light, quick. The ꜥ is the challenge — it is the sound of clearing your throat while voicing, of humming from the chest.

04

The Myths

Stories of creation, darkness, and the eternal cycle

The Beginning

The Self-Created

Before the world, there was only Nun — the primordial waters, dark, infinite, without form. No sky. No earth. No gods. Only the dark water and the potential within it. And from that water, — rose — alone, self-created, the first consciousness in a universe of silence. Some say he emerged as a lotus from the water. Some say he was born from an egg laid by the primeval goose. Some say he simply was — that he spoke his own name and by speaking it, created himself. And then he spoke again. And the world answered. He named Shu, the air, and the air separated the waters. He named Tefnut, moisture, and the rain fell. He named Geb, the earth, and the land rose. He named Nut, the sky, and the stars burned. He is the only god who created himself. Every other god is his creation.

The Journey

The Barge of the Sun

Each day, Rꜥ sails the Mandjet barge across the sky — the "Boat of Millions of Years." The gods accompany him. Ma'at stands at the prow. Thoth records the journey. Horus steers. The cobra Mehen coils around the solar disk to protect it. The journey takes twelve hours, from dawn to dusk. And at the western horizon, — descends into the underworld — the Duat — to begin the night voyage in the Mesekhet barge. Through twelve hours of darkness, through twelve gates guarded by serpents and demons, through the realm of the dead where Osiris judges souls. And in the deepest hour — the seventh — Apep, the serpent of chaos, rises from the waters to swallow the sun. But — is armed with magic, with fire, with the power of his own name. He defeats Apep. He emerges at the eastern horizon. Every dawn is a victory over chaos. Every sunrise is the proof that order endures.

The Wrath

The Eye of Ra

Rꜥ grew old. His bones turned to silver, his flesh to gold, his hair to lapis lazuli. Mankind grew bold, speaking against him, plotting rebellion. — sent his Eye — the cobra goddess Wadjet, the flame that strikes from above — to punish them. She became Sekhmet, the lioness, the most terrible of all goddesses. She descended upon Egypt and slaughtered mankind. She waded in their blood. She drank it. She revelled in it. The Nile ran red. The desert ran red. And —, seeing what he had wrought, wept. He sent messengers to fetch red ochre and seven thousand jars of beer. The beer was dyed with the ochre to resemble blood. It was poured across the land. Sekhmet drank it, thinking it was blood, and became drunk. She slept for three days. When she woke, her rage had passed. But the lesson remained: the sun gives life, but the sun also takes it. And the line between them is as thin as the edge of a cobra's fang.

The Secret

The Destruction of Mankind

In the beginning, Rꜥ ruled directly over mankind. He walked among them, spoke with them, taught them the arts of civilization. But they grew weary of his voice. They grew resentful of his power. They plotted against him. — learned of their plot from Nut, the sky, who heard their whispers. He summoned the gods to council. "Shall I destroy them?" he asked. The gods were silent. Hathor spoke first: "Let them live. They are your creation." But Sekhmet — the Eye, the flame — had already left. She was already among them, already burning. — tried to call her back. He could not. The destruction was too far along. He could only stop it with the beer, with the sleep, with the mercy that comes too late. And from that day, — withdrew from the world. He ascended to the sky. He no longer walks among men. He only sails above them — distant, eternal, untouchable. The sun still warms. But the sun no longer speaks.
