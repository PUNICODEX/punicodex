Huángdì walks into a ancestry & heritage services firm

Walk into any ancestry & heritage services outfit and watch for ten minutes. You will see a very old act performed with newer props. The act has a name. It is Huángdì.

Before the Office, the Story

Xuānyuán of the Bear Clan. “The Yellow Emperor was a son of Shǎodiǎn, surnamed Gōngsūn, and his name was Xuānyuán. ” So the Shiji opens the first reign of Chinese history (ch. 1): born head of the Yǒuxióng, the Bear clan, praised in the formula of sage-birth legend — “born divine, he spoke early, and as an infant was quick-witted” — and handed an army, not a cradle.

Banquan and Zhuolu. The founding required two victories. First, three battles at Banquan, after which “the hosts of the world gave allegiance to him where the Flame Emperor was concerned” (Shiji 1): Yándì, defeated, became not an enemy but the first subject — and, in time, the twin ancestor beside him. Then the great battle: Chīyóu of the Jiǔlí raised a fog over the field at Zhuolu, and the army lost its way — later tradition answered with the south-pointing chariot that always faced south, however the carriage turned.

Not the First Rebrand

Huángdì's true temple is a library — the most syncretic figure in the Chinese canon, claimed by every school that needed a founder. The Zhuāngzǐ made him the philosophical sovereign who must unlearn rule; the early Han made him a political program: the Huáng-Lǎo 黃老 school, “the Yellow Emperor and Lǎozǐ,” which guided the recovered economy of Emperors Wén and Jǐng, and whose lost scriptures — the Four Classics of the Yellow Emperor, recovered from the Mawangdui silk library in 1973 — show how literally the school took him as lawgiver. The office is only the latest translation.
