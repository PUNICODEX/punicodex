Mythology

The Skanda myth is the war-god’s birth-saga: the gods, unable to defeat Tāraka, whose boon made him unkillable except by a son of Śiva, contrived the god’s marriage ​to Pārvatī; the child was born as fire from the third eye, fostered by the Pleiades, and general of heaven before he was a week old. The Tamil Murugan traditions add the ‌wooing of Vaḷḷi, the huntress, and the gnostic doctrine of the Arupadaiveedu, the six stations of grace.

The Birth of Fire (Kumāra Cycle)

When Agni and the river Ganges could not carry the ​god’s seed, they left it in the reeds of the Gangā’s bank; six infants were found there by the six Kṛttikā nymphs, each nursing one — until Pārvatī gathered them into a single ‌six-headed child. His first week closed with the death of Tāraka, slain by the spear his mother gave him.

The Deva-Senāpati (Skanda Purāṇa)

The great Purāṇa bearing his name makes him general ​of the gods’ hosts and husband of Devasenā — ‘the army of the gods’ — a marriage arranged before his birth. His camp is on the world’s margin; his peacock mount, the demon ‌Siṃhakeśin subdued, struts beneath his banner.

Vaḷḷi and Teyvāyāṉai (Murugan Cycle)

The Tamil epics tell how Murugan wooed the huntress Vaḷḷi in the guise of a tree-shade, and wed both her and ​Teyvāyāṉai, Indra’s daughter, in the great marriage at Tiruchendur. The legend’s two wives read theologically: divine grace (Teyvāyāṉai) and the soul’s own effort (Vaḷḷi) are both needed for union with the lord.

The ‌Sage-Child (Subrahmaṇya Cycle)

As Subrahmaṇya — ‘the dear one to the Brahmins’ — he is the teacher-god, expounding the Vedas to his own father Śiva in the role of guru. The image ​flips the war-god into wisdom’s child, and fixes his southern temples as places of learning as much as of arms.

Across Cultures

Skanda is the great pan-Indian convergence: Vedic Rudra’s son, Tamil Murugan, Buddhist Kārttikeya of the north, and the Graha of the week’s Tuesday. ​The Greeks of the Northwest compared him to Ares and Mars; the Kushan coins show him as Skanda-Kumāra beside the Buddha. His peacock entered Christian art’s immortality symbolism by the strangest of ‌roads.

Within the Sanskrit tradition, closely related names in the corpus include Aditi, Agni, Ambikā, Aṃśa, Arjuna, and Bhakti.

Cultural Legacy

Murugan is Tamil country’s beloved god — the Kanda Ṣaṣṭhī festival at ​Tiruchendur draws millions, and the god’s hill-shrines anchor Tamil sacred geography. His six-headed infant image, the Kārttikeya of Gupta art, defined the war-god’s iconography for Asia; and the Ṣaṣṭhī vow is observed ​across the subcontinent by parents of sons.
