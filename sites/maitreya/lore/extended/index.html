Etymology & Word Family

From original script to Unicode restoration

Original Script मैत्रेय Maitreya — "'The Benevolent One' — the Bodhisattva who will return as the future Buddha"
Unicode Restoration Maitreya Canonical scholarly spelling — ASCII-identical
Modern ASCII maitreya Plain-ASCII fallback

The name Maitreya reaches the modern world through scholarly transliteration of the buddhist tradition. Because the conventional spelling uses only Latin letters, its Unicode and ASCII forms coincide — the tradition it carries is in the name itself, not in diacritics.

Derivatives & Descendants

Etymological Kin

  • Sanskrit — maitrī 'friendliness, benevolence,' from mitra 'friend' — Maitreya is the 'Benevolent One,' the abstraction made bodhisattva (Monier-Williams)
  • Pāli — Metteyya — the form in which the prophecy of DN 26 names the future Buddha
  • Indo-Iranian — mitra, 'friend, contract' — the shared root behind the Vedic god Mitra and Avestan Miθra, friendship divinized in two religions

04

Cultural Significance

From ancient cult to modern memory

Maitreya in Later Traditions

Maitreya's reception is the strangest career of any bodhisattva. In India he absorbed the iconography of a future world-ruler — crowned, princely, seated European-style with pendant legs, a god of the age to come. Central Asian Islam met him on the cave walls of Dunhuang and kept his silhouette in manuscript illumination. China fused him with the folk saint Budai and made the laughing fat monk his universal form; Korea and Japan inherited both the regal Maitreza/Miroku of the temples and the round-bellied Hotei of the gate. In Japan, Miroku became the focus of sundered-age hope — the mappō centuries looked to him as the one who comes when Śākyamuni's law has faded. Modern scholarship (Sponberg & Hardacre's Maitreya, the Future Buddha) treats him as the closest Buddhism came to a messiah: not a savior from outside history, but its next chapter.

Modern Legacy

No Buddhist figure is more painted. The colossal Maitreya niches of Bamiyan — one of the two great Buddhas was identified with him — dominated the Silk Road until their destruction in 2001; the third phase of Borobudur and the cave temples of Bingling and Maijishan climb toward his heaven. The Anāgatavaṃsa kept his cult alive in Sri Lanka, and Burmese and Thai Buddhism still recite the prophecy of his coming. In East Asia, Budai-Maitreya smiles from restaurant windows and temple gates, and the word for his sack of plenty entered the language of New Year greetings. The laughing form is the deeper truth of the name: a future guaranteed not by wrath but by friendliness — maitrī itself, scheduled to arrive.

In the Earth

Maitreya is among the earliest bodhisattvas imaged in stone. Kuṣāṇa-period Gandhāra (2nd–3rd c. CE) gives him his classic type: princely jewelry, stūpa-in-crown, water flask, the wheel at his feet; Mathurā workshops add the dharma-cakra-mudrā preaching pose. The colossi scale up along the Silk Road — the 55-meter Bamiyan east niche (6th–7th c., destroyed 2001) and the 27-meter Maitreya of Bingling Si's cave 171. In China the earliest datable Maitreya image (the gilt bronze of 443 CE in the Asian Art Museum tradition) and the great Northern Wei votive steles mark the cult's imperial sponsorship. Inscriptions from Swat to Khotan name him Metr — the future Buddha already receiving donations in his own lifetime's echo.

05

The Meditation

What Maitreya means — beyond the dictionary

Every religion promises an end; Buddhism alone promises a sequel. Maitreya is the argument that decline is not the last chapter — that the Dharma, having dimmed, will be re-taught in full, by a teacher already named, already enthroned in the heaven of delight. The figure dissolves despair without denying decay: the wheel does turn down, and it turns up again.

He is also the most human of the great bodhisattvas because his career is still open. The Buddha's story is closed and perfect; Maitreya's is a promise with a waiting room, and the tradition placed Śākyamuni himself in that room before his final birth. To venerate Maitreya is to admit that one's own age is incomplete — and to refuse to treat incompleteness as defeat. The future Buddha is Buddhism's answer to the oldest anxiety: what if it all ends with us? Not, says the prophecy. It ends, and it begins again, kinder.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about Maitreya, Future Buddha, Loving-Kindness, and Unicode restoration

01How do you pronounce Maitreya?

In reconstructed pronunciation, Maitreya is /ˈmɛːɪ̯.tɾe.jɐ/ — approximately 'MIGH-tray-yuh' — stress forward on the diphthong; the name is built on maitrī, 'loving-kindness,' so it should sound warm, not martial.

02What does Maitreya mean?

Maitreya means 'The Benevolent One' — the Bodhisattva who will return as the future Buddha in the buddhist tradition.

03What are the symbols of Maitreya?

Maitreya is associated with The stūpa in the crown (Gandhāran iconography crowns him with a small stūpa — Śākyamuni's relic-shrine marking Maitreya as the appointed successor), The water flask (kamaṇḍalu) (The āmra-ghataka of the princely ascetic — the elixir of the future age, medicine for a world whose Dharma has faded), The wheel of Dharma (Set beneath his feet on Kushan reliefs — the teaching he will turn again, three assemblies strong), The nāga-puṣpikā tree (The tree under which he will awaken in one night at Ketumatī — the future's Bodhi tree, named in the prophecy), The cloth sack (Budai) (His Chinese transformation: the laughing monk with his sack of sweets, the future Buddha as abundance and belly-laugh).

04Why restore Maitreya in Unicode?

Maitreya is written entirely in ASCII letters, so its restoration is about canonical fidelity rather than recovered diacritics: the temple fixes the name in its scholarly standard spelling, attested in the source tradition, instead of leaving it to drift.

05What is the most important myth about Maitreya?

“Metteyya at the End of the Decline” — Maitreya's mythology is unique in the Buddhist world: it is scheduled but unfulfilled — prophecy rather than biography. The canon names him, the Mahāyāna sūtras detail his future career, and Asia's art made him the most visible future the ancient world ever gave a face. The full telling lives on the lore page.
