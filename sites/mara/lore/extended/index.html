Etymology & Word Family

From original script to Unicode restoration

Original Script मार Māra — "‘death, the tempter’, the personification of death and desire who assailed the Buddha under the Bodhi tree (from √mṛ ‘to die’), MW."
Unicode Restoration Māra Restored stress, length, and script
Modern ASCII mara Plain-ASCII fallback

Māra derives from √mṛ, "to die" (Monier-Williams): "death, the killer" — the personification of everything that kills the spiritual life, from desire to death itself. The Pāli canon also knows him as Namuci (an old Vedic name), Pāpimā, "the Evil One," and Kaṇha, "the Dark One." The restoration's whole content is the long ā: the difference between "Mara" and Māra is the difference between a name and the word for death. Tier-1 by rule.

04

Cultural Significance

From ancient cult to modern Unicode

Ancient Domain

Māra is Death wearing every disguise the mind can manufacture: the lord of the desire-realm who marched his ten armies against the Buddha under the Bodhi tree, sent his three daughters when the armies broke, and lost — not to a weapon, but to being recognized.

Māra in Later Traditions

His oldest document is Vedic: Namuci, the asura of the Ṛgveda whom Indra slew with the foam of the waters, survived in the Pāli canon as Māra's other name — a borrowing as old as the texts themselves. Indian religion lent him Kāma's iconography, the sugarcane bow and flower arrows of the love-god, for desire was his first army. The scholastics then multiplied him: the Mahāyāna handbooks count four Māras — the aggregates, the defilements, death, and the god — turning the tempter into a complete theory of bondage. And the comparison with Satan, inevitable since the first missionaries, is inexact in the instructive way: Māra is no eternal adversary but a god of the highest desire-realm heaven, the Vasavattī, himself mortal, himself subject to the wheel — the ceiling of samsāra, not its enemy.

Modern Legacy

Māra gave Buddhism its psychology of the inner adversary: modern teachers from forest masters to mindfulness clinicians read the ten armies as the mind's own defenses against stillness, and "the Blessed One knows me" as the moment every compulsion loses power — when it is seen. His assault is the most-painted scene in Buddhist art, from Gandhāran panels to the murals of a thousand Thai wats, where the earth-goddess Thoranī wrings a flood from her hair to drown his host. And his name still does its original work: in the teaching languages of three continents, māra remains the common noun for what kills.

Unicode Restoration as Cultural Act

Restoring Māra in a domain name is more than orthographic accuracy. It is a statement that the internet should recognize the full range of human writing — not only the ASCII keyboard.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about Māra, Death, Temptation, Illusion, and Unicode restoration

01How do you pronounce Māra?

In reconstructed pronunciation, Māra is /ˈmɑː.rɐ/ — approximately MAH-ruh — the ā is long; two syllables, no ornament..

02What does Māra mean?

Māra means ‘death, the tempter’, the personification of death and desire who assailed the Buddha under the Bodhi tree (from √mṛ ‘to die’), MW. in the buddhist tradition.

03What are the symbols of Māra?

Māra is associated with The tenfold army (The taxonomy of temptation: every assault on resolve sorted by battalion), The flower arrows (Desire's camouflage: the shafts that arrive looking like gifts), The shadow at the seat (His claim on the Bodhi throne: the ego's final argument, "this place is mine").

04Why restore Māra in Unicode?

Plain ASCII mara strips the stress, length, and script that make the name specific. Unicode restoration returns the name to its original written dignity.

05What is the most important myth about Māra?

The Sutta Nipāta's Padhāna Sutta gives his order of battle, and it is a psychology textbook in disguise: sensual pleasure is his first army, discontent the second, hunger and thirst the third, craving the fourth, sloth and torpor the fifth, fear the sixth, doubt the seventh, hypocrisy and obstinacy the eighth, gains, fame, and honor the ninth, and the tenth is self-exaltation joined with contempt for others. The Buddha's answer is the template of every later victory: he sees each army for what it is. The tradition's whole art of temptation is this catalog — and its whole defense is recognition.

Archaeology & Art History

  • Material evidence — iconography, inscriptions, and temple archaeology — for Māra and related cults.
  • The assault on the Bodhi seat is among the oldest narrative scenes in Buddhist art: Gandhāran schist panels of the first centuries CE — now in Peshawar, Lahore, and Western collections — show the demon host shrinking from the seated figure, and the Pāla-period steles of the eight great events put the Māra-vijaya at the center of the sacred biography. The bhūmisparśa image that records the victory became the default Buddha of mainland Southeast Asia; Thai, Lao, and Khmer temple murals added the earth-goddess Thoranī wringing the flood from her hair; and Bodh Gayā itself — the vajrāsana, the "diamond throne" beneath the descendant of the very tree — remains the one site on earth where his defeat has a postal address.
