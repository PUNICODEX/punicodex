Scholarly Notes and the Gender of Time

Mama Quilla has attracted significant attention from feminist scholars of the Andes. As a lunar deity, she governs cycles: the month, the agricultural season, menstruation, pregnancy, and the rhythms of women's work. In a cosmology often dominated by solar, masculine, imperial imagery, the moon offers an alternative principle of organization. This does not mean that Mama Quilla was simply a goddess for women; men also participated in her cult and depended on the lunar calendar. But her cult provided a sacred framework for experiences that the solar cult could not fully address.

The syncretism between Mama Quilla and the Virgin Mary has been especially studied. The crescent moon, the association with fertility and marriage, and the timing of festivals all created opportunities for hidden continuity. Women could pray to the Virgin in church while still addressing the moon goddess in their hearts. This double voicing was a survival strategy under colonialism, but it also produced a rich religious aesthetics that persists in Andean Catholicism today.

The PuniCodex restoration of Mama Quilla preserves the two-word structure that colonial orthography often collapsed. In doing so, it honors the grammatical and theological partnership encoded in the name: Mama Quilla is not a single compressed word but a title, a relationship, a cosmology. The moon's address is a reminder that sacred time is not uniform; it has phases.

The restored name Mama Quilla keeps lunar time alive in a world that too often forgets that darkness and light follow one another in phases. Her monthly return is a promise that no eclipse is permanent and that cyclical time remains a viable alternative to the rush of modernity.
