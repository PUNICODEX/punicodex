​Overview

Yammu (yammu) — The Sea, Rival of Baʿal · Sea — belongs to the Phoenician tradition, where it is catalogued under the domain "Sea, Chaos". The name means ​"Sea".

Yammu is the Canaanite sea-god: the personified ocean whose rebellion against the divine council set the Baal Cycle in motion, and whose defeat by Baʿal — with ​two magic clubs thrown from the storm-cloud — established the storm-god’s kingship over the gods. The Ugaritic tablets of the fourteenth century BCE, found in 1929, gave him ​back his name after three thousand years of silence.

Mythology

Yammu’s mythology is the ​Baal Cycle’s opening act: the sea demands the storm-god’s surrender, the council wavers, and Baʿal — armed by the craftsman-god Kothar-wa-Khasis with two magic clubs — goes out ​to meet the flood and splits the sea’s skull between the eyes. It is the Levant’s own dragon-combat — the same pattern as Marduk and Tiamat, Indra and ‌Vritra, told in the oldest alphabetic literature on earth.

The Embassy of the Sea (KTU 1.2)

The cycle opens with Yammu’s envoys at the divine council, demanding that Baʿal ​be delivered into the sea’s power. El, the aged king of the gods, wavers — and it is left to Baʿal himself, with Kothar-wa-Khasis’s weapons, to answer the ‌demand. The scene sets the cycle’s whole theology: kingship is taken, not granted.

The Two Magic Clubs (KTU 1.2 iv)

Kothar-wa-Khasis gives Baʿal two clubs — Yaggarish, “Chaser”, ​and Ayyamarri, “Driver”. Baʿal strikes Yammu between the eyes, on the skull: ‘Yammu is dead! Baʿal is king!’ The lines are the Levantine storm-god’s coronation — and the ‌pattern that Hebrew poetry would echo in its own sea-combat, Yahweh versus Yam.

Yam in the Psalms (Biblical Echoes)

The Hebrew Bible keeps the sea-god’s name as a common ​noun and his defeat as a motif: ‘Thou didst divide the sea (yam) by thy strength’, ‘thou rulest the raging of the sea (yam)’. The Ugaritic tablets revealed ‌what the Psalms were arguing against — and what they preserved: Yammu’s defeat is the Old Testament sea’s backstory.

The Sea-Enemy Pattern (Comparative Frame)

Yammu belongs to the ​oldest myth-complex in the lexicon: the storm-god versus the sea. Tiamat and Marduk in Babylon, Vritra and Indra in the Rigveda, Typhon and Zeus in Greece — the pattern ‌crosses the whole ancient world, and the Ugaritic telling is among its most vivid witnesses. The sea is always first, and always beaten.

Across Cultures

Yammu is the Canaanite branch of the ancient Near Eastern sea-enemy — Tiamat’s cousin, with the Babylonian goddess-dragon as his nearest kin. The Hebrew ​tradition suppressed him into a noun but kept his combat; and modern study of the Baal Cycle restored him as the indispensable witness to what Israel’s religion argued ‌with. The Greeks of the coast heard Poseidon in him; the scholars hear the pattern.
