01

Mythology

Stories of Yoga

Yoga has no myths — it has sūtras, and the sūtras are their own literature of precision. Its ‘stories’ are the doctrine’s own history: the ascetics in the forests of the Upaniṣads, the Nātha-lineage’s wonder-working adepts, and the modern export that made a metaphysical discipline into the world’s most practised physical art.

Kaṭha Upaniṣad

The Chariot Simile

The body the chariot, the senses the horses, the mind the reins, the intellect the driver, the rider the self: the Kaṭha Upaniṣad’s simile is the oldest complete map of the psychophysical discipline. Who holds the reins attains the end of the road — ‘the still place from which the wise do not return’.

Bhagavadgītā

The Yoga of Three Paths

The Gītā democratizes the discipline: jñāna-yoga of knowledge for the philosopher, karma-yoga of selfless work for the householder, bhakti-yoga of devotion for the lover of god. ‘Yogasthaḥ kuru karmāṇi’ — established in yoga, act — is the verse on which Gandhi claimed to have fed his whole life.

Patañjali

The One Hundred Ninety-Five Sūtras

Patañjali’s Yoga-sūtra — c. 195 aphorisms — systematized what had been experiment: the eight limbs, the afflictions, the meditative absorptions, the supernormal powers that are signs on the path and traps at its end. Commentators from Vyāsa to the present have kept it the living constitution of the discipline.

Nātha Lineage

The Immortal Teachers

The medieval Nātha order gave yoga its adepts-legend: Matsyendra caught among the fishes, Gorakṣa the miracle-working founder, and later figures claimed to walk the centuries — the siddhas, perfected ones, whose bodies outlast death. Hatha-yoga’s physical canon comes from this line of embodied experimenters.

02

Union, the Yoking of Self to Divine

The Discipline That Joins

Yoga is the discipline of union: the yoking — the same root as English ‘yoke’ — of mind, breath, and self to the still point beneath them. Born in the ascetic experiments of the late Vedic age, codified by Patañjali’s sūtras, it is both one of the six orthodox schools of Indian philosophy and, in the Bhagavadgītā’s teaching, the art of every act done without attachment to its fruit.

The Eight Limbs

Patañjali’s aṣṭāṅga: restraint, observance, posture, breath, withdrawal, focus, meditation, absorption.

Union of Rivers

The yogin joins mind to breath to object as rivers join the sea — the Upaniṣadic image.

Yoga in the Gītā

Skill in action: the yogin works without craving the fruit — karma-yoga as the path for the householder.

The Cessation of the Waves

Patañjali’s definition: yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ — the stilling of thought’s waves, so the seer abides in itself.

04

The Name

From the original script to the living URL

Original Script

योग

The name in its original Sanskrit form. Yoga (योग) is attested in the source tradition — “Union, yoking — spiritual and ascetic discipline by which the individual self is joined to the divine; also the name of one of the six orthodox schools of Hindu philosophy.”. Its original diacritics and script distinctions carry the full phonetic and orthographic weight of the source tradition.

ASCII Constraint

yoga

The plain yoga form is identical to the Unicode restoration. Because this name is already written in Latin letters, no diacritics, stress, or script information were lost — only capitalization differs.

Unicode Restoration

Yoga

The Unicode restoration does not need to recover lost marks for Yoga. Its value is canonical spelling and consistent cataloguing, not the reconstruction of erased orthography. The domain is readable as-is to both DNS and humanity.

ASCII-Clean Name
Yoga.com

This restoration is written entirely in ASCII letters — no Punycode encoding stands between Yoga and the DNS. The domain reads identically to machines and to human eyes.

Pronunciation

How Yoga was spoken

/ˈjoʊɡə/ Sanskrit (IAST: Yoga)
Yo- From √yuj, “to yoke, join”; the o is long — the yoking of the self to the divine.
-ga The abstract noun: the yoking itself — the discipline by which union is achieved.

Modern Approximation

“YOH-guh”

The full word family — cognates, derivatives, and variant spellings — is traced in Extended Lore → Etymology.

The Accent / Script Rule

The word and English “yoke” are cousins: yoga is the yoke worn willingly. The root √yuj also yields English "yoke" — the cognate pair has named the same joining for five millennia.
