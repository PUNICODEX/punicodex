The working life of Rhiannon: from the myths to the modern trades

The myths did not retire. They moved offices. This is the file on where Rhiannon works now.

Rhiannon is the strongest female figure in the Four Branches of the Mabinogi, and the tales give her a complete arc: the uncatchable rider who chooses her husband, the accused mother who bears her penance, the captive of the enchantment who comes home still a queen. Every word of her story is medieval Welsh prose, preserved in two great manuscripts.

The pattern atlas — which maps every flagship temple to the industries its archetype demonstrably resonates with, each match argued and open to challenge — seats this temple in 3 industries. This file reads those seats the way they were earned: through the myths.

The Myth at Work

The Rider on the Pale Horse. Pwyll, prince of Dyfed, goes up to Gorsedd Arberth, the mound where a man either suffers blows or sees a wonder, and there he sees a lady in shining gold brocade riding past on a great pale horse — at a slow, even pace. He sends his swiftest rider after her; the faster the pursuit, the farther she draws ahead. — hold that image; the industries below are where it goes to work.

The Bag That Would Not Fill. At the wedding feast, Pwyll grants a request before he hears it — and the petitioner is Gwawl fab Clud, the suitor Rhiannon refused, who claims her by the promise. Rhiannon is not dismayed; she devises the counter-plot. — remember the shape of that act. You will see it again, wearing a logo.

The Long Employment

Lady Charlotte Guest's translation of the Mabinogion (1838–49) carried Rhiannon out of the Welsh manuscripts and into the English-speaking world, and the Victorian Celtic revival made her one of the best-known figures of Welsh tradition. Evangeline Walton's twentieth-century retellings of the Four Branches kept her before modern readers, and in 1975 Fleetwood Mac's 'Rhiannon' — written by Stevie Nicks, who found the name in a novel and only afterwards learned it belonged to a Welsh goddess — made the name famous worldwide; the given name Rhiannon, already living in Wales, spread across the English-speaking world in the song's wake. The age changed. The work did not.
