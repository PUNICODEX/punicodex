The Authentic Name

Unicode restoration and ASCII comparison

Original Script

伊邪那美

The name in its original Japanese form. Izanami (伊邪那美) is attested in the source tradition — “She-who-invites”. Its original diacritics and script distinctions carry the full phonetic and orthographic weight of the source tradition.

ASCII Constraint

izanami

The plain izanami form is identical to the Unicode restoration. Because this name is already written in Latin letters, no diacritics, stress, or script information were lost — only capitalization differs.

Unicode Restoration

Izanami

The Unicode restoration does not need to recover lost marks for Izanami. Its value is canonical spelling and consistent cataloguing, not the reconstruction of erased orthography. The domain is readable as-is to both DNS and humanity.

Punycode Encoding
Izanami.com → izanami.com

The non-ASCII characters in Izanami are encoded while the ASCII remains visible. To the DNS, it is Punycode. To humanity, it is Izanami.

How Izanami was spoken

/i.za.na.mi/ Modern Standard Japanese (Hepburn)
I- Close front [i]
-za- Voiced alveolar fricative [z] plus open central [a]
-na- Alveolar nasal [n] plus open central [a]
-mi Bilabial [m] plus close front [i]; from 美 'beautiful'

'EE-zah-nah-mee' — four even syllables, with a soft final vowel.

05

She-Who-Invites

Creator Goddess, Mother of Kami, Queen of Yomi

Izanami is the female half of the primordial creator pair and the first being to die. Her body became the landscape of the underworld, and her vengeful pursuit of Izanagi established the boundary between the living and the dead. She is both the mother of the gods and the fearsome ruler of Yomi.

Creator Goddess

With Izanagi, she created the islands of Japan.

Mother of Kami

She gave birth to the elemental gods, the land, and the sea.

Death in Childbirth

She died giving birth to the fire god Kagutsuchi.

Queen of Yomi

After death she became the ruler of the underworld.

Sacred Symbols

Comb The comb Izanagi lit to see her in Yomi, which became a source of light and betrayal
Boulder of Yomotsu-hirasaka The stone that seals the entrance to the underworld
Fire The element that killed her and that produced Kagutsuchi
Earth and underworld Her decaying body became the landscape of Yomi
06

Mythology

Stories of Izanami

Izanami's mythology is central to Japanese understandings of creation, death, and the separation between the living and the dead.

Kojiki / Nihon Shoki

The Creation of the Islands

Standing on the Floating Bridge of Heaven, Izanami and Izanagi stirred the primordial ocean with the jeweled spear. When they lifted it, brine dripped and congealed into the first island. They descended and, after a failed first marriage ceremony caused by Izanami speaking first, properly created the Japanese archipelago.

Kojiki

Death in Childbirth

Izanami gave birth to the fire god Kagutsuchi, whose flames burned her so severely that she died. From her dying body came further kami: the mountains, rivers, and earth. Her death introduced mortality into the world and sent Izanagi on his grief-stricken journey to Yomi.

Kojiki

The Queen of Yomi

In the underworld, Izanami was consumed by decay and rage. When Izanagi broke his promise not to look at her, she pursued him to the boundary of Yomi and cursed him: she would kill one thousand people each day. Izanagi replied that he would cause one thousand five hundred to be born. This exchange established the endless traffic between life and death.

Extended Lore

Izanami is the first separation. Her death created the underworld, and her curse set the rhythm of mortality. To remember her is to remember that life and death are not opposites but phases of a single story, and that the one who gives birth may also become the one who receives the dead.
