Inside the Nōaḥ restoration: what the marks recover

The domain you're reading about exists because someone refused to let Nōaḥ stay noah.

Nōaḥ's mythology is the rewriting of the world. The flood does not merely punish; it resets. After the waters, the same commands given to Adam — be fruitful, multiply, have dominion — are spoken again, as if creation itself has been given a second draft. God sees that human wickedness is great on the earth and resolves to blot out living things. But Noah finds favor because he is righteous and blameless in his generation.

That is the figure this file is about — and the temple now standing at its restored name.

The Name Before the Marks

Strip Nōaḥ to noah and one piece of evidence vanishes:

  • o → ō — Macron: long vowel: the vowel length the ASCII form cannot show.

None of these marks is decoration. Each one is a fact about how the name was spoken, and each one is checkable against the scholarly record the temple cites.
