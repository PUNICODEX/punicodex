Before the Office, the Story

Daughter of Persês. Hesiod (Theogony 409–452) makes Hekátē the daughter of the Titan Persês and the nymph Asteria. Zeús honors her above all other Titans, granting her a share of earth, sea, and sky. This triple portion makes her uniquely able to move between realms — a goddess of boundaries because she owns all boundaries.

Torch-Bearer in the Dark. When Hádês seized Persephonē, Hekátē heard her cries. In the Homeric Hymn to Demeter, she meets the grieving Dēmētēr with torches and guides her to Hēlios, who names the abductor. Later, she becomes Persephonē's companion in the underworld — a mediator between the realms.

Not the First Rebrand

Hekátē absorbed many minor deities of thresholds, ghosts, and night. In Roman religion she was identified with Trivia, the goddess of crossroads, and with the lunar Diana and underworld Proserpina in the famous 'triple Hecate' of later art. The office is only the latest translation.
