The Authentic Name

Unicode restoration and ASCII comparison

Original Script

Tōnatiuh

The name in its original Nahuatl form. Tōnatiuh (Tōnatiuh) is attested in the source tradition — “He who goes forth shining; the Nahuatl sun god”. Its macron-length vowels carry the full phonetic and orthographic weight of the source tradition.

ASCII Constraint

tonatiuh

Reduced to plain tonatiuh, the name loses everything that made it specific: macron-length vowels. What remains is an ASCII string that machines can parse but that no longer speaks with its original voice.

Unicode Restoration

Tōnatiuh

The Unicode restoration recovers what ASCII flattened. Tōnatiuh restores macron-length vowels, returning the name to its original written dignity. The domain encodes to Punycode, but the browser displays the truth.

Punycode Encoding
Tōnatiuh.com → xn--tnatiuh-5lb.com

The non-ASCII characters in Tōnatiuh are encoded while the ASCII remains visible. To the DNS, it is Punycode. To humanity, it is Tōnatiuh.

Etymology

Classical Nahuatl tōnatiuh 'sun, day'; the solar deity who travels through the underworld at night.

Meaning

Sun, day, light, the fifth age of the Aztec cosmos.

From original to transliteration

  1. Nahuatl was recorded by Spanish and native scribes in Latin alphabets from the sixteenth century onward.
  2. Tōnatiuh is the sun deity; the name is attested in the Florentine Codex and colonial devotional texts.
  3. The macron marks a long vowel in scholarly Classical Nahuatl orthography.
  4. The Unicode restoration Tōnatiuh preserves the long vowel; there is no indigenous logographic spelling for the personal name.
