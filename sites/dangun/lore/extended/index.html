04

Cultural Significance

From ancient cult to modern Unicode

Ancient Domain

Dangun is the legendary founder of Gojoseon and the first ancestor of the Korean people in Korean tradition. The name means "Lord of the sandalwood" and belongs to the domain "Founder of Korea, First King." Dangun is the son of Hwanung, the heavenly prince who descended to earth, and Ungnyeo, a bear who became a woman after enduring one hundred days in a cave eating garlic and mugwort. He is said to have founded the state of Gojoseon at Asadal in 2333 BCE, an event commemorated on National Foundation Day, 3 October.

Dangun in Later Traditions

The Dangun myth has been interpreted through many lenses: as a nativist charter myth, as a Confucian parable of virtuous rule, as a Buddhist tale of miraculous birth, and, in the modern era, as a national foundation narrative. North and South Korea both claim Dangun as a common ancestor, though their political interpretations differ. The myth also resonates with broader northeast Asian stories of bear ancestors and celestial founders.

Modern Legacy

Dangun is perhaps the most potent symbol of Korean national identity. His tomb near Pyongyang, the annual Gaecheonjeol ceremony, and countless school textbooks, monuments, and cultural productions keep the figure present in public life. The story is also invoked by overseas Korean communities and by inter-Korean dialogue that seeks a shared mythic past. In popular culture Dangun appears in films, video games, and webtoons, often reimagined as a heroic founder.

Unicode Restoration as Cultural Act

Restoring Dangun in a domain name is more than orthographic accuracy. It is a statement that the internet should recognize the full range of human writing — not only the ASCII keyboard.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about Dangun, Founder of Korea, First King, and Unicode restoration

01How do you pronounce Dangun?

In reconstructed pronunciation, Dangun is /taŋ.ɡun/ — approximately 'TAHNG-goon' — The initial consonant is an unaspirated velar stop, closer to a soft ‘t’ than to English ‘d’; the final nasal closes the first syllable. The scholarly capital marks the name as a proper noun..

02What does Dangun mean?

Dangun means Lord of the sandalwood in the korean tradition.

03What are the symbols of Dangun?

Dangun is associated with The sandalwood tree (Dang 檀 means sandalwood; the tree marks the sacred descent place of Hwanung.), Bear and tiger (The two animals who underwent Hwanung's trial; the bear's perseverance became the origin of the Korean people.), Garlic and mugwort (The austere foods of transformation, marking the passage from animal to human and from wilderness to culture.), Mountain peak (The place where Dangun withdrew and continues to dwell as a protective mountain spirit.).

04Why restore Dangun in Unicode?

Plain ASCII dangun strips the stress, length, and script that make the name specific. Unicode restoration returns the name to its original written dignity.

05What is the most important myth about Dangun?

Hwanung tested a bear and a tiger by locking them in a cave with only garlic and mugwort. The tiger gave up, but the bear endured one hundred days and became a woman named Ungnyeo. Hwanung then took her as his wife, and she bore Dangun.
