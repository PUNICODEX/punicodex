How Lakṣmī was rebuilt, mark by mark

From the restoration bench — the working notes on Lakṣmī, kept because the process is the proof.

What We Found

The attestation runs through the tradition's own sources: लक्ष्मी. Meaning: "The goddess of fortune and beauty — Viṣṇu's consort, born from the churning of the ocean".

The figure behind the name: Lakṣmī's mythology centers on emergence, courtship, and the precariousness of fortune.

What the Name Carried In

Born from the Churning Ocean. When the gods and demons churned the cosmic ocean in search of amṛta, the first treasures to surface were poison, the moon, and the wish-fulfilling cow. Then, robed in lotuses and glowing like a second dawn, Lakṣmī rose from the foam.

Sītā, Fortune Incarnate. In the Rāmāyaṇa, Sītā is understood as an embodiment of Lakṣmī who has descended with Viṣṇu's avatāra Rāma. Her birth from a furrow in the earth, her marriage to Rāma, and her abduction by Rāvaṇa form the central narrative of the epic.

A spelling that has to carry stories like these is not a label. It is equipment.

Where It Stood Before Us

Lakṣmī's earliest identifiable images predate the Kuṣāṇas: among the 2nd-century BCE railing reliefs of Bharhut a lotus-borne goddess is labeled Sirimā Devatā — Śrī herself — and the stūpa's medallions already show the lustration-by-elephants scheme that becomes her signature gajalakṣmī motif. Gupta-era temples and early Hindu cave shrines include her among the guardians and consorts of Viṣṇu. From the medieval period onward, her iconography becomes highly standardized: standing on a lotus, holding lotuses, showering coins, and accompanied by elephants or owls. Archaeological and numismatic evidence across South and Southeast Asia confirms her presence as a royal and mercantile deity from the early centuries CE onward. The bench inherits all of it — the web temple is the newest site, not the first.

What We Kept

The evidence, mark by mark — 1 feature the keyboard form lakshmi cannot hold:

  • i → ī — Macron: long /iː/. Kept.

What We Rejected

lakshmi as the primary form. It survives as the convenience it is; it does not get to lead. The hierarchy is full restoration first, and this name can afford to stand at the top of it.

What We Set Down

Lakṣmī — What survives here cannot be shown in ASCII: a feature of the original that the fallback alphabet flattens. That is the Tier-1 test, and this name passes it without argument.

For the voice: 'LUKH-shmee' — the middle consonant is a curled-tongue 'sh', and the final vowel is held long like 'ee' in 'see'.
