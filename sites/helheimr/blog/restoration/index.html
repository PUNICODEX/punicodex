The provenance file on Helheimr

Provenance record 097 · Helheimr · one temple, fully accounted for.

Provenance

1. The attestation. ᚼᛁᛚᚼᛁᛘᚱ, from the tradition's own record. On record: Poetic Edda: Vafþrúðnismál (Óðinn's wisdom contest on the fate of the dead and the underworld), Saxo Grammaticus, Gesta Danorum (Hadingus's descent to the realm of the dead), Snorri Sturluson, Edda: Skáldskaparmál (kennings for Hel and the underworld journey).

2. The narrative record. Hermóðr's Ride to Hel — When Baldr is killed by the mistletoe dart, the gods mourn so deeply that Frigg sends his brother Hermóðr to Helheimr to bargain for his return. Hermóðr takes Óðinn's eight-legged horse Sleipnir and rides for nine nights through valleys so dark and deep that he sees nothing, until he reaches the river Gjöll and the gold-floored bridge that leads to Hel's hall. The Halls of Hel — In Gylfaginning, Snorri describes Helheimr as a realm of high walls and forbidding gates. Hel herself is half living flesh and half corpse-blue, a visual emblem of the threshold she guards.

3. The physical record. Viking-Age burial practice gives Helheimr its material context. The Lindholm Høje cemetery at Aalborg preserves hundreds of stone-ship settings and cremations, while Gotland picture stones such as the Tjängvide and Ardre VIII stones depict afterlife journeys, valkyries, and the otherworld. Runestones across Denmark, Sweden, and Norway record the dead by name and kin.

4. The current holding. The temple at /sites/helheimr/, classified Tier 2. The restoration keeps one feature the ASCII form drops. One is enough to matter, and the label says so plainly: Tier-2.

5. The documented resonances. Gaming, Fantasy & Entertainment IP, Cybersecurity & Digital Protection, Disaster Resilience & Recovery — seats argued and weighted in the pattern atlas, read through the myths in this temple's Resonance File.

6. The primary sources. Poetic Edda, Prose Edda — the library this file answers to, open at the Sacred Texts collection.

7. The continuing record. The name lives on in modern fantasy, Neopagan practice, Scandinavian heritage, and the global reception of Viking-Age literature. Restoring Helheimr in Unicode preserves the name's cultural specificity against the flattening force of plain ASCII.
