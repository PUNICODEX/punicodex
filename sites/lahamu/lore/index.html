The Authentic Name

Unicode restoration and ASCII comparison

Original Script

𒀭𒆷𒄩𒈬

The name in its original Mesopotamian form. Lahamu (𒀭𒆷𒄩𒈬) is attested in the source tradition — “The hairy one (fem.)”. Its original diacritics and script distinctions carry the full phonetic and orthographic weight of the source tradition.

ASCII Constraint

lahamu

The plain lahamu form is identical to the Unicode restoration. Because this name is already written in Latin letters, no diacritics, stress, or script information were lost — only capitalization differs.

Unicode Restoration

Lahamu

The Unicode restoration does not need to recover lost marks for Lahamu. Its value is canonical spelling and consistent cataloguing, not the reconstruction of erased orthography. The domain is readable as-is to both DNS and humanity.

Punycode Encoding
Lahamu.com → lahamu.com

The non-ASCII characters in Lahamu are encoded while the ASCII remains visible. To the DNS, it is Punycode. To humanity, it is Lahamu.

Pronunciation

How Lahamu was spoken

/laˈhaːmu/ Akkadian Reconstruction
La- Open vowel [a] preceded by lateral [l]; the first syllable is light and opens the name toward the primordial deep.
-ha- Voiceless glottal fricative [h] between two open vowels; the rough breath at the center of 'the hairy one.'
-mu Bilabial nasal [m] plus close back rounded vowel [u]; the feminine ending of Akkadian lāhamu/lahamu.

Modern Approximation

'la-HAH-moo' — stress the middle syllable and let the final vowel settle like water in a deep basin.

Etymological Kin

  • Akkadian 𒀭𒆷𒄩𒆳𒌝 (lahamu / lāhamu), 'the hairy one' — feminine counterpart to Lahmu, firstborn of the primordial waters
  • Sumerian comparison The name has no direct Sumerian attestation; it enters Mesopotamian cosmogony through the Akkadian Enuma Elish
  • Hebrew comparison lāḥem, 'to be eager, ardent,' and Arabic laḥima, 'to cling, adhere' — possible but uncertain Semitic echoes of the root

The Accent / Script Rule

Lahamu is Tier 2: the PuniCodex restoration is the plain transliteration Lahamu, with no preserved diacritic or distinctive letter. Some Assyriological editions write Lāhamu with a macron on the first a to mark a possible vowel length, but the cuneiform evidence is not sufficient to make that length certain, and the canonical Unicode form here follows the lexicon's unmarked Lahamu. The name is therefore an ASCII-transparent Tier-2 entry.

05

LAHAMU — Firstborn of the Primordial Waters

Primordial Being · First Generation · Genealogical Root

Lahamu is the feminine half of the first divine pair born from the mingling of Apsû, the freshwater abyss, and Tiāmat, the salt sea. Her name means 'the hairy one' (feminine), paired with her brother-consort Lahmu, 'the hairy one' (masculine). She is not a goddess of cult or temple in the usual sense; she is a genealogical necessity, the first female principle through whom the line of the great gods begins.

In the Babylonian Enuma Elish, Lahmu and Lahamu appear immediately after the generation of the waters themselves. From them come Anshar and Kishar, the sky-horizon and the earth-horizon, and from Anshar and Kishar comes Anû, the sky. Every subsequent god — including Ea and Marduk — descends from this first pairing. To name Lahamu is therefore to name one of the deepest roots of the Mesopotamian cosmic family tree.

Primordial Waters

Born from the mingling of Apsû's fresh abyss and Tiāmat's salt sea — the first life emerging from water's union.

The First Pair

Lahmu and Lahamu stand at the head of the divine genealogy; the masculine and feminine principles of the first generation.

Mother of the Horizon

With Lahmu she begets Anshar and Kishar, the sky-circle and earth-circle, from whom heaven and earth are ordered.

The Hairy One

Her name evokes the undifferentiated, the tangled, the pre-formed — like water-weeds or the first matter before shape.

Sacred Symbols

Coiling hair or water-weed The 'hairy' etymology — the first matter tangled and undifferentiated before cosmic separation
Mingled fresh and salt water The union of Apsû and Tiāmat from which Lahamu and Lahmu arise
Horizon circle Anshar and Kishar, her children, who bound the visible sky and earth
Cuneiform wedge The written sign that preserves her name in the standard Babylonian recension of the Enuma Elish
06

Mythology

Stories of Lahamu

Lahamu's mythology is almost entirely cosmogonical. She does not act in the later dramas of flood, descent, or combat; she is the silent root from which those dramas grow. Her significance lies in her position at the transition from primordial matter to divine person.

Enuma Elish

Birth from the Waters

The Babylonian Enuma Elish opens with the primordial pair: 'When on high the heaven had not been named, and below the earth had not been called by name — Apsû, the primeval, and Tiāmat, she who gave birth to them all, had mixed their waters together.' From that mingling came Lahmu and Lahamu, the first divine generation, the first beings to whom personal names and a paired relationship are ascribed (Enuma Elish I:1–10, standard Babylonian version).

Genealogy

The First Divine Pair

Lahamu is the feminine counterpart of Lahmu. Together they form the first sexually differentiated pair in the cosmos, the model for every later divine marriage. Their names share the root lhm, 'to be hairy,' understood as a quality of the primordial — the uncombed, undifferentiated state of matter before Marduk's ordering wind divides and shapes it.

Cosmogony

Mother of Anshar and Kishar

The generations proceed from Lahamu and Lahmu in swift succession: 'Lahmu and Lahamu were brought forth, were named; as they grew tall and great, Anshar and Kishar came into being — they surpassed them.' Anshar is the horizon of heaven, Kishar the horizon of earth; their names bound the visible cosmos and prepare the way for Anû, Ea, and finally Marduk (Enuma Elish I:11–18).

Legacy

The Root of Kingship

Because Lahamu stands at the base of the genealogy, every later claim to cosmic kingship passes through her. Marduk's authority as king of the gods is justified not only by his defeat of Tiāmat but by his descent from the primordial line that begins with Apsû, Tiāmat, Lahmu, and Lahamu. The first pair is therefore politically as well as cosmologically foundational: without Lahamu, the lineage of Marduk has no beginning.

Extended Lore

Lahamu is easy to overlook because she does so little. She is not the dragon split to make the world, nor the storm-god who defeats it. She is simply the first female, the first differentiated form to arise from the waters. Yet that 'simply' contains everything.
