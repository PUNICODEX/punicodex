The Authentic Name

Unicode restoration and ASCII comparison

Original Script

𒀭𒆥𒄖

The name in its original Mesopotamian form. Kingu (𒀭𒆥𒄖) is attested in the source tradition — “Unknown”. Its original diacritics and script distinctions carry the full phonetic and orthographic weight of the source tradition.

ASCII Constraint

kingu

The plain kingu form is identical to the Unicode restoration. Because this name is already written in Latin letters, no diacritics, stress, or script information were lost — only capitalization differs.

Unicode Restoration

Kingu

The Unicode restoration does not need to recover lost marks for Kingu. Its value is canonical spelling and consistent cataloguing, not the reconstruction of erased orthography. The domain is readable as-is to both DNS and humanity.

Punycode Encoding
Kingu.com → kingu.com

The non-ASCII characters in Kingu are encoded while the ASCII remains visible. To the DNS, it is Punycode. To humanity, it is Kingu.

Pronunciation

How Kingu was spoken

/ˈkiŋɡu/ Akkadian Reconstruction
Ki- Voiceless velar stop [k] followed by close front vowel [i] — the first syllable is light and sharp, like a command.
-ŋ- Voiced velar nasal [ŋ] — the g in Kingu represents this single nasal sound in the Akkadian cluster, not a stop-g.
-gu Voiced velar stop [g] plus close back rounded vowel [u] — the final syllable closes the name with weight.

Modern Approximation

'KEENG-goo' — stress the first syllable; the middle consonant is a soft ng-sound, and the final vowel is rounded and firm.

Etymological Kin

  • Akkadian Kingu, the name of Tiamat's consort and commander in the Enuma Elish
  • Etymology Unknown; no certain Sumerian or Semitic etymology is established (Black-Green). The name may be foreign or archaic.

The Accent / Script Rule

Kingu is Tier 2: the Unicode restoration 'Kingu' is simply the ASCII form capitalized, with no preserved diacritic or distinctive non-ASCII letter. The name's etymology is unknown, so there is no reconstructed vowel length or accent to mark. Standard Assyriology writes Kingu; PuniCodex displays the canonical capitalized form while acknowledging that the name remains philologically opaque.

05

KINGU — The Dragon Consort & Commander of Chaos

Dragon · Consort of Tiamat · Bearer of the Tablets of Destiny

Kingu is the dragon-god who stands at the right hand of Tiāmat in the Babylonian Enuma Elish. When the younger gods slay Apsû and disturb the primordial order, Tiāmat takes Kingu as her consort, places the Tablet of Destinies (ṭuppi šīmāti) upon his breast, and makes him commander of the monster-host that marches against the divine assembly. He is terror made general: the chaotic deep given a strategic mind and a regal badge.

Yet Kingu's defeat is the pivot on which the human world turns. Marduk kills him, strips away the Tablets, and from Kingu's blood the gods create humankind to bear the toil of heaven and earth. The dragon who would have unmade order becomes, unwillingly, the raw material of civilization. This temple tracks that double identity: the consort of chaos and the substrate of humanity.

The Dragon

Kingu is described as a god of dragon-form, the monstrous counterpart to Marduk's storm-order.

Tablet of Destinies

Tiāmat fastens the ṭuppi šīmāti on Kingu's breast, making his command authoritative over fate itself.

Blood of Origin

From Kingu's blood the gods fashion humankind — a stark myth of life arising from the defeated enemy.

The Monster Host

Serpents, dragons, and scorpion-men follow Kingu into battle against Marduk and the younger gods.

Sacred Symbols

Dragon body The monstrous form that opposes Marduk's cosmic order

06

Mythology

Stories of Kingu

Kingu enters the Enuma Elish not as a primordial element like Apsû or Tiāmat, but as a response — the consort Tiāmat chooses after her first partner is destroyed. He is chaos with a general's mind: the one who turns Tiāmat's grief into organized war. His story moves from elevation to defeat to transformation, because in Mesopotamian cosmology the slain enemy does not simply disappear; he is reworked into the world.

Elevation

Tiāmat Makes Kingu Her Consort

After Ea kills Apsû, Tiāmat resolves to avenge the primordial freshwater abyss. She takes Kingu as her consort and sets him over the gods she has assembled: 'She placed him in the front, leader of the host, to direct the battle, to gain the victory' (Enuma Elish II:122–124, trans. Foster). Kingu is not the source of the rebellion but its appointed head — the dragon who gives tactical shape to Tiāmat's formless rage.

Authority

The Tablet of Destinies

To legitimate Kingu's command, Tiāmat fastens the Tablet of Destinies (ṭuppi šīmāti) upon his breast. The tablet is the inscribed record of fates and decrees; to hold it is to hold the power to confirm or cancel the destinies of gods and worlds. Kingu thus briefly becomes a rival sovereign, and the younger gods are thrown into terror because fate itself seems to have defected to chaos.

Conflict

Marduk Defeats Kingu

Marduk of Babylon accepts single combat with Tiāmat on the condition that the assembly grant him kingship. After he splits Tiāmat and creates heaven and earth from her body, he turns to her army. Kingu is captured, stripped of the Tablets, and killed. Marduk takes the Tablet of Destinies from Kingu and fixes it to his own breast, so that the power of fate now serves the order of Babylon.

Transformation

The Creation of Humankind

With Kingu dead, Ea proposes a solution to the gods' need for servants: 'Let one of the gods be handed over; he alone will be slaughtered, and the gods be created from his flesh' (Enuma Elish VI:5–8, trans. Foster). Kingu is chosen. From his blood Ea fashions humankind, charging them with the service of the gods so that the divine assembly may rest. The enemy's life-fluid becomes the clay of civilization.
