Pronunciation

How Mꜣꜥt was spoken

/muːʕaʔ/ Egyptian Reconstruction
m Bilabial nasal. The lips close, the sound hums. In Egyptian, m could function as a prefix indicating participial or nominal forms — here it anchors the root.
ꜣ The Egyptological alef. Scholars reconstruct this as a glottal stop [ʔ] in most periods, though some argue for a voiced pharyngeal fricative [ʕ] or palatal approximant [j] in Old Egyptian. It is a consonant, not a vowel — a closure of the throat that ASCII erases entirely.

Modern Approximation

"MOO-ah" — with a deep glottal catch in the throat on the second syllable. The m is long and resonant. The ꜣ is not a smooth vowel; it is a stop, a moment of closure. Think of the sound between the syllables of "uh-oh," but deeper, more deliberate.

The Alef Rule

In our Tier-2 system, the alef (ꜣ) preserves a consonant that every Egyptological transliteration recognises but ASCII cannot render.
