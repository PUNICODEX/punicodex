The Authentic Names

Why apóllon.com and apollon.com are both correct forms

Greek Original

Ἀπόλλων

The name in its original Greek form. The smooth breathing on the alpha, the acute on the first ámicron, the long omega. A name that sings when spoken — the rhythm of a lyre string being plucked.

ASCII Constraint

APOLLO

Reduced to a space program. A record label. A god of light and prophecy — the most beautiful of all Olympians, whose music could tame wild beasts — reduced to six uppercase letters in a database field.

Unicode Restoration

apóllon · apollon

Tier-1 preserves the acute on the first á — the pitch accent that rises like a note in music. Tier-2 strips the accent but keeps the macron on o. Both are authentic scholarly orthographies. Oxford prefers one. Cambridge prefers the other. The PUNYCODEX owns both. Neither is a compromise. Each is a different lens on the same divinity.

Punycode Encoding

apollon.com → xn--apolln-fgb.com

Both forms encode their non-ASCII characters — Tier-1 with ó (U+00F3) and o (U+014D), Tier-2 with o (U+014D) alone. To the DNS, they are different Punycode strings. To humanity, they are both apóllon.

Tier Classification

Where Apóllon stands in the PUNYCODEX elegant tier system

Tier 1
apóllon.com

Apóllon carries the acute on the ó and the macron on the o. It satisfies the dual-mark criterion exactly: acute plus macron, stress plus length. Under the elegant tier system, this is Tier 1.

The Greek original Ἀπόλλων contains both features — the rising pitch on the first syllable and the long omega that holds the name like a held note on a lyre. Apóllon does not flatten. It carries the full scholarly orthography. Tier 1 is not an opinion. It is a measurement.

Tier 2
apollon.com

Apollon preserves the macron on the o without the acute. It is Tier 2 — single-mark Greek. This is not a deficiency. In Liddell-Scott-Jones, Cambridge, and Oxford editions, macron-only is the standard convention. The length is preserved even when the stress is not. Apollon is not a compromise. It is a lens.

02

Pronunciation

How the God of Music was truly spoken

/'á.pol.lɔːn/ Attic Greek Reconstruction
á- Short a, pitched upward with the acute — the sound of a name being called, of light arriving with announcement. It is the syllable of invocation.
-poll- Short o, unstressed — the sound of a lyre string vibrating after being plucked. The stress has moved to the first syllable, making the name a proclamation. The p is crisp, the double l liquid and flowing.
-lɔːn The long omega sustains, holding the final vowel like a held note. The l is liquid, the n closes gently. It is a name that resolves — a cadence, not a collision.

Modern Approximation

"ah-POL-lone" — with the second syllable carrying a slight rise in pitch, as if asking a question. The final o is held longer than English allows, like the "o" in "bone" sustained for half a second.

Etymological Notes

  • Possible origin — ἀπόλλυμι (apollymi), "to destroy"
  • Also linked to pre-Greek, non-Indo-European roots
  • The Destroyer — of plagues, enemies, falsehoods
  • Also the Healer — the same arrows bring sickness and cure

The Tier Identity

Because the acute falls on a short vowel (the first a), Tier-1 preserves it with the accent mark. Tier-2 removes it, giving apollon. Both are correct. One is more precise. Together, they form a dual-tier distinct pair owned by the PUNYCODEX.

The Gift

Domains, symbols, and the radiance of truth

apóllon is the most complete of the Olympians. He is not merely the god of one thing. He is the god of clarity itself — the light that reveals, the music that orders chaos, the prophecy that speaks truth whether mortals wish to hear it or not. He is beauty with teeth. Harmony with consequences.

Light & the Sun

Not merely illumination — revelation. His light does not just show what is. It shows what must be. He drives the chariot across the sky each day because order demands it. The sun rises because apóllon wills it.

Music & the Lyre

He invented the lyre from a tortoise shell and taught Orpheus to play. His music could calm storms, tame wild beasts, and make stones weep. Music, to apóllon, is not entertainment. It is cosmic law made audible.

Prophecy & Truth

The Oracle at Delphi spoke his words. Kings and generals traveled for days to hear a single sentence. His prophecies were never wrong — only misunderstood. Truth does not adjust itself to human comprehension.

Healing & Plague

The same bow that brings sickness brings cure. He is the god who decides whether a city lives or dies. His physicians were the best in the ancient world. His arrows were the most feared. Both come from the same hand.

04

The Myths

Stories of beauty, consequence, and absolute truth

The Birth

Born on the Wandering Island

Hera, in jealousy, cursed Leto so that no land fixed to the earth could give her shelter to bear her children. All islands refused her — until Delos, a floating rock, offered itself. There, Leto clung to a palm tree and gave birth to apóllon and his twin sister Artemis. The moment he was born, swans circled the island seven times, and light flooded the Aegean. Delos became fixed to the earth forever — the weight of divinity anchors what chaos cannot hold.

The Conquest

Slaying Python

Python, a monstrous serpent, guarded the oracle at Delphi and had pursued Leto during her pregnancy. apóllon, barely days old, took his bow and tracked the beast to its lair. He fired a single arrow. Python fell. The serpent's body rotted — python gave us the word putrefaction. On that spot, apóllon founded his greatest sanctuary. He did not inherit Delphi. He conquered it.

The Contest

Marsyas and the Lyre

Marsyas, a satyr, found the flute that Athena had discarded and played it so skillfully that he challenged apóllon to a musical contest. The Muses judged. Marsyas played beautifully — wild, passionate, free. apóllon played the lyre upside down and still won. Then he played it behind his back. The god of music does not merely play better. He plays from positions of impossible disadvantage and still prevails. Marsyas was flayed alive for his hubris. In apóllon's world, excellence is not optional.

The Curse

Cassandra and the Gift

Cassandra, a Trojan princess, was so beautiful that apóllon offered her the gift of prophecy in exchange for her love. She accepted the gift and refused him. In his fury, he spat into her mouth — she would still speak true prophecy, but no one would ever believe her. She warned Troy of the wooden horse. She warned them of Agamemnon. No one listened. This is apóllon's darkest lesson: truth without trust is worse than ignorance. The god of light can also be the architect of blindness.
