The Myth at Work

The Shadow of the Sun. Born of Sūrya and Chhāyā — "Shadow," the substitute-wife the sun's true wife left in her place — Śani was the son the light refused to look at. His gaze fell so baleful that his own father's skin darkened at the meeting. — remember the shape of that act. You will see it again, wearing a logo.

Even the Gods Are Judged. His stories form a single syllabus: when Śani's gaze fell on Śiva, the great god was made to wander as a beggar; on Rāma's line, even the righteous king's fortune bowed. The Hanumān legend adds the one counter: when Śani pressed Hanumān, the mighty one crushed him instead — and Śani, begging release, vowed never to afflict Hanumān's devotees. — remember the shape of that act. You will see it again, wearing a logo.

The Long Employment

"Śani chal raha hai" (Saturn is upon us) is the everyday explanation for every run of bad luck from Mumbai to Trinidad. His Saturday fast, his black-and-blue colors, his iron-and-sesame remedies constitute the largest planetary cult in the world. The age changed. The work did not.
