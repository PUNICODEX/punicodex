Śākyamuni: the restoration, the temple, and the world it opens

The domain you're reading about exists because someone refused to let Śākyamuni stay shakyamuni.

The life of Śākyamuni is the best-attested sacred biography of the ancient world — told in the Pāli Nikāyas, versified by Aśvaghoṣa, expanded in the Nidānakathā and the Lalitavistara, and anchored in the ground by Aśoka's own inscriptions. Queen Māyā, traveling to her father's house, gave birth standing in the grove at Lumbinī, holding the branch of a sāla tree; the child took seven steps and declared this his last birth. The legends multiplied — the shower of blossoms, the two streams of water, the prophecy of the sage Asita — but the place is fixed in stone: Aśoka's pillar at Rummindei, raised in his twentieth regnal year, reads in plain Prakrit, hida budhe jāte sakyamuni — "here the Buddha Śākyamuni was born.

That is the figure this file is about — and the temple now standing at its restored name.

The Name Before the Marks

The name is attested as शाक्यमुनि. On the evidence, it means "‘sage of the Śākya clan’, epithet of the historical Buddha Siddhārtha Gautama, MW.".

Strip Śākyamuni to shakyamuni and one piece of evidence vanishes:

  • a → ā — Macron: long /aː/: the vowel length the ASCII form cannot show.

A reader who knows the marks hears the name correctly on the first try. A reader who never sees them never gets the chance.
