The Cycle, Read Forward

Funerary & Memorial Services — through The Commission in the Trāyastriṃśa Heaven

The Sūtra of the Past Vows of Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva is set in the heaven of the Thirty-Three, where the Buddha preaches for his mother's sake before his passing. There he recounts Kṣitigarbha's long record of vows and formally entrusts to him all beings of the coming age — the interval between Śākyamuni's parinirvāṇa and Maitreya's arrival.

The atlas states the seat in one line: The vow to empty the hells; the bodhisattva who attends the dead between lives. The story above is the long version of the same sentence. Industries do not invent their archetypes; they inherit them and call them best practice.

Travel, Tourism & Place Branding — through The Brahmin Daughter

The sūtra's most beloved past life is a daughter's. A pious brahmin girl's mother died slandering the Dharma, and the daughter, selling all she had to make offerings, was granted a vision of the Avīci hell — where she learned that her merit had already lifted her mother toward heaven.

The atlas states the seat in one line: Protector of travelers on the roads of the six realms; the roadside guardian. The story above is the long version of the same sentence. That is the cycle working: story becomes duty, duty becomes trade.

Wedding, Family & Maternity — through The Silla Prince of Jiuhua

In the eighth century a Korean prince of Silla, Jin Qiaojue, came to Mount Jiuhua in Anhui and practiced there in austerity until his death; the mountain's monks, finding his body uncorrupted, enshrined him as Dìzàng's own emanation, and Jiuhuashan became the bodhisattva's bodhimaṇḍa — one of China's four sacred Buddhist mountains. His "flesh body" is venerated there still, and Dìzàng's birthday on the thirtieth of the seventh lunar month closes the Ghost Month across the Chinese world.

The atlas states the seat in one line: Guardian of children; as Jizō he watches over the young at every roadside. The story above is the long version of the same sentence. That is the cycle working: story becomes duty, duty becomes trade.

The Theme That Runs the Cycle

Kṣitigarbha's mythology is the story of a vow repeated across lifetimes — a brahmin daughter, a king — each life renewing the same impossible promise.
