The name in its original Japanese form. Sarutahiko (猿田彦) is attested in the source tradition — “Monkey-field prince”. Its original diacritics and script distinctions carry the full phonetic and orthographic weight of the source tradition.

ASCII Constraint

sarutahiko

The plain sarutahiko form is identical to the Unicode restoration. Because this name is already written in Latin letters, no diacritics, stress, or script information were lost — only capitalization differs.

05

Monkey-Field Prince

Guide of the Heavenly Descent, Guardian of Crossroads

Sarutahiko is the kami who stands at the boundary between heaven and earth. When Ninigi descended from Takamagahara, Sarutahiko blocked the way until Ame-no-Uzume persuaded him to become the guide. With his long nose, glaring eyes, and red face, he became the model for the taikomochi and tenjin figures of Japanese folk performance and the patron of those who stand at thresholds.

Guide of Ninigi

He led Ninigi's descent from heaven to earth.

Crossroads Guardian

He protects boundaries, paths, and transitions.

Husband of Uzume

Ame-no-Uzume married him after negotiating the heavenly descent.

Folk Performer

His appearance influenced comedic and ceremonial performers.

06

Mythology

Stories of Sarutahiko

Sarutahiko's mythology centers on the moment when the heavenly line met the earthly kami and found a guide rather than an enemy.

Kojiki / Nihon Shoki

The Blockade at the Heavenly Bridge

When Ninigi prepared to descend from heaven, the earthly kami Sarutahiko stood at the Floating Bridge of Heaven and refused passage. None of the heavenly envoys could persuade him. Finally Ame-no-Uzume, the goddess of dance and laughter, went forward. She exposed herself and made him laugh, then convinced him to submit and guide Ninigi's descent.

Kojiki

Marriage to Ame-no-Uzume

After guiding Ninigi, Sarutahiko married Ame-no-Uzume. Their union symbolized the alliance between heavenly and earthly powers and the role of humor and persuasion in resolving cosmic standoffs.

Folk religion

Patron of Performers and Boundaries

Sarutahiko's grotesque and powerful appearance made him a patron of street performers, comedians, and those who manage transitions. His image appears at festival processions and at shrines dedicated to safe travel and guidance.

Extended Lore

Sarutahiko stands at the edge of two worlds. He is not an enemy of heaven but its necessary guide: the one who knows the way down. His marriage to Uzume reminds us that the most stubborn boundaries can be crossed with courage, humor, and respect.
