The name Meru reaches the modern world through scholarly transliteration of the sanskrit tradition. Because the conventional spelling uses only Latin letters, its canonical and ASCII forms coincide — the tradition it carries is in the name itself, not in diacritics.

Cult & Worship

How the sacred was kept — rites, sanctuaries, remains

The Cult

No separate cult — every cosmological rite is his. The consecration of a stupa ‘plants Meru’; the temple’s foundation deposits place the mountain under the god’s throne. The circumambulation of any sacred centre is its summit-walk.

In the Earth

Meru’s monuments are every stupa and pagoda — the Borobudur terraces walk the pilgrim up Sumeru; the temple mountains of Angkor are Meru in sandstone. The maṇḍala-paintings of Tibet diagram it ten thousand times.

05

Iconography

How Meru was imaged across the ages

The Image

The stepped pyramid-mountain, four-faced and jewel-coloured, crowned by Indra’s palace or the thousand-armed deity; or the square mandala with its four gates. The stupa’s harmikā tower is Meru’s tip in portable form.

06

Reception & Transformation

The name after its own age — transmission, translation, survival

Afterlives

History of science studies Meru as the ancient world’s most influential cosmic model — the flat-earth cosmography’s greatest rival, and the template of every Asian sacred building. The pagoda’s archaeology is, in one reading, nothing but Meru’s long migration east.

Identifications Across Traditions

Meru is shared property: Hindu Meru, Buddhist Sumeru, Jain Meru of the madhyaloka. The Greeks of Bactria heard Olympus in it; and the East Asian Buddhist cosmologies drew Sumeru as the world’s spike on temple banners from Java to Japan. The mountain out-religioned every religion that mapped it.

Modern Legacy

Every mandala, stupa, and temple plan is a Meru; the prayer-wheel’s axis and the pagoda’s spire are its hardware. ‘Sumeru’ and ‘Meru’ entered the names of a thousand monasteries and mountains — and travellers to the real Himalaya argued for centuries which peak the geographers had meant.

The Meditation

What Meru means — beyond the dictionary

Meru: the name of the centre, restored at the centre of a plain line of letters. It needs no diacritic — the mountain carries its own height, and every reader’s eye climbs it. Two syllables, eighty-four thousand leagues.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about Meru, Cosmic Mountain, Axis Mundi, and canonical restoration

01How do you pronounce Meru?

In reconstructed pronunciation, Meru is /ˈmɛruː/ — approximately “MEH-roo”.

02What does Meru mean?

Meru means Name of a fabulous mountain — the central point of Jambūdvīpa, round which the sun, moon and planets revolve; the Olympus of Hindu mythology and abode of the gods. in the sanskrit tradition.

03What are the symbols of Meru?

Meru is associated with The Axis (The world turns on Meru; the pole star wheels above its summit as the heavens’ nail-head.), The Four Faces (Gold to the east, crystal south, ruby west, lapis north — the four colours of the mandala.), City of the Gods (Indra’s Amarāvatī crowns the summit; the thirty-three gods feast in its halls.), The Mandala’s Map (Every maṇḍala diagrams Meru: centre, four gates, and the enclosing world-rings.).

04Why use the canonical spelling of Meru?

Meru is written entirely in ASCII letters, so its restoration is about canonical fidelity rather than recovered diacritics: the temple fixes the name in its scholarly standard spelling, attested in the source tradition, instead of leaving it to drift.

05What is the most important myth about Meru?
