The Myth at Work

Son of Darkness and Night. In Hesiod's Theogony (124–125), Chaos gives rise to Erebus and Nyx; their union produces Aithḗr and Hemera (Day). Where Night and Darkness are bound together, their luminous opposites are born: bright air and daylight. — remember the shape of that act. You will see it again, wearing a logo.

The Rampart of Zeus. Later cosmographers imagined Aithḗr as the protective wall of Zeús, the bright boundary that holds the cosmos together and keeps the chthonic powers — Tartaros and its prisoners — outside the ordered world. In this reading, Aithḗr is not merely above us; he is the fortification of divine order. — that is not just a story. It is a business model, four thousand years early.

The Long Employment

Aithḗr outlived his mythology to become one of the most durable words in Western science. Newton revived 'aether' as the medium through which light waves were thought to propagate; Victorian physicists filled space with 'luminiferous ether' until the Michelson–Morley experiment of 1887 cast it into doubt. The age changed. The work did not.
