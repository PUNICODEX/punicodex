The Collection

Níkē through the eyes of sculptors, painters, and craftsmen across three millennia

Winged Victory of Samothrace c. 190 BCE · Parian Marble · Louvre, Paris

Perhaps the most celebrated sculpture of victory ever created. Níkē stands on the prow of a warship, wings spread, gown plastered by wind, descending to crown the naval victor.

Temple of Athena Nike c. 420 BCE · Acropolis, Athens

The Ionic temple on the southwest bastion of the Athenian Acropolis, dedicated to Athena in her role as bringer of victory. The frieze depicted battles from Greek history.

Ápteros Níkē c. 450 BCE · Temple of Athena Nike

The Wingless Victory — depicted without wings so she would not fly away from Athens. A prayer made stone, a hope made architecture.

Níkē on Athenian Coinage 5th–4th c. BCE · Silver Tetradrachm

Athena and Níkē appear together on countless Athenian coins — wisdom and victory, the two forces that built an empire.

Níkē Slaying a Bull Hellenistic Period · Terracotta Relief

A lesser-known but vivid depiction of Níkē in active triumph, representing victory over brute force through divine favor.

Níkē Driving a Chariot c. 400 BCE · Attic Red-Figure Vase

The goddess in her most dynamic role — not standing still, but moving forward, driving the chariot of triumph across the sky.
