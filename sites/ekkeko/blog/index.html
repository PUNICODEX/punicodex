Scholarly Notes and the Politics of Miniature Abundance

Anthropologists who study Ekkeko often find themselves caught between two interpretive poles. One pole treats the figure as a classic example of sympathetic magic: the miniature object stands for the desired object, and the ritual act channels wish into reality. The other pole treats Ekkeko as a social and economic institution: the Alasita festival is a marketplace, a performance of aspiration, and a mechanism for redistributing goods and money through the urban economy. Both interpretations capture something true, but neither exhausts the phenomenon.

Recent scholarship has emphasized the role of Ekkeko in negotiating Indigenous identity under modernity. In La Paz, the figure appears in political imagery alongside Indigenous flags and slogans. He is not a retreat into tradition but a way of asserting that Andean values, reciprocity, and household ritual have a place in the contemporary city. The miniature objects on his back increasingly include laptops, passports, and university degrees, not because the tradition has sold out, but because devotees want the same things everyone else wants and have found a culturally specific way to ask for them.

The PuniCodex restoration of Ekkeko participates in this politics of recognition. By assigning the name a proper Unicode domain, the project refuses the colonial and bureaucratic habit of treating Indigenous spiritual vocabulary as folk material unworthy of precise spelling. The restoration is modest, technically speaking, but symbolic weight is not measured in diacritics. For a household deity whose power lies in being seen, being correctly named on the internet is its own kind of offering.

For scholars, devotees, and the simply curious, the restored name Ekkeko offers a place to begin: a doorway into Andean household religion, festival economics, and the enduring power of miniature wishes. As the internet becomes the primary archive of human names, restoring Ekkeko is a way of ensuring that household devotion and festival joy remain findable, citable, and respected.
