An Awakening-Being, Not Yet a Buddha (The Word)

The word is a compound: bodhi, awakening, plus ‌sattva, being — "one whose essence is awakening." In the Pāli canon bodhisatta is a technical term for the Buddha before his awakening: the Nidānakathā and the Mahāpadāna Sutta (DN 14) use ​it of Gotama's past lives, from the bodhisatta enthroned in Tuṣita down to the last birth at Lumbinī. The Mahāyāna inverted the lens: the bodhisattva is no longer the road one man ‌walked but a vocation open to all — one who deliberately defers final nirvāṇa until the grass of beings is dry, in the Sanskrit formula of the Kāraṇḍavyūha. The compound thus carries ​an entire schism in six syllables: the earlier tradition's biography against the later tradition's program. An awakening-being is the answer of a religion to its own founder's success: if one man woke, ‌the door was never locked.

The Promise That Builds a Bridge (The Vow)

Everything begins with a vow. The Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra — the final book of the Avataṃsaka — sends the pilgrim ​Sudhana through fifty-three teachers and ends inside Maitreya's tower, where past vows stand visible as architecture, each floor a former life; the praṇidhāna, "may I not sit on awakening's seat until every ‌being is freed," is the bodhisattva's founding act. Śāntideva's Bodhicaryāvatāra (8th c.) gives the vow its classic liturgy: "For as long as space endures, and for as long as beings remain, may ​I too abide, to dispel the misery of the world." The jātakas test the vow against the body itself — Prince Śibi giving his eyes to the hawk (Jātaka 499), Vessantara giving ​away his wife and children (Jātaka 547). The mythic kernel: a promise made not to a god but to the uncountable future — compassion as a contract written across lifetimes, signed before ‌any power existed that could enforce it.

From Joyful to Dharma-Cloud (The Ten Stages)

The Daśabhūmikasūtra maps the bodhisattva career as ten bhūmis, each a world of its own. Pramuditā, Joyful: the ‌first glimpse, irreversible; Vimalā, Stainless, where the ten courses of action are purified; Prabhākarī, Luminous, where the factors of awakening blaze; Arciṣmatī, Blazing; Sudurjayā, Difficult-to-Conquer; Abhimukhī, Facing — the turn toward suchness; ‌Dūraṅgamā, Gone-Afar; Acalā, Immovable, where nothing mundane can shake the vow; Sādhumatī, Good-Intelligence; and Dharmameghā, Dharma-Cloud, where Buddhas of the ten directions pour consecration from their crowns and the bodhisattva can rain ​the Dharma like a storm. The scheme seeded every later system — the five Maitreya treatises' summaries, the Tibetan graded paths, the Huayan's own rounds. Its meaning: enlightenment is not a moment ​but a landscape, surveyed in advance so that travelers can know exactly where they stand.
