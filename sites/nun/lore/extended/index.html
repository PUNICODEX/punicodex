Etymology & Word Family

The name Nnw reaches the modern world through scholarly transliteration of the egyptian tradition. Because the conventional spelling uses only Latin letters, its Unicode and ASCII forms coincide — the tradition it carries is in the name itself, not in diacritics.

Cult & Worship

How the sacred was kept — rites, sanctuaries, remains

The Cult

His cult was enacted rather than addressed: the flood’s arrival, the temple lake’s stillness, the purity-ablutions of every priest at dawn. Hymns call him ‘father who revived himself’ — the water that needs no cult because it is the condition of all cult.

In the Earth

No temple was exclusively Nun’s — water has no address — but every temple carried him: holy lakes, ‘ocean’ basins, and the primeval-mound imagery of the sanctuary floor. The Ogdoad’s frog and snake figures crowd the Late Period faience amulets found across Egypt.

05

Iconography

How Nnw was imaged across the ages

The Image

A man waist-deep in water, arms raised to lift the solar barque or the mound — the ḥꜣ gesture of uplift; or one of the Ogdoad’s frog-heads in profile. His hieroglyph is three rippling lines: the same sign that writes water everywhere in the language.

Reception & Transformation

The name after its own age — transmission, translation, survival

Afterlives

Greek doxographers listed Nun among the ‘Egyptian first-principles’, and the comparison with Thales became a commonplace of ancient philosophy’s self-history. Modern cosmology has its own echo: the quantum vacuum, the sea of potential from which the universe’s mound arose, reads uncannily like Nun re-described in mathematics.

Identifications Across Traditions

Nun is the substrate into which every creator-god’s theology sinks its roots: Atum self-created upon him, Amun hidden within him, Ptah speaking over him. In the late period he was invoked as ‘father of the gods’ while remaining, strictly, no-one’s child. Greek cosmologists drew the parallel with their own water-first doxographies — Thales’ ‘all is water’ found its Egyptian precedent in Nun.

Modern Legacy

The image of the world as a bubble of order in an infinite flood passed from Egyptian cosmogony into Hermetic and Gnostic speculation about the deep; and the Nile flood as ‘the return of the uncreated’ remains one of the most quoted ideas in Egyptological writing on time and renewal.

The Meditation

What Nnw means — beyond the dictionary

Nun is the shortest name with the largest referent in the entire lexicon: the water before names. To write him in ASCII is almost redundant — his element needs no spelling, only sounding. Read the name aloud and you have performed his nature: the open vowel, the held n, the open vowel again — a breathing in and out, the flood arriving and withdrawing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about Nnw, Primordial Waters, and Unicode restoration

01How do you pronounce Nnw?

In reconstructed pronunciation, Nnw is /nuːn/ (conventional) — approximately “NOON”.

02What does Nnw mean?

Nnw means The inert one, waters in the egyptian tradition.

03What are the symbols of Nnw?

Nnw is associated with The Primeval Water (The chaos-flood from which the first mound — and every creator — emerged.), Bearer of the Mound (He lifts the ben-ben: the primeval hill on which the creator stood to speak the world.), The Ogdoad Pair (With Naunet he heads the eight frog- and snake-headed forces of primordial inertness.), Surrounding Ocean (The world floats in him as a bubble in a flood; beyond the horizon, Nun resumes.).

04Why restore Nnw in Unicode?

Nnw is written entirely in ASCII letters, so its restoration is about canonical fidelity rather than recovered diacritics: the temple fixes the name in its scholarly standard spelling, attested in the source tradition, instead of leaving it to drift.

05What is the most important myth about Nnw?

“The Ogdoad and the First Mound” — the Hermopolitan Cosmogony story that anchors the mythology of Nnw, told in full on the lore page.
