01

Mythology

Stories of Nnw

Nun’s mythology is Egypt’s creation mythology seen from the water’s side. He never acts — that is his nature, and the meaning of his name, ‘the inert one’ — but everything that acts, acts upon or out of him. The creator gods of every temple city are alike in one thing: they stood on Nun’s surface and spoke.

Hermopolitan Cosmogony

The Ogdoad and the First Mound

At Hermopolis the theology ran: four frog-headed males and four snake-headed females — Nun and Naunet, Huh and Hauhet, Kuk and Kauket, Amun and Amaunet — swam in the primordial water until their inertness ripened into an egg, or a lotus, or the first mound. From that the sun rose. Nun heads the eight because water is first among the inert things.

Memphite Theology

The Word Upon the Waters

The Shabaka Stone’s Memphite account has Ptah conceiving the world in the heart and speaking it on the primeval waters: creation as a command uttered over Nun. The theology made the water the page and the god’s tongue the pen — the deepest Egyptian metaphor for creation as articulation.

Solar Books

The Sea That Holds the Barque

In the underworld books the sun’s night barque sails the Nun beneath the world — the primeval water is also the water below, the source of renewal. At dawn the barque re-emerges from Nun’s eastern flood: every sunrise is a re-creation, every night a return to the uncreated.

Flood Theology

The Inundation as Epiphany

The Egyptians called the annual flood simply ‘the arrival’; its waters were Nun revisiting the world, refertilising the mound. Temple holy lakes and the ‘Ocean of the Temple’ pools staged the same theology in architecture: a miniature primeval water at the god’s door, in which purity and creation are the same act.

02

God of the Primeval Waters

The Inert One, the Flood Before the World

Nnw — Nun — is not a god of things but of the nothing before things: the dark, inert, life-teeming water that existed before creation and surrounds the world still. Every cosmogony begins in him; every temple’s holy lake is his miniature; and every year the Nile’s flood re-stages his one eternal act — potential flooding into form.

The Primeval Water

The chaos-flood from which the first mound — and every creator — emerged.

Bearer of the Mound

He lifts the ben-ben: the primeval hill on which the creator stood to speak the world.

The Ogdoad Pair

With Naunet he heads the eight frog- and snake-headed forces of primordial inertness.

Surrounding Ocean

The world floats in him as a bubble in a flood; beyond the horizon, Nun resumes.

The Name

From the original script to the living URL

Original Script

𓈗

The name in its original Egyptian form. Nnw (𓈗) is attested in the source tradition — “The inert one, waters”. Its original diacritics and script distinctions carry the full phonetic and orthographic weight of the source tradition.

ASCII Constraint

nun

The plain nun form shares the canonical spelling. Because this name is already written in Latin letters, no recovered diacritics are required; capitalization follows the proper-noun convention.

Canonical Restoration

Nnw

The canonical spelling of Nnw needs no recovered marks. Its value is consistent citation, transparent provenance, and a stable digital home for the scholarly record. The address is readable as-is to both DNS and humanity.

ASCII-Clean Name
Nnw.com

This restoration is written entirely in ASCII letters — no special domain encoding stands between Nnw and the DNS. The domain reads identically to machines and to human eyes.
