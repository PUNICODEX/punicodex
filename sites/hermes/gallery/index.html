Praxiteles' Hermes

Praxiteles' Hermes and the Infant Dionysos

c. 340 BCE, Archaeological Museum of Olympia — The most famous sculpture of Hermes, showing him at rest, holding the infant god of wine.

Hermes Psychopomp

Hermes Psychopomp Vase Painting

Attic red-figure krater, c. 450 BCE — Hermes guides the soul of a warrior, caduceus in hand, across the boundary between life and death.

The Caduceus

The Caduceus in Roman Imperial Art

Roman marble relief, 1st century CE — The intertwined serpents of the caduceus rendered with serpentine precision, symbolizing commerce and negotiation.

Hermes & Argus

Hermes Slaying Argus Panoptes

Black-figure amphora, c. 540 BCE — The infant messenger, sword drawn, standing over the hundred-eyed giant whose eyes now close forever.

The Herm

The Roadside Herm

Marble herm from the Athenian Agora, c. 425 BCE — The stone pillar with Hermes' head and erect phallus that stood at every Athenian crossroad.

Hermes & Pandora

Hermes Leading Pandora to Epimetheus

Attic red-figure krater, c. 470 BCE — Hermes in his winged sandals leads the first woman, Pandora, to her destiny, the caduceus held like a shepherd's staff.
