Ꜣḫ at work: funerary & memorial services, biotech, longevity & life sciences, and the myths that got there first

Every age hires the same gods; only the job titles change. Ꜣḫ never stopped working.

The Ꜣḫ is the Egyptian self made effective. After death, once the ba has flown and the ka has been fed, once the heart has been weighed and found true, the justified dead becomes an akh—a luminous, potent spirit capable of speech, movement, and influence among gods and mortals. No longer merely a soul in pieces, the akh is the completed person, transfigured into a being of light and power who can travel freely through the Duat and even return to the world of the living. Egyptian letters to the dead address the akh by name, asking it to appear in dreams, heal the sick, or settle family disputes, treating the transfigured dead as an active member of the household rather than a distant memory. The transformation into an akh was not automatic; it required correct burial, continued offerings, and the successful navigation of the underworld. Magical spells therefore protected the akh from gatekeepers, demons, and the second death. In this theology, a name correctly spoken and a body correctly preserved were the twin guarantees of eternal agency.

The Myth at Work

The King Ascends as an Akh. In the Pyramid Texts, the oldest corpus of Egyptian religious literature, the king is repeatedly assured that he will ascend to the sky as an akh. Utterance 263 declares that he goes &ldquo;as an akh, as lord of the horizon,&rdquo; joining Re in his solar barque and taking his place among the circumpolar stars. — that is not just a story. It is a business model, four thousand years early.

The Transfiguration Spells. The Book of the Dead treats akh-hood as the corpus's goal-state. Spell 97 exists &ldquo;for causing a man to be an akh in the realm of the dead&rdquo;; Spell 130 gathers the disparate parts of the deceased's being into an effective akh with an eternal ba; and the rubrics of Spells 133 and 136A are &ldquo;for making a spirit excellent&rdquo; &mdash; in Egyptian, sꜣḫ, the causative of the very word ꜣḫ. — that is not just a story. It is a business model, four thousand years early.
