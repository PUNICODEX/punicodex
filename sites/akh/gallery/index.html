Weighing of the Heart

The Weighing of the Heart

Book of the Dead of Hunefer, c. 1275 BCE — Anubis kneels beside the scales, adjusting the beam, as the heart of the deceased is weighed against the feather of Ma'at. Thoth records the verdict. The justified soul becomes an akh.

Anubis Shrine

The Anubis Shrine of Tutankhamun

Tomb of Tutankhamun (KV62), c. 1323 BCE — A life-sized recumbent Anubis jackal, carved from wood and gilded, guarded the canopic shrine containing the king's embalmed viscera. The most famous depiction of Anubis in Egyptian art.

Opening of the Mouth

The Opening of the Mouth Ceremony

Tomb of Seti I (KV17), c. 1279 BCE — The sem priest, wearing a leopard skin and an Anubis mask, touches the mouth of the mummy with an adze, restoring the senses so the dead may breathe, speak, and eat in the afterlife.

Field of Reeds

The Field of Reeds

Tomb of Sennedjem (TT1), c. 1279 BCE — The Egyptian paradise, where the justified akh lives forever among abundant wheat, clear water, and the company of the gods. Anubis has guided the soul through the Duat to this final destination.

Canopic Jars

Canopic Equipment of Tutankhamun

Tomb of Tutankhamun, c. 1323 BCE — The four canopic jars, each with a lid shaped like the king's face, held the embalmed organs. The jars were protected by four goddesses — Isis, Nephthys, Selket, and Neith — and watched over by Anubis.

Pyramid Texts

Pyramid Texts of Unas

Pyramid of Unas, c. 2400 BCE — The oldest religious texts in the world, inscribed in blue hieroglyphs on the walls of the royal tomb. They contain the spells that transform the dead king into an akh and send him to join the imperishable stars.
