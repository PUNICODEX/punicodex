The Cycle, Read Forward

Manufacturing, Craft & Automation — through The Killing of Talos

Daidalos was born in Athens, where his skill as an inventor aroused the jealousy of his nephew Talos (or Perdix). When the boy invented the saw and the potter's wheel, Daidalos threw him from the Acropolis in envy.

Food & Hospitality — through The Labyrinth

At Knossos, Daidalos built the Labyrinth to house the Minotaur, the half-man, half-bull born of Pasiphae. The maze was so cunningly designed that even its maker could barely find his way out.

The atlas states the seat in one line: Resonant seat: Daidalos shares symbolic territory with food hospitality. The story above is the long version of the same sentence. The trade thinks it is optimizing a process. It is re-enacting a charter.

Gems, Jewelry & Luxury Materials — through The Wings

When Minos imprisoned Daidalos and his son Icarus in the Labyrinth, Daidalos made wings from feathers and wax. He warned Icarus not to fly too close to the sun, but the boy, exulting in flight, ignored him.

Textiles, Weaving & Networks — through Death of Daidalos

In Sicily, Daidalos built marvellous works for King Cocalus. When Minos came to demand his surrender, Cocalus' daughters killed Minos in his bath.

The Theme That Runs the Cycle

The myths of Daidalos are preserved in Homer, the lost Catalogue of Women, Diodorus Siculus, Ovid, and Apollodorus. They celebrate the power of human invention and warn against its costs: jealousy, exile, and the death of a son.
