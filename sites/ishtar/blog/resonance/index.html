The Cycle, Read Forward

Love, Dating & Relationships — through Inanna's Descent to the Underworld

In the Sumerian Descent of Inanna, the goddess abandons heaven and earth to descend to the netherworld ruled by her sister Ereshkigal. At each of the seven gates she is stripped of a garment or ornament until she stands naked before the throne of the dead.

The atlas states the seat in one line: Love and war together; Mesopotamia’s queen of hearts. The story above is the long version of the same sentence. The trade thinks it is optimizing a process. It is re-enacting a charter.

Beauty, Fashion & Cosmetics — through Ištar and the Bull of Heaven

In Tablet VI of the Epic of Gilgamesh, Ištar proposes marriage to Gilgamesh, king of Uruk. He rejects her, listing the fates of her previous mortal lovers — Dumuzi, the shepherd; the lion; the horse; the shepherd-bird; and the gardener Išullanu.

The atlas states the seat in one line: Love and glory; the adorned queen of heaven. The story above is the long version of the same sentence. That is the cycle working: story becomes duty, duty becomes trade.

Defense, Military & Security — through Dumuzi and Inanna

The Sumerian sacred-marriage songs celebrate the union of Inanna and Dumuzi, the shepherd-king. Their love ensures the fertility of the land and the legitimacy of the king.

The atlas states the seat in one line: Love and war in one figure; the Mesopotamian front line. The story above is the long version of the same sentence. Industries do not invent their archetypes; they inherit them and call them best practice.

Space, Astronomy & Celestial Science — through Ištar in the Enuma Elish and Atrahasis

In the Enuma Elish, Marduk's victory over Tiamat echoes older Sumerian narratives in which Inanna confronts the forces of chaos. In Atrahasis, Ištar/Belet-ili mourns the destruction of her human children and helps broker the post-diluvian order.

The atlas states the seat in one line: Venus as goddess; the planet carries her sign. The story above is the long version of the same sentence. The trade thinks it is optimizing a process. It is re-enacting a charter.

The Theme That Runs the Cycle

Ištar's myths are stories of extremity: descent into death, demand for the impossible, and the transformation of gender and power. She is never merely an object of desire; she is desire in motion, war in motion, the star that crosses the boundary between heaven and the underworld.
