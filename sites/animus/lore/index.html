The Authentic Name

Unicode restoration and ASCII comparison

Original Script

ANIMUS

The name in its original Greek form. Animus (ANIMUS) is attested in the source tradition — “Mind, spirit, courage”. Its original diacritics and script distinctions carry the full phonetic and orthographic weight of the source tradition.

ASCII Constraint

animus

The plain animus form is identical to the Unicode restoration. Because this name is already written in Latin letters, no diacritics, stress, or script information were lost — only capitalization differs.

Unicode Restoration

Animus

The Unicode restoration does not need to recover lost marks for Animus. Its value is canonical spelling and consistent cataloguing, not the reconstruction of erased orthography. The domain is readable as-is to both DNS and humanity.

Punycode Encoding
Animus.com → animus.com

The non-ASCII characters in Animus are encoded while the ASCII remains visible. To the DNS, it is Punycode. To humanity, it is Animus.

'AH-nih-moos' — stress the first syllable; the final vowel is short and rounded.

  • Latin animus, mind, spirit, courage, wrath
  • Greek equivalent θυμός (thymos), spiritedness; νοῦς (nous), mind

The Accent / Script Rule

Animus is a Latin word, not a Greek proper name. It has no indigenous alphabetic 'original script' other than the Latin alphabet. The ASCII form animus is already the Latin spelling; the restoration capitalises the proper-noun form as a scholarly citation. Because no distinctive diacritic or special letter is added, the entry is Tier 2.

05

Mind, Spirit, and Courage

The Active Power of the Soul

Animus is the Latin word for mind, spirit, courage, and sometimes fierce anger. It names the active, directing part of the person: the faculty that thinks, decides, resists, and fights. Where anima is breath and life, animus is will and consciousness. Roman ethics placed great weight on the animus as the seat of virtue, and the word survives in English as a sign of both spiritedness and settled hostility.

Mind

The rational faculty that deliberates, remembers, and plans.

Spirit

The animating energy of character and resolve.

Courage

The quality that enables a person to face danger without yielding.

Wrath

Animus can also mean fierce temper — the same energy turned destructive.

Sacred Symbols

Heart The seat of courage and emotional resolve in Roman and modern symbolism.
Sword The instrument of courage and the willingness to act with force.
Flame The fire of spirit, ambition, and anger that drives action.
Brow or forehead The visible place of mind and determination, where animus shows on the face.
06

Mythology

Stories of Animus

Animus is not a mythological character but a concept that runs through Roman literature, rhetoric, and ethics. Its 'mythology' is the story of how Latin speakers imagined the active self: the mind that commands, the spirit that endures, and the anger that destroys.

Roman virtue

The Seat of Courage

For Roman writers such as Cicero and Livy, the animus is the source of virtus, the courage that makes a citizen worthy of the state. Scipio Africanus, Horatius at the bridge, and the unnamed soldiers of the Roman legions are all praised for the strength of their animus. The word carries the conviction that character is an inner force that outward action reveals.

Stoic philosophy

The Ruling Faculty

Roman Stoics such as Seneca and Marcus Aurelius identified the animus with the *hegemonikon*, the ruling part of the soul. The goal of philosophy was to make the animus tranquil, rational, and independent of fortune. Passions such as anger were diseases of the animus, to be cured by reason and self-examination.

Epic poetry

The Wrath of the Mind

Virgil uses animus to describe both noble resolution and violent fury. Aeneas is praised for an animus that does not break under suffering; Turnus is driven by an animus that rages against fate. The same word thus contains the whole moral range of Roman heroism, from self-control to self-destructive passion.

Modern psychology

Jung's Animus

In Jungian psychology the animus is the unconscious masculine image in the female psyche, the spirit-figure that mediates between the ego and the unconscious. Like anima, the term preserves the Latin sense of soul while taking on a new psychological meaning: the animus is the inner mind that thinks, argues, and inspires.

Extended Lore

To meditate on Animus is to meditate on the will. The Latin word contains both the noblest and the most dangerous forms of spiritedness: courage and wrath, resolution and resentment. The Stoics were right to see the animus as a faculty that needs governance. Without reason it becomes mere force; with reason it becomes virtue.
