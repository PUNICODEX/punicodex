The Myth at Work

Fourth of the Primordials. Hesiod places Érōs fourth in the procession of being: after Cháos, Gaia, and Tartarus, but before the children of Night. "He is the most beautiful among the immortal gods," Hesiod writes, "he makes the limbs go limp and overcomes the intelligence and prudent counsel in the breasts of all gods and men" (Theogony 120–122). — hold that image; the industries below are where it goes to work.

First of All. The pre-Socratic philosopher Parmenides made Érōs the first of all gods to come into being — not a child of Aphrodite but the primal force of attraction that organizes reality. In this philosophical reading, love is not an emotion; it is a cosmological principle. — hold that image; the industries below are where it goes to work.
