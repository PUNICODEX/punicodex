Ogma walks into a defense, military & security firm

Somewhere right now, in a defense, military & security office, someone is doing exactly what the myths describe — and has never heard of Ogma. This file is the introduction.

Before the Office, the Story

The Son of Elatha. The Lebor Gabála Érenn, Ireland's medieval book of invasions, writes Ogma into the divine genealogy: Ogma son of Elatha, of the line of Delbaeth, and brother of the Dagda himself. When the Túatha Dé Danann came to Ireland out of the four northern cities, Ogma came with them as the champion of the race — in trenfer, the strong man, the standing title the texts give him. His place in the pantheon is therefore double from the first mention: the arm of the Túatha Dé Danann, and the brother of their all-father.

The Firewood of the Serf-Years. When the half-Fomorian Bres was made king and laid the Túatha Dé Danann under tribute, the greatest of them were put to work like serfs. The Dagda was set to building raths, and Ogma — the champion of the whole divine race — was set to hauling firewood for the fortress. The tale does not spare him: so weakened was he by hunger that the sea washed over him as he carried the fuel.

Not the First Rebrand

Ogma has the strongest cross-border witness of any Irish god, and it is a classical author's eyewitness account. Lucian of Samosata, writing in the second century CE, describes a picture the Gauls showed him of their Herakles: not the young Greek athlete but a very old man, bald and wrinkled and sun-dark, drawing after him a great crowd of men — each one fastened by the ears to the god's tongue with slender chains of gold and amber, and following willingly, none struggling. The office is only the latest translation.
