The provenance file on Ītzpāpālōtl

Catalogue entry — Ītzpāpālōtl. Every claim below carries its chain of custody.

Provenance

1. The attestation. —, from the tradition's own record. On record: Sahagún, Florentine Codex, Codex Borgia, Karttunen, An Analytical Dictionary of Nahuatl, López Austin, Tamoanchan, Tlalocan.

2. The preserved evidence. i → Ī (Macron: long /iː/); a → ā (Macron: long /aː/); a → ā (Macron: long /aː/); o → ō (Macron: long /oː/) — absent from the ASCII form itzpapalotl, present in the restoration, verifiable against the sources above.

3. The narrative record. The Lady of Tamoanchan — Tamoanchan is the mythical paradise of origins, a lush mountain of flowers and fruit where the creator couple dwelt. Itzpapālōtl rules there as both guardian and destroyer, her obsidian wings protecting the tree from which the first humans were fashioned. The Cihuateteo — Women who died in childbirth were not ordinary dead. They became Cihuateteo, 'divine women', fierce spirits who accompanied the sun from zenith to sunset and prowled crossroads at night.

4. The current holding. The temple at /sites/itzpapalotl/, classified Tier 1. What survives here cannot be shown in ASCII: a feature of the original that the fallback alphabet flattens. That is the Tier-1 test, and this name passes it without argument.

5. The documented resonances. Gems, Jewelry & Luxury Materials, Women's Health & Femtech, Astrology, Tarot & Esoteric Services — seats argued and weighted in the pattern atlas, read through the myths in this temple's Resonance File.

6. The continuing record. Itzpapālōtl survives where Mesoamerican antiquity meets contemporary creativity. Her obsidian-butterfly form has inspired jewelry, tattoos, graphic novels, and installations that explore the beauty of the lethal.

The Emblems
