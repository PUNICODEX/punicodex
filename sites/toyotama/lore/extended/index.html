Cultural Significance

From ancient cult to modern Unicode

Ancient Domain

Toyotama is the daughter of the sea god Ryūjin and the wife of Hoori. Her name, 'Abundant Jewel,' evokes the treasures of the undersea palace. She is the mother of Ugayafukiaezu, making her an ancestor of Emperor Jimmu and a bridge between the ocean depths and the imperial line.

Toyotama in Later Traditions

Toyotama belongs to a widespread East Asian motif: the animal bride whose true form must not be seen. Her story parallels Chinese and Korean dragon-palace tales, but in Japan it was woven into the imperial genealogy. She also overlaps with other sea-women and dragon daughters in Japanese folklore.

Modern Legacy

Toyotama is remembered as a tragic mother and a link between the sea and the imperial house. Her story appears in Noh, folk tales, and modern retellings of Japanese myth. The Ryūgū motif remains popular in Japanese art and entertainment.

Unicode Restoration as Cultural Act

Restoring Toyotama in a domain name is more than orthographic accuracy. It is a statement that the internet should recognize the full range of human writing — not only the ASCII keyboard.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about Toyotama, Sea, Mother of Jimmu, and Unicode restoration

01How do you pronounce Toyotama?

In reconstructed pronunciation, Toyotama is /to.jo.ta.ma/ — approximately 'TOH-yoh-tah-mah' — four even syllables, with the second beginning with a soft 'y'..

02What does Toyotama mean?

Toyotama means Abundant jewel in the japanese tradition.

03What are the symbols of Toyotama?

Toyotama is associated with Jewel (The abundance and treasure of the sea), Sea palace (Ryūgū) (The undersea realm where she was born), Dragon or crocodile (Her true form, seen by Hoori when he broke his promise), Cormorant feather robe (The garment that allowed travel between sea and land).

04Why restore Toyotama in Unicode?

Plain ASCII toyotama strips the stress, length, and script that make the name specific. Unicode restoration returns the name to its original written dignity.

05What is the most important myth about Toyotama?

When Hoori descended to the undersea palace seeking the lost fishhook, Ryūjin welcomed him and gave him Toyotama in marriage. For three years they lived in the palace, and Toyotama became pregnant. Hoori longed for the land, and she agreed to accompany him.
