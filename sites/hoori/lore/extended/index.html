Cultural Significance

From ancient cult to modern Unicode

Ancient Domain

Hoori is the younger son of Ninigi and Konohana-Sakuya-hime, the husband of the sea princess Toyotama, and the great-grandfather of Japan's first emperor, Jimmu. His name, 'Fire-Subduing,' marks him as the brother who overcame Hoderi's fire and whose line would rule the land.

Hoori in Later Traditions

Hoori's story fuses several themes common across East Asia: the mortal who visits the dragon palace, the animal bride whose true form must not be seen, and the younger brother who supersedes the elder. These motifs have parallels in Chinese, Korean, and Ainu narratives, but in Japan they were organized into a single genealogy culminating in the emperor.

Modern Legacy

Hoori is remembered chiefly as an ancestor, but his story has inspired countless retellings. The dragon-palace motif appears in folk tales, Noh plays, and modern fiction. His descendants' claim to rule both land and sea shaped Japanese political mythology for centuries.

Unicode Restoration as Cultural Act

Restoring Hoori in a domain name is more than orthographic accuracy. It is a statement that the internet should recognize the full range of human writing — not only the ASCII keyboard.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about Hoori, Agriculture, Hunter, and Unicode restoration

01How do you pronounce Hoori?

In reconstructed pronunciation, Hoori is /hoː.ɾi/ — approximately 'HOH-ree' — hold the first syllable long; the 'r' is a light tap..

02What does Hoori mean?

Hoori means Fire-subduing in the japanese tradition.

03What are the symbols of Hoori?

Hoori is associated with Bow (The weapon Ninigi gave him, symbolizing rule over the mountains), Fishhook (The lost and recovered hook that brought his brother Hoderi to submission), Sea palace (Ryūgū) (The undersea realm where he won Toyotama), Cormorant feather robe (The gift that allowed him to travel between land and sea).

04Why restore Hoori in Unicode?

Plain ASCII hoori strips the stress, length, and script that make the name specific. Unicode restoration returns the name to its original written dignity.

05What is the most important myth about Hoori?

After exchanging gifts with his brother Hoderi, Hoori received the fishhook but lost it in the sea. Hoderi refused any substitute. Desperate, Hoori descended to the undersea palace of Ryūjin, the dragon god of the sea, where he was welcomed and married to the sea god's daughter, Toyotama.
