The Authentic Name

Unicode restoration and ASCII comparison

Original Script

火折命

The name in its original Japanese form. Hoori (火折命) is attested in the source tradition — “Fire-subduing”. Its original diacritics and script distinctions carry the full phonetic and orthographic weight of the source tradition.

ASCII Constraint

hoori

The plain hoori form is identical to the Unicode restoration. Because this name is already written in Latin letters, no diacritics, stress, or script information were lost — only capitalization differs.

Unicode Restoration

Hoori

The Unicode restoration does not need to recover lost marks for Hoori. Its value is canonical spelling and consistent cataloguing, not the reconstruction of erased orthography. The domain is readable as-is to both DNS and humanity.

Punycode Encoding
Hoori.com → hoori.com

The non-ASCII characters in Hoori are encoded while the ASCII remains visible. To the DNS, it is Punycode. To humanity, it is Hoori.

Pronunciation

How Hoori was spoken

/hoː.ɾi/ Modern Standard Japanese (Hepburn)
Hō- Voiceless glottal [h] plus long open-mid back [oː]; from 火 'fire'
-ri Alveolar tap [ɾ] plus close front [i]; from 折 'to subdue/break'

Modern Approximation

'HOH-ree' — hold the first syllable long; the 'r' is a light tap.

Etymological Kin

  • Japanese 火折命 (Hoori-no-mikoto), 'Fire-Subduing Prince'
  • Brother Hoderi (Fire-Shining Prince), his elder brother
  • Descendant Great-grandfather of Emperor Jimmu through his son Ugayafukiaezu

The Accent / Script Rule

Hoori is Tier 2 in this restoration: the long ō is not represented, so the ASCII form is identical to Hepburn. He is a pivotal ancestor in the imperial genealogy.

05

Fire-Subduing Prince and Imperial Ancestor

Hunter, Sea-Voyager, and Founder of the Royal Line

Hoori is the younger son of Ninigi and Konohana-Sakuya-hime, the husband of the sea princess Toyotama, and the great-grandfather of Japan's first emperor, Jimmu. His name, 'Fire-Subduing,' marks him as the brother who overcame Hoderi's fire and whose line would rule the land.

Master of the Mountains

He received the bow and became a hunter of the highlands.

Husband of Toyotama

Married the daughter of the sea god Ryūjin in the undersea palace.

Father of Ugayafukiaezu

His son by Toyotama continued the line to Emperor Jimmu.

Imperial Forebear

His story explains how the imperial line gained legitimacy from both sea and land.

Sacred Symbols

Bow The weapon Ninigi gave him, symbolizing rule over the mountains
Fishhook The lost and recovered hook that brought his brother Hoderi to submission
Sea palace (Ryūgū) The undersea realm where he won Toyotama
Cormorant feather robe The gift that allowed him to travel between land and sea
06

Mythology

Stories of Hoori

Hoori's mythology is the bridge between the heavenly descent of Ninigi and the earthly reign of Jimmu. It is a story of exile, marriage, and return.

Kojiki

The Lost Fishhook

After exchanging gifts with his brother Hoderi, Hoori received the fishhook but lost it in the sea. Hoderi refused any substitute. Desperate, Hoori descended to the undersea palace of Ryūjin, the dragon god of the sea, where he was welcomed and married to the sea god's daughter, Toyotama.

Kojiki

The Promise and the Broken Taboo

Toyotama agreed to accompany Hoori to land on the condition that he not watch her give birth. Curiosity overcame him; he peeked and saw her in her true form as a sea dragon or crocodile. Ashamed, Toyotama returned to the sea, leaving their son Ugayafukiaezu to be raised on land. She sent her sister Tamayori to care for the child.

Kojiki

Founder of the Line

Ugayafukiaezu married his aunt Tamayori, and their son was Kamuyamato-Iwarebiko, later known as Emperor Jimmu. Thus Hoori's voyage to the sea and back became the mythical foundation of the imperial line.

Extended Lore

Hoori is the one who looked. His curiosity cost him the presence of his divine wife, yet it also produced the imperial line. His story suggests that legitimacy is born not from perfect obedience but from the risks taken in pursuit of knowledge and connection.
