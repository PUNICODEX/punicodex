The provenance file on Forseti

Catalogue entry — Forseti. Every claim below carries its chain of custody.

Provenance

1. The attestation. ᚠᚢᚱᛋᛁᛏᛁ, from the tradition's own record. On record: Poetic Edda: Grímnismál 15 (Glitnir, 'propped with gold and thatched with silver'; Forseti, who 'puts all suits to sleep'), Snorri Sturluson, Prose Edda: Gylfaginning 17 (Glitnir among the heavenly halls) and 32 (Forseti, son of Baldr and Nanna; the best court of judgment among gods and men); Skáldskaparmál þulur (Forseti in the roll of the Æsir), Alcuin, Vita Willibrordi 10 (the island Fosetesland, the silent spring, the sacred cattle, Radbod's lots), ed. W. Levison, MGH Scriptores rerum Merovingicarum 7, Jacob Grimm, Deutsche Mythologie (the Fosite equation and the legend of the thirteenth law-speaker), trans. Stallybrass as Teutonic Mythology.

2. The narrative record. The Tenth Hall — Grímnismál counts the dwellings of the gods, and the tenth is his: 'Glitnir is the tenth; it is propped with gold and thatched with silver too, and there Forseti dwells most days and puts all suits to sleep. ' The verb is the miracle — svæfir, he lulls: not wins, not crushes, but puts to sleep, the way one settles a restless child. The Best Court Among Gods and Men — Snorri builds the same hall twice. In the survey of the heavenly places, Glitnir stands with walls, pillars, and posts of red gold and a roof of silver.

3. The physical record. Forseti is a textual and onomastic god: no runestone invokes him, no image or amulet names him, and no votive deposit can be laid at his hall. The ground evidence is the place-name: Forsetlund, 'Forseti's grove', in Vestfold near the Oslo fjord, attests that the name lived in Scandinavian cult-geography and not only in Icelandic books. The Frisian witness is earlier than any Norse manuscript: Alcuin's Life of Willibrord (c. 796) preserves the island Fosetesland — Heligoland, 'holy land' — with its silent spring and sacred herd, the only near-contemporary description of the cult site of the god-name's continental form. The Norse literary witnesses are the Codex Regius (Grímnismál) and the fourteenth-century codices of the Prose Edda. The Younger Futhark rendering ᚠᚢᚱᛋᛁᛏᛁ is a normalized reconstruction of how the name would have been carved; no Viking-Age inscription of the theonym is known.

4. The current holding. The temple at /forseti/, classified Tier 2. Tier-2 here means one preserved truth — not less important, just singular. The temple shows it and names it.

5. The documented resonances. Legal, Justice & Compliance, Faith & Spiritual Organizations, Leadership, Governance & Public Administration — seats argued and weighted in the pattern atlas, read through the myths in this temple's Resonance File.

6. The primary sources. Poetic Edda, Prose Edda — the library this file answers to, open at the Sacred Texts collection.

7. The continuing record. Forseti's afterlife is the word itself. Old Norse forseti was the law-term for the presiding judge — the one who sits before the court — and when Iceland became a republic in 1944 it reached for the same word for its head of state: the President of Iceland is to this day Forseti Íslands, and no other god of the North still signs laws.
