The Authentic Name

Unicode restoration and ASCII comparison

Original Script

하나님

The name in its original Korean form. Hwanin (하나님) is attested in the source tradition — “Lord of Heaven”. Its original diacritics and script distinctions carry the full phonetic and orthographic weight of the source tradition.

ASCII Constraint

hananim

The plain hananim form is identical to the Unicode restoration. Because this name is already written in Latin letters, no diacritics, stress, or script information were lost — only capitalization differs.

Unicode Restoration

Hwanin

The Unicode restoration does not need to recover lost marks for Hwanin. Its value is canonical spelling and consistent cataloguing, not the reconstruction of erased orthography. The domain is readable as-is to both DNS and humanity.

Punycode Encoding
Hwanin.com → hwanin.com

The non-ASCII characters in Hwanin are encoded while the ASCII remains visible. To the DNS, it is Punycode. To humanity, it is Hwanin.

Hwanin, Lord of Heaven

the supreme source of the Dangun founding myth

Hwanin is the supreme celestial figure who rules from heaven in the Dangun founding myth in Korean tradition. The name means "Lord of Heaven" and belongs to the domain "Supreme God, Heaven." In the Samguk Yusa, Hwanin is the ruler of heaven who answers the Buddha's request for a land fit for his teachings by sending his son Hwanung to earth. Hwanin is not a creator in the biblical sense; he is the apex of a celestial hierarchy that authorises the descent of culture and law to the Korean peninsula.

Lord of Heaven

Hwanin sits at the apex of the Korean celestial order, the source from which legitimate authority descends.

Celestial Court

Heaven is imagined as a court with ministers, retainers, and an ethical law that mirrors human government.

Ancestor of Hwanung

Hwanin authorises the descent that makes possible the birth of Dangun and the founding of Korea.

Moral Order

The myth encodes the belief that rulers and peoples are accountable to a higher heavenly standard.

Sacred Symbols

The heavens The celestial realm from which Hwanin dispenses authority and blessing.
The sandalwood tree The axis linking heaven and earth in the Dangun myth; the place where Hwanung alighted.
The heavenly seal The token of delegated authority that Hwanin gives to Hwanung.
Sun and moon Celestial emblems of the luminous, ordering presence of heaven.
06

Mythology

Stories of Hwanin

The mythology of Hwanin is preserved in Korean oral tradition, shamanic ritual texts, and classical sources such as the Samguk Yusa. The narratives are not merely stories; they encode social values, ritual obligations, and relationships between humans, nature, and the sacred. Some details vary by region and by shamanic lineage.

Samguk Yusa

The Request from Heaven

According to Iryeon's Samguk Yusa, the Buddha asked Hwanin whether there was a land on earth where his teachings could flourish. Hwanin looked down and saw the Korean peninsula, then offered his son Hwanung to descend with heavenly attendants and sacred regalia.

Foundation myth

The Descent of Hwanung

Hwanin granted Hwanung three heavenly seals and three thousand retainers. Hwanung descended beneath a sandalwood tree on a high mountain, bringing laws, grains, and the arts of civilisation to the human world.

Living usage

Hananim and Korean Religion

The vernacular title Hananim became the standard Korean Christian name for God, while in folk and shamanic contexts Hwanin remains the celestial ancestor of the Korean people. The two usages live in parallel, one theological and one mythological.
