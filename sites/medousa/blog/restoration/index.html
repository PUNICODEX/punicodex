Médousa: the stories first, then the spelling

You cannot weigh an accent without knowing what it carries. Here is what Médousa has been carrying.

The Stories That Carry the Name

From Maiden to Monster. In Ovid's Metamorphoses (4. 790–803), Médousa was a maiden famed for her beauty and above all for her hair. Poseidôn raped her in Athena's temple, and Athena, enraged at the desecration of her temple, transformed Médousa's beautiful hair into serpents.

Perseus and the Head. Perseus, sent by Polydectes to fetch the Gorgon's head, used Athena's polished shield as a mirror to approach Médousa without looking directly at her. While she slept, he struck off her head with a sickle given by Hermês. From her neck sprang Pegasus and the warrior Chrysaor.

The Gorgoneion. Perseus gave Médousa's head to Athena, who fixed it in the center of her aegis. The Gorgoneion became the most powerful apotropaic image in Greek art, warding off evil with the very power of the monster. It appears on shields, temple pediments, coins, and doorways throughout the Greek and Roman world.

And Here Is What the Spelling Preserves

Against those stories, the attestation: Μέδουσα, as LSJ and Beekes record. The name means "Guardian, ruler (from μέδω)".

The restoration keeps one feature the ASCII form medousa cannot show:

  • e → é — Acute on epsilon: where the voice peaks.

A storyteller pronouncing the name would restore every one of them by instinct. The file only writes down what the voice already knows.

The Name Travels

The Romans adopted Médousa directly, and her image proliferated on shields, mosaics, and architectural elements. In Neoplatonism she became a symbol of the material world that must be transcended — the petrifying gaze of illusion.

The Address It Earned

The temple of Médousa stands at /sites/medousa/ — lore, scholars, patterns — because a name carrying this much story deserves more than a parking page. What survives here cannot be shown in ASCII: a feature of the original that the fallback alphabet flattens. That is the Tier-1 test, and this name passes it without argument.
