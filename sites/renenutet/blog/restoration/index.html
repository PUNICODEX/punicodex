Rnnwtt: the stories first, then the spelling

Before the spelling, the story. Rnnwtt earns its restoration the old way — through what was actually told.

The Stories That Carry the Name

The Cobra in the Last Sheaf. Egyptian reapers left the last sheaf standing and addressed it as Renenutet: the goddess still resident in the field. To cut it was to release her into the granary for the year’s safekeeping. The custom — attested from the Old Kingdom harvest scenes onward — made every field’s end a small theological drama.

Mother of the Vineyard. In the Fayum and at Dendera she presided over the wine-press as over the threshing-floor; the wine-jar docket ‘Renenutet’s portion’ marked the divine tithe of the vintage. With Sobek as her consort in the Fayum she mothered the crop-cycle whole — grain, grape, and the flood that feeds both.

Nurse of the King. The king’s titulary calls him ‘the nursling of Renenutet’: the harvest feeds the state as the breast feeds the child, and both are her body. Royal birth-scenes show her cobra rearing behind the throne of the nursing mother, transferring the goddess’ own milk into the legitimate succession.

The Name Travels

Renenutet merged early with Wadjet, the cobra of Lower Egypt — both are the rearing uraeus — and with Meretseger of the Theban necropolis in her serpent aspect. As Thermouthis she was equated with Isis and Demeter, the two great maternal goddesses of the classical world; the triangulation made her the most exported of Egypt’s agricultural deities.
