The Cycle, Read Forward

Writing, Publishing & Media — through The Household by Llyn Tegid

In the beginning, the tale says, Ceridwen lived beside Llyn Tegid in the land of Penllyn, the wife of Tegid Foel — Tegid the Bald — whose name the lake still carries. They had two children whose fortunes were weighed in opposite scales: a daughter, Creirwy, the fairest maiden in the world, and a son, Morfran, so dark and ill-favoured that he was called Afagddu, Utter Darkness.

Agriculture, Food & Harvest — through The Cauldron of a Year and a Day

From the books of the feryllt — the Welsh name for the alchemist's art — Ceridwen compounded a cauldron of Inspiration and Science. It must boil a year and a day, ceaselessly, until three drops of the spirit of inspiration should be won from it.

The atlas states the seat in one line: The year-long brew of herbs and grains — inspiration as crop. The story above is the long version of the same sentence. Industries do not invent their archetypes; they inherit them and call them best practice.

Astrology, Tarot & Esoteric Services — through The Three Drops and the Flight of Shapes

At the year's end the three charmed drops leapt from the cauldron — and fell, not on Morfran, but on the finger of Gwion Bach. The boy put his burning finger in his mouth, and in that instant all the awen was his: he knew all that was past and all that was to come, and knew above all that Ceridwen would kill him.

The Theme That Runs the Cycle

Ceridwen's whole dossier is a single tale — Hanes Taliesin, the Tale of Taliesin — preserved in sixteenth-century Welsh manuscripts and customarily printed with the Mabinogion, though it is none of the Four Branches. Within that tale she owns its great engine: the cauldron, the hunt, and the rebirth.
