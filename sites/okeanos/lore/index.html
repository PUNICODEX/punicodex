The Authentic Name

Why Ōkeanós.com is the correct form

Original Script

Ὠκεανός

The name in its original Greek form. Ōkeanós (Ὠκεανός) is attested as ocean, fresh water — “The great river encircling the world”. Its acute accents carry the full phonetic and orthographic weight of the source tradition.

ASCII Constraint

okeanos

Reduced to plain okeanos, the name loses everything that made it specific: acute accents. What remains is an ASCII string that machines can parse but that no longer speaks with its original voice.

Unicode Restoration

Ōkeanós

The Unicode restoration recovers what ASCII flattened. Ōkeanós restores acute accents, returning the name to its original written dignity. The domain encodes to Punycode, but the browser displays the truth.

Punycode Encoding

The non-ASCII characters in Ōkeanós are encoded while the ASCII remains visible. To the DNS, it is Punycode. To humanity, it is Ōkeanós.

Pronunciation

How Ōkeanós was spoken

/reconstructed/ Greek Approximation
Vowels Ancient Greek had twelve vowels, including long and short pairs; the restored form preserves length where it matters.
Accent The circumflex marks a rise-and-fall pitch on a long vowel, the signature of elevated diction.
Consonants Classical Greek distinguished aspirated stops (ph, th, kh) from plain ones; these are not English f, th, or kh.

Modern Approximation

Traditional English pronunciation tends to stress the first syllable, but the ancient pitch accent gave the name a rise and fall that modern English does not reproduce.

04

Mythology

Stories of Ōkeanós

Okeanos is the great river that encircles the world, the eldest of the Titans, and the source from which all seas, rivers, and springs derive. Unlike most of his generation, he remains apart from the wars of Olympus, a figure of boundary, origin, and inexhaustible flow.

Cosmography

The River Around the World

Hesiod calls Okeanos 'the perfect river', and Homer places him at the edge of the earth where the sun rises and sets. This is not an ordinary sea but a circular stream that bounds the habitable world. Beyond it lie the strange lands of dawn and dusk; within it move all the waters known to mortals.

Genealogy

Father of the Oceanids

With his sister and wife Tethys, Okeanos fathers three thousand Oceanids — the nymphs of springs, streams, and clouds — and as many Potamoi, the river-gods. This vast progeny makes him the ancestor of every watery place in Greece and beyond. Even the underworld rivers Styx, Acheron, and Cocytus are numbered among his children.

Titanomachy

Neutral in the War of the Gods

When the Olympians warred against the Titans, Okeanos refused to take arms against Zeus. Some sources say he even sheltered his daughter Metis, who would later bear Athena to Zeus. His neutrality preserved him when the other Titans were cast into Tartarus; he continued to flow around the world as he had from the beginning.

Source

Source of All Waters

Every river and every sea is believed to flow from Okeanos. He is the backwater of the world, the reservoir that never runs dry. In cult, sailors and travellers prayed to him; in philosophy, his circling stream became an image of the boundless and the eternal. To invoke Okeanos is to invoke the edge of the known.
