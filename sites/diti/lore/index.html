01

Mythology

Stories of Diti

Diti’s mythology is her maternity: everything the Daityas do, she enables — and one thing she undoes, in the Maruts episode, where Indra’s violence against her pregnant body produces the storm-gods from the womb’s fragments. She is the anti-mother of the anti-gods, the bound one whose children strain against every bond except hers.

Ṛgveda 1.99

The Maruts from the Broken Womb

The oldest Diti-story has Indra hurl the vajra into her womb, shattering a hundred sons in embryo; the fragments become the Maruts, the storm-gods who yet serve Indra. Diti’s grief and the storm-gods’ loyalty are the hymn’s tension: the anti-mother’s broken children, adopted by her enemy.

Purāṇic Cycle

The Boon of Hiraṇyakaśipu

Her son Hiraṇyakaśipu, devastated by his brother Hiraṇyākṣa’s death at Varāha’s tusk, won from Brahmā the boon that he could not die by day or night, indoors or out, by man or beast, on earth or sky. The boon made him lord of the three worlds — until Narasiṃha, the man-lion at dusk on a threshold, ended him. Diti’s line is the engine of the avatāra cycle.

Prahlāda Cycle

The Grandson Who Worshipped the Enemy

Prahlāda, son of Hiraṇyakaśipu and thus Diti’s grandson, loved Vishnu from the womb — the devotee born in the anti-god’s house. The Purāṇas make his devotion the theology of bhakti itself: the bound dynasty’s finest fruit was the boy who could not be burned, poisoned, or thrown, for his god held him.

Genealogical Frame

Daughter of Dakṣa, Wife of Kaśyapa

The Purāṇic theogonies make Diti one of Dakṣa’s thirteen daughters given to Kaśyapa: Aditi mothers the Ādityas, Diti the Daityas, Danu the Dānavas, and the rest of creation’s factions follow the wives. The marriage-bed of Kaśyapa is, in the genealogists’ scheme, the factory of the cosmos’s parties.

02

Mother of the Daityas

The Bound One, Mother of the Anti-Gods

Diti is the mother of the anti-gods: the Daityas, the demonic dynasty whose wars with the gods drive half the Purāṇic mythology. Daughter of Dakṣa, wife of the sage Kaśyapa, twin in name of the unfettered Aditi, she mothers the bound ones — the race of Hiraṇyākṣa and Hiraṇyakaśipu, whose boons made them nearly invincible.

Mother of the Daityas

04

The Name

From the original script to the living URL

Original Script

दिति

The name in its original Sanskrit form. Diti (दिति) is attested in the source tradition — “Daughter of Dakṣa and wife of the sage Kaśyapa, mother of the Daityas, the demon race — the counterpart of her sister Aditi, who is mother of the gods.”. Its original diacritics and script distinctions carry the full phonetic and orthographic weight of the source tradition.

ASCII Constraint

diti

The plain diti form shares the canonical spelling. Because this name is already written in Latin letters, no recovered diacritics are required; capitalization follows the proper-noun convention.

Canonical Restoration

Diti

The canonical spelling of Diti needs no recovered marks. Its value is consistent citation, transparent provenance, and a stable digital home for the scholarly record. The address is readable as-is to both DNS and humanity.

ASCII-Clean Name
Diti.com

This restoration is written entirely in ASCII letters — no special domain encoding stands between Diti and the DNS. The domain reads identically to machines and to human eyes.

Pronunciation

How Diti was spoken

/ˈdiːti/ Sanskrit (IAST: Diti)
Di- Long ī as in “see”; the name is a-diti with the negation removed.
-ti The feminine abstract ending; the name means “the bound one”.
The sister of Aditi — boundless — and mother of the anti-gods.

Modern Approximation

“DEE-tee”

The full word family — cognates, derivatives, and variant spellings — is traced in Extended Lore → Etymology.

The Accent / Script Rule

The name is a mirror of her sister’s: where Aditi is “un-bound”, Diti is the “bound” — and her sons are the gods’ enemies.
