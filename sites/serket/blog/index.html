Mythology

Serket is a guardian more than a protagonist: she appears wherever protection is most needed, arms outspread over the thing she shields. Her ​mythic presence clusters around the dying god — she is one of the four goddesses of the canopic equipment in the Osiris cycle — and around the threatened child, ‌above all the young Horus hidden in the Chemmis marshes.

The Four Guardians of the Dead (Osiris Cycle)

When Isis wrapped the reassembled Osiris, the four great protective ​goddesses — Isis, Nephthys, Neith and Serket — took station at the corners of his bier and canopic chest. Serket’s scorpion stands for the poisoned threats of the night ‌that must be kept from the god’s body; her arms spread in the ‘Isis-knot’ gesture frame every royal sarcophagus hall.

The Scorpion and the Child Horus (Metternich Stela)

The ​great cippi of Horus the child — the Metternich Stela above all — tell how the infant god was stung by a scorpion in the marshes and revived ‌by the divine mother's magic. Serket stands within that cycle as the power over the venom itself: the sting belongs to her, and so, therefore, does its antidote.

Across Cultures

Serket was early fused ​with the greater scorpion-powers of the pantheon and by the New Kingdom is barely separable from Isis in her protective aspect; some temples give her a healing consort-form of ​Horus. The Greeks called her Selkis and kept her as a goddess of the dead; her name entered the magical papyri of late antiquity as a power-word against ‌poison.
