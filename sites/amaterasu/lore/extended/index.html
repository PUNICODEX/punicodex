Etymology & Word Family

From original script to Unicode restoration

Original Script 天照 Amaterasu — "Sun goddess in Shinto"
Unicode Restoration Amaterasu Canonical scholarly spelling — ASCII-identical
Modern ASCII amaterasu Plain-ASCII fallback

The name Amaterasu reaches the modern world through scholarly transliteration of the japanese tradition. Because the conventional spelling uses only Latin letters, its Unicode and ASCII forms coincide — the tradition it carries is in the name itself, not in diacritics.

Cultural Significance

From ancient cult to modern memory

Amaterasu in Later Traditions

In the medieval honji-suijaku system Amaterasu was identified with the cosmic Buddha Vairocana (Dainichi Nyorai) — the sun of the Dharma itself — and Buddhist chapels and sutras stood within Ise's precincts until the Meiji separation edicts. State Shinto then stripped away these Buddhist layers, taught the descent of Ninigi as national history, and made her the theological center of imperial ideology; overseas shrines carried her cult into the empire. Her modern reception extends to the video game Ōkami, whose wolf-goddess is drawn from her iconography — evidence that the sun still needs a face.

Modern Legacy

Ise Grand Shrine remains the supreme sanctuary of Shinto, its twenty-year rebuilding unbroken since the seventh century, and the Daijōsai enthronement rite of each new emperor reenacts Ninigi's descent. The red disc of the flag keeps her sign on every public building, and pilgrims stream across the Uji Bridge at New Year. In the countryside, folk religion still addresses her as the guarantor of the harvest sun. Abroad, Ōkami introduced her to millions who will never visit Ise — the newest link in an unbroken chain of sunlight made visible.

In the Earth

The Ise Grand Shrine (Mie Prefecture) is Amaterasu's principal dwelling: its Inner Shrine (Kōtai Jingū), traditionally founded in the reign of Suinin (1st century BCE), is rebuilt every twenty years (shikinen-zōtai) on alternating adjacent plots, a rite documented from 690 CE and continued without break. Architectural and documentary study of the precincts — the Uji Bridge, the Shōden, the treasure halls — makes Ise the best-documented ritual complex in Japan, though the mirror itself is never displayed. Heian ritual compendia (Engishiki) and shrine records (Shintōshū) list the annual offerings of cloth and first fruits presented by the court.

The Meditation

What Amaterasu means — beyond the dictionary

Amaterasu teaches that light is not automatic. Her withdrawal shows that even the sun can be wounded into silence, and her return shows what it takes to heal her: not argument, not force, but the laughter of Uzume and the honesty of a mirror. The community of gods succeeds precisely because each plays a part — the dancer, the strong-arm, the ritualist, the mirror-bearer.

To read the myth politically is correct but incomplete: it is also a psychology of withdrawal and return. Everyone who has sealed themselves behind a rock door knows that the way back is not a plea but a celebration worth peeking at. And the mirror matters most of all: she returns not because others praise her, but because she is allowed to see her own radiance reflected.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about Amaterasu, Sun Goddess, Shintō, and Unicode restoration

01How do you pronounce Amaterasu?

In reconstructed pronunciation, Amaterasu is /a.ma.te.ɾa.sɯ/ — approximately 'ah-mah-TEH-rah-soo' — the 'r' is a light tap, and the final 'u' is barely voiced.

02What does Amaterasu mean?

Amaterasu means Sun goddess in Shinto in the japanese tradition.

03What are the symbols of Amaterasu?

Amaterasu is associated with Mirror (yata no kagami) (One of the Three Imperial Regalia; the mirror hung outside the cave showed Amaterasu her own light, and its dwelling at Ise is her own), Cave of Heaven (Ama-no-Iwato) (The rock-door of her withdrawal; in ritual memory the sealed cave marks every crisis in which order must be coaxed back), Sun-disc (The radiant disk she embodies, echoed today in the red circle of the Japanese flag), Magatama beads (The curved jewels Izanagi gave her; her own necklace generated the gods of the ukei rite with Susanoo), Shimenawa rope (The sacred rope Ame-no-Futodama stretched across the cave mouth — the first boundary between retreat and the common world).

04Why restore Amaterasu in Unicode?

Amaterasu is written entirely in ASCII letters, so its restoration is about canonical fidelity rather than recovered diacritics: the temple fixes the name in its scholarly standard spelling, attested in the source tradition, instead of leaving it to drift.

05What is the most important myth about Amaterasu?

“From the Left Eye of Izanagi” — Amaterasu's mythology, told in the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, is a theology of light, legitimacy, and the restoration of order after violence. It moves from her birth in purification to her withdrawal from a wounded heaven and her dispatch of the imperial line to earth. The full telling lives on the lore page.
