The Authentic Name

Unicode restoration and ASCII comparison

Original Script

天照

The name in its original Japanese form. Amaterasu (天照) is attested in the source tradition — “Sun goddess in Shinto”. Its original diacritics and script distinctions carry the full phonetic and orthographic weight of the source tradition.

ASCII Constraint

amaterasu

The plain amaterasu form is identical to the Unicode restoration. Because this name is already written in Latin letters, no diacritics, stress, or script information were lost — only capitalization differs.

Unicode Restoration

Amaterasu

The Unicode restoration does not need to recover lost marks for Amaterasu. Its value is canonical spelling and consistent cataloguing, not the reconstruction of erased orthography. The domain is readable as-is to both DNS and humanity.

Punycode Encoding

The non-ASCII characters in Amaterasu are encoded while the ASCII remains visible. To the DNS, it is Punycode. To humanity, it is Amaterasu.

Pronunciation

How Amaterasu was spoken

/a.ma.te.ɾa.sɯ/ Modern Standard Japanese (Hepburn)
Ama- Open front [a], labiodental [m], open central [a]; from 天 'heaven/sky'
-tera- Alveolar tap [ɾ] between close-mid [e] and open central [a]; from 照 'to shine'
-su Voiceless alveolar fricative [s] plus close back unrounded [ɯ]; the final vowel is soft

Modern Approximation

'ah-mah-TEH-rah-soo' — the 'r' is a light tap, and the final 'u' is barely voiced.

Etymological Kin

  • Japanese 天照 (Amaterasu), short for Amaterasu-ōmikami, 'Heaven-Illuminating Great August Deity'
  • Siblings Tsukuyomi (moon) and Susanoo (storms), born with her in Izanagi's misogi

The Accent / Script Rule

Amaterasu is Tier 2 here: the ASCII form is identical to Hepburn romanization. Her authority is mythological and political, not phonetically distinctive.

05

Sun Goddess of the High Plain

Light, Imperial Ancestry, and Cosmic Order

Amaterasu is the central deity of the Japanese pantheon, the sun itself made divine and the ancestral kami of the imperial house. Her realm is Takamagahara, the High Plain of Heaven. Without her light, rice cannot grow, order collapses, and the world sinks into darkness.

Sun Sovereign

Ruler of Takamagahara and source of light, warmth, and agricultural fertility.

Imperial Ancestor

Great-grandmother of Ninigi, who descended to earth with the regalia of rule.

Weaver and Cultivator

In the Kojiki she weaves divine garments in the heavenly weaving hall.

Withdrawal and Return

Her retreat into the Ama-no-Iwato cave is the primal Japanese myth of restored order.

Sacred Symbols

Mirror (yata no kagami) One of the Three Imperial Regalia; the mirror that lured her from the cave
Cave of Heaven (Ama-no-Iwato) The rock-door of withdrawal and return, marking cosmic crisis and renewal
Sun-disc The radiant disk she embodies, often rendered as a red sun
Magatama beads Curved sacred jewels associated with her and imperial legitimacy
06

Mythology

Stories of Amaterasu

Amaterasu's mythology is told chiefly in the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki. It is a theology of light, legitimacy, and the restoration of order after violent disruption.

Kojiki / Nihon Shoki

Birth from Izanagi's Purification

After escaping the underworld where his wife Izanami had died, Izanagi performed ritual purification (misogi) in a river. As he washed his left eye, Amaterasu was born; from his right eye came Tsukuyomi, the moon kami; and from his nose came Susanoo, the storm kami. Amaterasu was given rule over the high plain of heaven, making her the senior sibling and source of celestial order.

Kojiki

The Cave of Heaven

When Susanoo rampaged through heaven, destroying her rice-fields and terrifying her weaving-maiden, Amaterasu withdrew into the Ama-no-Iwato cave and sealed it with a great rock. The world fell into darkness. The eight hundred myriads of kami gathered and performed a raucous dance (kagura). The goddess Ame-no-Uzume danced on an overturned tub, exposing herself and making the assembly laugh. Curious, Amaterasu peeked out; a mirror was held before her, and the strong-handed Tajikarao pulled her into the light.

Nihon Shoki

The Descent of Ninigi

Amaterasu sent her grandson Ninigi-no-mikoto to rule the terrestrial world, entrusting him with the Three Sacred Regalia: the curved jewel, the mirror, and the sword. Ninigi's great-grandson Jimmu became the first emperor of Japan, establishing the imperial house as descended from the sun.

Extended Lore

Amaterasu teaches that light is not automatic. It can be wounded, withdrawn, and must be coaxed back by community, laughter, and courage. Her retreat is not weakness but a refusal to tolerate disorder; her return is the triumph of relational persuasion over force.
