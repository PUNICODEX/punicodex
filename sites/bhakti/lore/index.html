01

Mythology

Stories of Bhakti

Bhakti has no biography — it is the biography of millions. Its ‘myths’ are the movement’s own history: the Gītā’s canonization of devotion, the Tamil saints’ songs, the Maharashtrian kīrtan, and the global chant of the Hare Krishna movement. The tradition’s foundational stories cluster around the saints themselves — the hagiographies of Mirabai, Tukaram, and the gopīs of Braj.

Bhagavadgītā 9

The Easiest Path

Krishna’s ninth discourse tells the anxious that devotion needs no learning, no wealth, no austerity: a leaf, a flower, a fruit, water — given with love, accepted. The verse is the charter of every subsequent bhakti movement, and the most quoted single sentence of the Gītā in the modern world.

Āḻvār Cycle

The Poet-Saints of Tamil Country

Between the sixth and ninth centuries, twelve poet-saints — Nammāḻvār foremost — sang Viṣṇu into Tamil verse, walking the temple towns and composing the Nālāyira Divya Prabandham, four thousand hymns still sung daily. The bhakti they invented was personal, vernacular, and ecstatic — religion in the mother-tongue, against the Sanskrit monopoly.

Gopī Cycle

The Love of the Gopīs

The Bhāgavata Purāṇa’s tenth book tells of the cowherd women who loved Krishna past shame, past reason — their love the tradition’s highest image of devotion: uncalculating, unrequited-looking, and finally rewarded. The rasa-theologians read it as the soul’s love for god; the devotees simply wept.

Modern Kīrtana

The Chant Goes Global

From Caitanya’s sixteenth-century Bengali kīrtans to the Hare Krishna movement’s twentieth-century global sankīrtana, the name-chant carried bhakti around the world. The mahāmantra — sixteen words, one call — became the most widely sung Sanskrit formula on earth.

02

Devotion, Loving Attachment to the Divine

The Path of Loving Participation

Bhakti is devotion as a complete path: the loving attachment to a personal god that the Bhagavadgītā crowns as the easiest and swiftest way, and that the medieval saints of Tamil country made into India’s most popular religion. From temple hymns sung in the vernacular to the quiet love of the quietist traditions, bhakti is the subcontinent’s answer to the question: how shall an ordinary person love the infinite?

04

The Name

From the original script to the living URL

Original Script

भक्ति

The name in its original Sanskrit form. Bhakti (भक्ति) is attested in the source tradition — “Devotion, loving attachment, faith — worship and piety directed toward the divine, esp. as a religious principle and means of salvation.”. Its original diacritics and script distinctions carry the full phonetic and orthographic weight of the source tradition.

ASCII Constraint

bhakti

The plain bhakti form is identical to the Unicode restoration. Because this name is already written in Latin letters, no diacritics, stress, or script information were lost — only capitalization differs.

Unicode Restoration

Bhakti

The Unicode restoration does not need to recover lost marks for Bhakti. Its value is canonical spelling and consistent cataloguing, not the reconstruction of erased orthography. The domain is readable as-is to both DNS and humanity.

ASCII-Clean Name
Bhakti.com

This restoration is written entirely in ASCII letters — no Punycode encoding stands between Bhakti and the DNS. The domain reads identically to machines and to human eyes.

Pronunciation

How Bhakti was spoken

/ˈbʌkti/ Sanskrit (IAST: Bhakti)
Bh- The bh is a voiced aspirate — “b” with breath; not a plain English b.
-ak- Short a as in “cup”; the root is bhaj, “to share, partake, adore”.
-ti The abstract ending: “devotion”, “participation” — the quality made a noun.

Modern Approximation

“BUK-tee”

The full word family — cognates, derivatives, and variant spellings — is traced in Extended Lore → Etymology.

The Accent / Script Rule

The word begins as “partaking” — devotion as sharing in the divine, not mere adoration.
