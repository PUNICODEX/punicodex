Etymology & Word Family

From original script to Unicode restoration

Original Script भक्ति Bhakti — "Devotion, loving attachment, faith — worship and piety directed toward the divine, esp. as a religious principle and means of salvation."
Unicode Restoration Bhakti Canonical scholarly spelling — ASCII-identical
Modern ASCII bhakti Plain-ASCII fallback

The name Bhakti reaches the modern world through scholarly transliteration of the sanskrit tradition. Because the conventional spelling uses only Latin letters, its Unicode and ASCII forms coincide — the tradition it carries is in the name itself, not in diacritics.

Cult & Worship

How the sacred was kept — rites, sanctuaries, remains

The Cult

Its liturgy is song: the nāma-saṅkīrtana of the name, sung in streets and kitchens. The ārādhana worship of the big temples is bhakti’s formal rite — the god fed, bathed, and sung to sleep like a beloved guest.

In the Earth

Bhakti’s monuments are the great temple-towns it animated — Śrīraṅgam, Kanchipuram, Pandharpur — and the palm-leaf hymnals of the Prabandham. The modern ISKCON temples of a hundred cities are its newest layer.

Iconography

How Bhakti was imaged across the ages

The Image

The flute and the cowherd (Krishna-bhakti), the liṅga and the river (Śiva-bhakti), the goddess of the pandals (Devī-bhakti) — bhakti has no single icon because it has no single beloved. Its emblem is the joined palms: the gesture itself.

Reception & Transformation

The name after its own age — transmission, translation, survival

Afterlives

Religious studies uses bhakti as the type-case of devotional religion: emotional, vernacular, anti-elitist, and unstoppably popular. The movement’s historians (A. K. Ramanujan foremost) made the bhakti poets canonical world literature; and the Hare Krishna chant made the word familiar in every airport on earth.

Identifications Across Traditions

Bhakti absorbed the whole pantheon: Viṣṇu, Śiva, Devī, and the unnameable all received the same love. It crossed into Sufism — the bhakti-sant poets read like Rūmī’s cousins — and into Christianity via the Syrian churches of the Malabar coast. The modern global movements (ISKCON, Sathya Sai) are bhakti’s export form.

Modern Legacy

The bhakti movement rewrote Indian religion in the vernaculars — its hymns are the founding literature of half a dozen languages. ‘Bhakti’ entered English as a loan-word in the nineteenth century; and the Hare Krishna chant remains, measured by human-hours, the most performed Sanskrit text in history.

The Meditation

What Bhakti means — beyond the dictionary

Bhakti: the shortest word for the largest love. Restored plainly, it needs no mark — the aspiration of the bh is the only ornament devotion ever wanted, and English “bh” is close enough for love. The lexicon keeps it as the path’s own name for itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about Bhakti, Devotion, Love, and Unicode restoration

01How do you pronounce Bhakti?

In reconstructed pronunciation, Bhakti is /ˈbʌkti/ — approximately “BUK-tee”.

02What does Bhakti mean?

Bhakti means Devotion, loving attachment, faith — worship and piety directed toward the divine, esp. as a religious principle and means of salvation. in the sanskrit tradition.

03What are the symbols of Bhakti?

Bhakti is associated with The Path of Love (The Bhagavadgītā’s ninth teaching: “offer me even a leaf, a flower, a fruit, water — I accept it”.), The Āḻvārs (The twelve Tamil poet-saints whose hymns to Viṣṇu founded the tradition — composed, not compiled.), Nāma-Saṅkīrtana (The chanting of the name — Hare Krishna, Rāma Rāma — as devotion’s simplest engine.), Grace over Works (Prapatti, śaraṇāgati — the “surrender” that asks nothing and receives everything.).

04Why restore Bhakti in Unicode?

Bhakti is written entirely in ASCII letters, so its restoration is about canonical fidelity rather than recovered diacritics: the temple fixes the name in its scholarly standard spelling, attested in the source tradition, instead of leaving it to drift.

05What is the most important myth about Bhakti?
