The Authentic Name

Unicode restoration and ASCII comparison

Original Script

迦具土

The name in its original Japanese form. Kagutsuchi (迦具土) is attested in the source tradition — “The burning force”. Its original diacritics and script distinctions carry the full phonetic and orthographic weight of the source tradition.

ASCII Constraint

kagutsuchi

The plain kagutsuchi form is identical to the Unicode restoration. Because this name is already written in Latin letters, no diacritics, stress, or script information were lost — only capitalization differs.

Unicode Restoration

Kagutsuchi

The Unicode restoration does not need to recover lost marks for Kagutsuchi. Its value is canonical spelling and consistent cataloguing, not the reconstruction of erased orthography. The domain is readable as-is to both DNS and humanity.

Punycode Encoding
Kagutsuchi.com → kagutsuchi.com

The non-ASCII characters in Kagutsuchi are encoded while the ASCII remains visible. To the DNS, it is Punycode. To humanity, it is Kagutsuchi.

05

God of Fire and Volcanic Force

Creation, Destruction, and the Origin of Death

Kagutsuchi is the fire god whose birth burned his mother Izanami to death, introducing mortality into the mythic world. He is creation and destruction in one figure: the flame that forges and the flame that consumes. After his death at the hands of Izanagi, his scattered body became the volcanoes and hot springs of Japan.

Birth of Fire

His birth from Izanami introduced both fire and death into the world.

Slain by Izanagi

Izanagi avenged Izanami's death by cutting Kagutsuchi into pieces.

Volcano God

His body became the volcanoes and geothermal features of the land.

Forge and Hearth

Fire for cooking, warmth, and metalwork descends from his power.

Sacred Symbols

Flame The elemental fire he embodies
Sword The weapon with which Izanagi slew him
Volcano His dismembered body became Japan's volcanic peaks
Forge The fire of the blacksmith and the hearth
06

Mythology

Stories of Kagutsuchi

Kagutsuchi's mythology is brief but pivotal: his birth kills the creator goddess and sets in motion the separation of life and death.

Kojiki

The Burning Birth

When Izanami gave birth to Kagutsuchi, the fire god, his flames were so intense that they burned her genitals and killed her. From her dying body came further kami of mountains, rivers, and earth. Kagutsuchi thus became the first cause of death in the mythic world.

Kojiki

Izanagi's Vengeance

Grief-stricken and enraged, Izanagi drew his sword and cut Kagutsuchi into pieces. From the blood on his sword and from Kagutsuchi's body came numerous kami associated with swords, mountains, and water. The fire god's destruction paradoxically produced more life.

Folk belief

Fire and the Hearth

Despite his destructive origin, Kagutsuchi became a deity honored in hearth rituals and by blacksmiths. Fire could kill, but it also cooked food, warmed homes, and transformed metal. This double nature made him an object of both fear and reverence.

Extended Lore

Kagutsuchi is the fire we cannot live without and cannot fully control. His birth destroyed the world of innocence, but his death scattered the seeds of mountains and rivers. He reminds us that the energies that sustain us can also consume us.
