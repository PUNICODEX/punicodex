The provenance file on Kagutsuchi

Provenance record 216 · Kagutsuchi · one temple, fully accounted for.

Provenance

1. The attestation. 迦具土 — "The burning force". On record: Kojiki (birth and death of Kagutsuchi), Nihon Shoki (parallel account), Philippi, Kojiki.

2. The narrative record. The Burning Birth — When Izanami gave birth to Kagutsuchi, the fire god, his flames were so intense that they burned her genitals and killed her. From her dying body came further kami of mountains, rivers, and earth. Izanagi's Vengeance — Grief-stricken and enraged, Izanagi drew his sword and cut Kagutsuchi into pieces. From the blood on his sword and from Kagutsuchi's body came numerous kami associated with swords, mountains, and water.

3. The physical record. Kagutsuchi has no major independent shrine. His importance is expressed in hearth rituals, blacksmith traditions, and the volcanic landscape of Japan itself, which preserves the memory of fire as both creative and destructive.

4. The current holding. The temple at /kagutsuchi/, classified Tier 2. The restoration keeps one feature the ASCII form drops. One is enough to matter, and the label says so plainly: Tier-2.

5. The documented resonances. Disaster Resilience & Recovery, Thermal, Geothermal & Fire Energy, Environmental & Climate Services — seats argued and weighted in the pattern atlas, read through the myths in this temple's Resonance File.

6. The primary sources. Kojiki, Nihon Shoki — the library this file answers to, open at the Sacred Texts collection.

7. The continuing record. Kagutsuchi appears in popular media as a volcanic or destructive deity, and his name is invoked in discussions of Japanese volcanism and geothermal energy. The ritual care of fire — in the hearth, the forge, and the volcano — preserves his ancient significance.

The Emblems

The name does not travel alone. Flame — The elemental fire he embodies; Sword — The weapon with which Izanagi slew him; Volcano — His dismembered body became Japan's volcanic peaks. Each emblem is filed in the temple's record with its own provenance, the same discipline the spelling gets.

For the Voice

'KAH-goo-tsoo-chee' — the 'tsu' is crisp, and the final 'chi' is like 'chee'. The reconstructed line runs `/ka.ɡɯ.tsɯ.tɕi/` — the temple's pronunciation atlas carries the phonemes one by one.
