Mídas: the stories first, then the spelling

You cannot weigh an accent without knowing what it carries. Here is what Mídas has been carrying.

The Stories That Carry the Name

Silenos Returned. Silenos, the drunken companion of Dionysos, strayed and was brought to Mídas — in some tellings caught in the king's own rose gardens. Mídas entertained him ten days and returned him to the god, who offered any gift in thanks. The king, thinking of his treasury, wished that everything he touched might become gold.

All He Touches Turns to Gold. The gift revealed itself at table: the bread stiffened in his hands, the wine ran molten and would not wet his lips. Hungry and thirsty in a house of gold, Mídas begged release. Dionysos sent him to wash in the spring of the Pactolus at Sardis; the power passed from his body into the river, whose sands bore gold ever after.

The Ears of an Ass. Old Tmolos judged the music-contest of Pan and Apollo for the lyre; Mídas alone declared for the pipes. Apollo would not suffer such ears to keep their human shape: they lengthened, furred, and moved. The king hid them under a Phrygian cap, but his barber knew, and whispered the secret into a hole in the ground — and the reeds that grew there have murmured 'King Mídas has ass's ears' on every wind since.

And Here Is What the Spelling Preserves

Against those stories, the attestation: Μίδας, on the evidence Herodotus and Ovid set down. The name means "Phrygian royal name".

The restoration keeps one feature the ASCII form midas cannot show:

  • i → í — Acute on i: where the voice peaks.

A storyteller pronouncing the name would restore every one of them by instinct. The file only writes down what the voice already knows.

The Name Travels

Mídas absorbed no foreign gods; instead, the myth absorbed the man. Greek storytellers took a historical Phrygian dynasty and hung upon it their two great parables of appetite and judgment.

The Address It Earned

The temple of Mídas stands at /sites/midas/ — lore, scholars, patterns — because a name carrying this much story deserves more than a parking page. Tier-2 here means one preserved truth — not less important, just singular. The temple shows it and names it.
