Inside the Aṃśa restoration: what the marks recover

The domain you're reading about exists because someone refused to let Aṃśa stay amsa.

Aṃśa has no single great narrative — like many Vedic gods, he is a function given face: the apportionment that the sacrifice performs and the year repeats. The Ṛgveda knows the Ādityas as the sons of Aditi — "the unbound" — guardians of ṛta, cosmic order; the later lists count twelve, one for each month, and Aṃśa stands among them as the sun of apportionment. The Bhāgavata Purāṇa's roster names him plainly: the year itself is his household, and each month is a room of it.

That is the figure this file is about — and the temple now standing at its restored name.

The Name Before the Marks

Strip Aṃśa to amsa and one piece of evidence vanishes:

  • s → ś — Stress mark (acute/tone): the stress position the ASCII form cannot show.

None of these marks is decoration. Each one is a fact about how the name was spoken, and each one is checkable against the scholarly record the temple cites.
