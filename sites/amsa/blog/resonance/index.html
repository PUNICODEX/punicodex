The Test

Seat 1: Finance, Wealth & Commerce

Claim: The allotted share; equity and portion personified. Exhibit: Son of Aditi — The Ṛgveda knows the Ādityas as the sons of Aditi — "the unbound" — guardians of ṛta, cosmic order; the later lists count twelve, one for each month, and Aṃśa stands among them as the sun of apportionment.

Verdict: survives scrutiny. The myth and the trade do the same work.

Seat 2: Timekeeping & Calendar Systems

Claim: The year’s twelve portions; the month as allotted share. Exhibit: Every God a Share — The Vedic sacrifice is an economy of portions: the hymn distributes the offering — "this share to Agni, this to Soma" — and the Brāhmaṇas explain that the gods' shares were fixed at creation.

Verdict: holds under load. The correspondence is functional, not decorative.

Seat 3: Legal, Justice & Compliance

Claim: The just portion; inheritance and allotment law’s oldest icon. Exhibit: The Gods' Shares — The Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa tells how the gods and anti-gods contended for the sacrifice, and how the portions were assigned — some by merit, some by trickery.

Verdict: survives scrutiny. The myth and the trade do the same work.

The Control Group

Against the seats that survived, one control: the temple's own summary of its figure — Aṃśa has no single great narrative — like many Vedic gods, he is a function given face: the apportionment that the sacrifice performs and the year repeats. The seats do not drift far from it. That is the audit's cleanest result.
