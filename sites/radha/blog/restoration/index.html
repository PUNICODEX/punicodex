The provenance file on Rādhā

Provenance record 223 · Rādhā · one temple, fully accounted for.

Provenance

1. The attestation. राधा, from the tradition's own record. On record: Gītagovinda, Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Brahma-vaivarta Purāṇa, Caitanya-caritāmṛta.

2. The preserved evidence. a → ā (Long vowel); a → ā (Long vowel) — absent from the ASCII form radha, present in the restoration, verifiable against the sources above.

3. The narrative record. The Unnamed Beloved — The Bhāgavata Purāṇa tells the rāsa-līlā across five chapters of its tenth book — the autumn full-moon night on the Yamunā's bank, Krishna's flute emptying the village, the dance in which he multiplies himself among the gopīs — and never once speaks her name. Yet one verse (10. The Poem That Crowned Her — In the twelfth century Jayadeva wrote the Gītagovinda, and the unsigned portrait got its signature. His aṣṭapadīs — songs in eights — stage the whole drama of separation and union: the go-between urging Rādhā through the spring night to the tryst, Krishna begging her foot on his head, the refrain rādhā-mādhavayor jayati yamunā-kūle raḥaḥ-kelayaḥ — "the secret love-play of Rādhā and Mādhava triumphs on the Yamunā's bank.

4. The physical record. Her monuments are the sacred geography of Braj: Varṣāṇā (Barsana), her village, crowned by the Lārlījī temple on its ridge; Govardhan hill, around which pilgrims still circle barefoot; and Rādhā-kuṇḍa, her bathing-pool, restored and excavated in the sixteenth century under Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmin and venerated by the Gauḍīyas as the holiest water in existence. The Rādhāvallabha temple in Vrindavan anchors her own sampradāya, and the illustrated Gītagovinda manuscripts — palm-leaf and paper, from Odisha to the Punjab Hills — are among the glories of Indian painting.

5. The current holding. The temple at /sites/radha/, classified Tier 1. The restoration preserves more than the ASCII form can express — stress, length, or a letter the Latin keyboard lost — which places it, mechanically, in Tier-1.

6. The documented resonances. Faith & Spiritual Organizations, Music, Arts & Performance, Wedding, Family & Maternity — seats argued and weighted in the pattern atlas, read through the myths in this temple's Resonance File.

7. The primary sources. Rig Veda, Ramayana — the library this file answers to, open at the Sacred Texts collection.

8. The continuing record. Rādhā is one of the most-spoken divine names on earth: "Rādhe Rādhe" is the everyday greeting of Braj, her Rādhāṣṭamī fills Varṣāṇā's lanes, and the spring Holi of Barsana — the Lāthmār Holi where the women playfully beat the men of Krishna's village — is broadcast worldwide. Kangra and Pahari painters made her longing the face of Indian miniature art; Sūrdās and the bhakti poets sang her in every northern tongue.
