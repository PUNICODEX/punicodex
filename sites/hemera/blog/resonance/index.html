Before the Office, the Story

Born from Darkness. Hesiod's Theogony (123–125) names Érebos and Nyx as the parents of Aithḗr and Hēméra. The genealogy is elegant: from the first gap (Cháos) comes darkness, and from darkness comes both the bright upper air and the day. Hēméra is therefore two generations removed from the origin of things, a luminous daughter of the underworld.

The House Beyond the Bronze Threshold. In a famous passage of the Theogony (744–757), Hesiod describes the house of Nyx and Hēméra beyond the reach of gods and mortals. The two never occupy it together: when one goes out through the bronze threshold, the other enters. Hēméra goes forth to greet the earth, while Nyx covers all things with her veil.

Not the First Rebrand

The Romans personified the day as Dies, though she never achieved a developed mythic personality like the Greek Hēméra. Hēméra was often conflated in later thought with Eos/Aurora, the dawn, and with Hēlios/Sol, the sun, because all three bring light. The office is only the latest translation.
