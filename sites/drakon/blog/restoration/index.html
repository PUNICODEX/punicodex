How Drákōn was rebuilt, mark by mark

Bench notes, Drákōn: what we found, what we rejected, and what we finally set down. Kept in the open, because a restoration you cannot audit is just a spelling with confidence.

What We Found

The attestation, as LSJ and Homeric Hymns preserve it: Δράκων. Meaning: "'The one who sees clearly' (from δέρκομαι, to see)".

The figure behind the name: Every Greek dragon is a threshold made flesh: kill one and a sanctuary, a city, or a marriage becomes possible — which is why the founders of cities are all, sooner or later, dragonslayers.

What the Name Carried In

Apóllōn and Python. The Homeric Hymn to Apóllōn tells how the god, seeking a site for his oracle, found the spring at Delphoi guarded by a great she-dragon who had nursed Typhaon and ravaged the flocks. He shot her and left her to rot in the sun — "now rot (pytheu) here upon the fruitful soil" — and the place was called Pythō, the priestess Pythia, ever after.

Kadmos at the Spring. Sent to fetch water from the Ismenian spring, Kadmos' companions were killed by its guardian dragon, offspring of Árēs. Kadmos crushed it with a rock, and on Athēnā's counsel sowed half its teeth: armed men, the Spartoi, rose from the furrows and killed each other until five remained — the ancestors of Thebes' noble houses.

A spelling that has to carry stories like these is not a label. It is equipment.

Where It Stood Before Us

Greek dragons are text-and-image creatures: the finest evidence is vase painting, where Python, Ladon, and the Kolchian warden appear as bearded, crested serpents from the 6th century BCE onward — a Ladon on an Apulian vase in Naples shows the many-coiled form most clearly. The dragon's civic role is epigraphic: Thebes' founding myth was official city ideology, and Athens kept a sacred snake on the Acropolis (the "guardian of the citadel" of Herodotus 8.41) as a living drakōn of the polis. The bench inherits all of it — the web temple is the newest site, not the first.

What We Kept

The evidence, mark by mark — 2 features the keyboard form drakon cannot hold:

  • a → á — Stress mark (acute/tone). Kept.
  • o → ō — Macron marks the long vowel. Kept.

What We Rejected

Any form invented for availability. The rule on the bench: if the evidence does not carry it, the domain does not get it — whatever the registrar has in stock.

What We Set Down

Drákōn — Tier-1 is not a compliment; it is a measurement. This restoration keeps something the ASCII form provably loses, and the measurement is repeatable.

For the voice: dRAH-kone, stress on the first syllable with a long final ō.

The temple stands at /sites/drakon/ — the notes above are public in its Scholarly Edition, sources and all.
