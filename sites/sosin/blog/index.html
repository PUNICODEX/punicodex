Across Cultures

Sosin is primarily a shamanic figure and does not have a large presence in Confucian, Buddhist, or Daoist canonical literature. It belongs to the specialised pantheon addressed by mudang and reflects the Korean view that the spirit world is structured like a bureaucracy, with messengers, inspectors, and intermediaries. Scholars of Korean shamanism compare such figures to the spirit messengers found in other Central and East Asian religions, while practitioners treat them as necessary participants in ritual efficacy.

Sosin is not a household name in the way that Yongwang or Samshin are, but it remains important within shamanic practice and the academic study of Korean religion.
