Evidence dossier: Keraunós

Evidence dossier 259 — Keraunós. Every exhibit catalogued, every ruling stated with its grounds.

At a Glance

  • Canonical form: Keraunós
  • Original script: κεραυνός
  • Pantheon: Greek
  • Convention: the restored-stress convention: acute and circumflex for pitch, macrons for vowel quantity, exactly as the sources write
  • False forms on record: 2
  • Contested forms: none attested
  • Series: The Canonical Register, No. 259 of 546

The Evidence

Exhibit 1 — the attestation. The name is attested as κεραυνός, as LSJ and Pape-Benseler record. On the evidence, it means "Thunderbolt (the weapon of Zeus)". The canonical transliteration is Keraunós.

Exhibit 2 — the preserved marks. o → ó (Acute on omicron). Each is verifiable against the sources named in the attestation.

Exhibit 3 — the flattened form. The plain form keraunos is not false — it is a fallback, and the register is exact about what that means. In systems that cannot carry diacritics (legacy databases, older tooling, parts of the address-bar pipeline) the flattened form is the accepted vehicle, and the rulebook names it explicitly as the final rung of the fallback hierarchy: full restoration first, circumflex where attested, macron-only by the academic standard, an attested variant, and only then plain ASCII.

What the fallback status forbids is promotion: keraunos may carry the name where marks are impossible, but it may not represent the name where marks are possible. Every mark it drops — o → ó — is a fact the sources recorded. A fallback is a vehicle, never the primary.

Exhibit 4 — struck from the record: Ké́raunós. Origin: the English ear — English speakers slam the first syllable of every borrowed name (DEL-fi, MY-cene), so the accent drifts forward online. Grounds: moves the stress from o (where the Greek puts it) to E — a different word with a different rhythm; the sources stress o and nothing else.

Exhibit 5 — struck from the record: Keraunôs. Origin: the circumflex habit — writers who learned that "the little hat means Greek" and apply it wherever an acute belongs. Grounds: the source carries an acute (oxeia, a rising tone), not a circumflex (perispomene, a rise-fall): the two marks are different facts about pitch, and only the acute is attested on o.

Exhibit 6 — the physical record. Keraunós is attested in the textual and iconographic record of the Greek tradition.

Exhibit 7 — the continuing record. Keraunós continues to appear in scholarship, art, and popular reception as a significant figure of the Greek tradition. Restoring the name in Unicode returns the original marks that ASCII removes.

The Convention's Own History

The marks this register defends were not invented for typesetting. Ancient Greek carried a melodic pitch accent, and the acute and circumflex were added by Alexandrian scholars — Aristophanes of Byzantium, tradition holds — precisely to preserve it when the spoken language began to level. Vowel quantity (the macron's concern) is older still: it changes meter, meaning, and grammar, and the poets of the Theogony depend on it. To drop either class of mark is to drop two millennia of deliberate preservation. The restored-stress convention simply continues the oldest documented project in European scholarship: keeping the sound of Greek alive in its letters.

The Protocol's Position

Can the canonical form live in the address bar? Almost always, yes. IDNA 2008 admits every mark this register defends — the DNS stores them as `xn--` punycode and modern browsers render them natively. The real frictions are practical, not legal: some registrars stock only part of the IDN space, so a theoretically valid name can be temporarily unbuyable; and stacked combinations (an acute riding a macron) remain awkward to type on mobile keyboards even where the protocol accepts them.

The project's answer is a ranked ladder, not a compromise: the full restoration first, then attested fallbacks in descending order of completeness. The ladder's lowest rung, plain ASCII, is a vehicle for legacy systems — sanctioned, but never presented as the scholarly form.

The Verdict

Case closed on Keraunós: canonical, cited, and defended. The keyboard form keraunos rides as the sanctioned fallback — and stays in that lane. The register's files remain open: if a source surfaces that changes any line of this entry, the entry changes. Until then, the temple at /keraunos/ holds the canonical form.

The docket for this ruling: 2 false forms ruled against, Ké́raunós first on the docket; 1 preserved mark verified: o → ó; no contested forms attested; the attestation rests on 3 cited sources — LSJ, Pape-Benseler, Beekes.
