01

Mythology

Stories of Yama

Yama’s mythology moves from Vedic pathfinder to epic judge to Purāṇic king of hells. He begins as the twin who chose to die — the first of the race to walk the path — and becomes the stern accountant whose city the dead enter by the southern gate. Buddhism and the epics gave him his tortures and his buffalo; the Veda kept his dignity.

Rigveda 10.10

The Twin Dialogue

The oldest Yama-text is a dialogue: Yama and his twin Yamī, the first two beings, and Yamī’s love for her brother. Yama refuses — ‘the immortals watch’ — and the hymn becomes the charter of exogamy and of the incest-taboo itself, argued between the world’s first twins in the world’s first debate.

Rigveda 10.14

The Path of the Fathers

The funeral hymn sends the dead king along the path Yama blazed, past the two dogs and the waters, to the feast of the fathers where Yama waits with Varuṇa. ‘Leave the sin here, keep the glory’ — the formula of every Vedic funeral, spoken to the first traveller’s successor.

Mahābhārata

The King Under the Earth

In the epic’s underworld-book, Yama rules below with his city Saṃyamanī, his account read by Citragupta, his noose and his buffalo — the god of justice who once disguised himself as a crab to test a king. The epic makes him dreaded and fair: the judge who cannot be bribed because he already owns everything.

Purāṇic Hell

The Lord of the Narakas

The Purāṇas enumerate eighty-four hells under Yama’s administration, each a precise torment for a precise sin — the liar’s tongue, the thief’s hands. Yet the same texts insist his justice is karma’s arithmetic, not malice: even the tortures are purification, and release comes in time.

02

God of Death, the First Mortal, Judge of the Dead

The King of the Ancestral World

Yama is death’s king in the Indian traditions: the first man to die, who found the way to the fathers’ world and now rules it — and judges who may enter. With his noose and his two four-eyed dogs, he waits at the southern direction, the realm of the ancestors, and his account-book is the oldest audit in human literature.

The First Mortal

He died before anyone — the twin who found the path to the fathers and became its king.

The Noose

His pāśa, the noose, finds every soul at its hour; none outrun it, though all may beg.

The Two Dogs

Śyāma and Śabala, four-eyed, follow his road and report the dead; they guard the way.

Judge of the Deeds

In the Mahābhārata and Purāṇas he reads the record of every soul — the karma, weighed without scales.

04

The Name

From the original script to the living URL

Original Script

यम

The name in its original Sanskrit form. Yama (यम) is attested in the source tradition — “The god who presides over the Pitṛs (the departed fathers) and rules the spirits of the dead.”. Its original diacritics and script distinctions carry the full phonetic and orthographic weight of the source tradition.

ASCII Constraint

yama

The plain yama form is identical to the Unicode restoration. Because this name is already written in Latin letters, no diacritics, stress, or script information were lost — only capitalization differs.

Unicode Restoration

Yama

The Unicode restoration does not need to recover lost marks for Yama. Its value is canonical spelling and consistent cataloguing, not the reconstruction of erased orthography. The domain is readable as-is to both DNS and humanity.

ASCII-Clean Name
Yama.com

This restoration is written entirely in ASCII letters — no Punycode encoding stands between Yama and the DNS. The domain reads identically to machines and to human eyes.

Pronunciation

How Yama was spoken

/ˈjʌmə/ Sanskrit (IAST: Yama)
Ya- Palatal y as in “young”; the a is short and open.
-ma The name means “twin” — Yama is the twin of Yamī in the earliest tradition.
Yam- In compound names the stem surfaces as yam- — the river Yamunā preserves his twin sister’s name.

Modern Approximation

“YUM-uh”
