Etymology & Word Family

From original script to canonical restoration

Original Script अर्जुन Arjuna — "The third of the Pāṇḍava princes, a son of Indra and Kuntī; the great archer of the Mahābhārata, to whom Kṛṣṇa speaks the Bhagavad-gītā."
Canonical Restoration Arjuna Canonical scholarly spelling — ASCII-identical
Modern ASCII arjuna Plain-ASCII fallback

The name Arjuna reaches the modern world through scholarly transliteration of the sanskrit tradition. Because the conventional spelling uses only Latin letters, its canonical and ASCII forms coincide — the tradition it carries is in the name itself, not in diacritics.

Cult & Worship

How the sacred was kept — rites, sanctuaries, remains

The Cult

No temple to Arjuna, but his svayaṃvara-archery is the wedding-ceremony’s standard boast, and the Gītā’s recitation is a complete liturgy. The Arjuna-penance reliefs of Mamallapuram’s great bas-relief panel may show his Kirātārjunīya austerities.

In the Earth

Arjuna’s monuments are the epic’s geography itself — Indraprastha’s Purāṇa Qila in Delhi claims his city; and the wayang kulit’s refined Arjuna puppets of Java are among the finest objects of Indonesian art. The Gītā’s palm-leaf manuscripts number in the millions.

Iconography

How Arjuna was imaged across the ages

The Image

The archer with the Gandiva and the two inexhaustible quivers, Krishna as charioteer before him; the diadem and the ape-banner. His icon is the drawn bow at rest — the moment before the Gītā, frozen forever.

06

Reception & Transformation

The name after its own age — transmission, translation, survival

Afterlives

World literature knows him through the Gītā, where he is the eternal student; comparative epic pairs him with Achilles’ rival-enemies and with the archers of every tradition. In modern India he is the sportsperson’s patron, the doubter honoured above the certain — the hero whose question was worth more than his arrows.

Identifications Across Traditions

Arjuna is the Nara of the Nara-Nārāyaṇa pair — the human half of Vishnu’s eternal companion — making his friendship with Krishna a theology in dialogue-form. In Javanese and Balinese tradition he becomes the refined knight of the wayang cycle; and the Gītā made him, doubting, the everyman of all later Indian philosophy.

Modern Legacy

The Gītā’s chariot is the world’s most reproduced scene of instruction; the phrase ‘Kurukshetra’ entered the political vocabulary of every Indian language as the name of any field where duty and doubt collide. His name crowned South Indian kings (Arjunavarman) and the great tank of Rajput legend; and ‘Arjuna award’ is India’s highest honour for a sportsperson — the archer’s prize.

The Meditation

What Arjuna means — beyond the dictionary

Arjuna: the silvery one, the third of five, the hero whose greatest act was a question. Restored plainly, the name holds the Gītā’s whole setting — the bow lowered, the armies waiting, the friend about to become God. Four syllables, one chariot, eighteen chapters.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about Arjuna, Archery, Dharma, Bhagavad Gītā, and canonical restoration

01How do you pronounce Arjuna?

In reconstructed pronunciation, Arjuna is /ˈɑːrdʒʊnə/ — approximately “AHR-joo-nuh”.

02What does Arjuna mean?

Arjuna means The third of the Pāṇḍava princes, a son of Indra and Kuntī; the great archer of the Mahābhārata, to whom Kṛṣṇa speaks the Bhagavad-gītā. in the sanskrit tradition.

03What are the symbols of Arjuna?

Arjuna is associated with The Gandiva (The bow of Brahmā, won from Agni at Khaṇḍava; its twang alone routs armies.), The Svayaṃvara (He alone shot the eye of the rotating fish at Draupadī’s bride-choice, looking only at its reflection.), The Gītā’s Chariot (Between the armies he faltered — and Krishna answered with eighteen chapters that became a scripture.), The Tīrtha Pilgrimage (His year of exile became the Tīrthayātrā — the epic’s encyclopedia of India’s sacred geography.).

04Why use the canonical spelling of Arjuna?

Arjuna is written entirely in ASCII letters, so its restoration is about canonical fidelity rather than recovered diacritics: the temple fixes the name in its scholarly standard spelling, attested in the source tradition, instead of leaving it to drift.

05What is the most important myth about Arjuna?

“The Pāṇḍavas’ Birth” — the Ādi Parvan story that anchors the mythology of Arjuna, told in full on the lore page.
