Etymology & Word Family

From original script to Unicode restoration

Unicode Restoration Vasubandhu Canonical scholarly spelling — ASCII-identical
Modern ASCII vasubandhu Plain-ASCII fallback

The name Vasubandhu reaches the modern world through scholarly transliteration of the buddhist tradition. Because the conventional spelling uses only Latin letters, its Unicode and ASCII forms coincide — the tradition it carries is in the name itself, not in diacritics.

Derivatives & Descendants

Etymological Kin

  • Sanskrit — वसुबन्धु (vasubandhu), 'kinsman of wealth' — vasu 'wealth, good' + bandhu 'kinsman, friend' (Monier-Williams, s.v.)
  • Pāli / Tibetan — Vasubandhu in Pāli sources; Tibetan dbyig gnyen, the standard calque translating vasu as 'wealth' (dbyig) and bandhu as 'friend' (gnyen)
  • Family names — His mother was named Virañcī; his father Kauśika, a brahmin of Puruṣapura (Peshawar) — his elder half-brother was Asaṅga (Tibetan biographies)

Cultural Significance

From ancient cult to modern memory

Vasubandhu in Later Traditions

Vasubandhu's legacy is the creation of a pan-Asian scholastic commons. In China, Xuanzang's pilgrimage was undertaken substantially to resolve disputes over the Kośa, and his massive translation of 649–660 CE made Vasubandhu the doctrinal backbone of the Faxiang (Consciousness-Only) school. In Japan the Hossō school of Nara claimed him as its patriarch, while the Kyoto lineages of the future would read his Viṁśikā as the foundation of 'mind-only' meditations. In Tibet every monastery curriculum begins with the Kośa. Modern comparative philosophy — from Thich Nhat Hanh's engaged Buddhism to analytic treatments of idealism — still frames its questions in his vocabulary. He is perhaps the only ancient philosopher whose works are simultaneously a living liturgy, a graduate textbook, and a modern research programme.

Modern Legacy

The Abhidharmakośa never went out of use: it has been memorized, commented, and debated continuously for some fifteen centuries, from Nālandā to the great Tibetan commentarial traditions (Prajñākaramati's and Yaśomitra's subcommentaries in Sanskrit; Sthiramati's in the mind-only line). Its author is honored as a patriarch in China and Japan, and Tibetan debate courtyards still open with his verses. In the modern academy Vasubandhu anchors the study of Buddhist philosophy — his Viṁśikā is a standard text in courses on philosophy of mind, cited in debates about idealism and representation. A brahmin-born monk of Gandhāra thus became one of the most-read authors in human history, his name a byword for the possibility that analysis itself can be a path to compassion.

In the Earth

Vasubandhu left no shrine — his witnesses are manuscripts. Fragments of the Abhidharmakośa survive in Central Asian Sanskrit finds, and the complete tradition rides on Xuanzang's Chinese translation (Taishō 1558) and the later Tibetan canon (Toh. 4089–90). Gandhāra, his homeland, is one of the richest archaeological regions of the Buddhist world: the monastery and stupa complexes around Peshawar — where the biographies place his birth — have yielded the oldest datable Buddha images (c. 1st–3rd c. CE). At Nālandā, the university whose ruins still stand in Bihar, his Kośa was a founding text; the debate-court culture his legend describes is the same institution archaeology reveals. The colophons of the Dunhuang and Gilgit manuscript finds carry his name as authority across the Silk Road.

05

The Meditation

What Vasubandhu means — beyond the dictionary

Vasubandhu is the scholar as religious figure — proof that in the Buddhist world, thinking carefully was itself a sacred practice. His two careers look like a contradiction: the great classifier of the mind's parts, then the teacher who said the parts and the world they compose are 'mind-only'. Read together, they form one argument: you must take the world apart completely before you can see what holds it together. His legend conversion turns not on a vision but on a letter from a dying brother — intellect bending at last to love. Modern philosophy of mind still returns to his Viṁśikā, where a dream, a reflection, and the waking world are made to trade places in a few pages. Few ancient authors remain so contemporary: the question of what the world is made of, and whether mind is on the list, is still his question.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about Vasubandhu, Abhidharma, Yogacara, and Unicode restoration

01How do you pronounce Vasubandhu?

In reconstructed pronunciation, Vasubandhu is /vasubandhu/ — approximately Say "vasubandhu" with the stress and length implied by the scholarly restoration "Vasubandhu".

02What does Vasubandhu mean?

Vasubandhu means The celebrated Buddhist scholar — author of the Abhidharmakośa, co-founder of the Yogācāra school in the buddhist tradition.

03What are the symbols of Vasubandhu?

Vasubandhu is associated with The Kośa stanza (A verse (kārikā) whose eight-syllable lines hold whole Abhidharma treatises — scholarship compressed into memorable sound), The begging bowl (The monk's only equipment; Vasubandhu remained a bhikṣu all his life even while moving courts and schools), The letter from Asaṅga (In the Tibetan biographies, the note that converted him — affection and mortality doing what doctrine could not), Treasury (kośa) (The storehouse metaphor of the Abhidharmakośa: every dharma catalogued like treasure in a vault, each with its own nature).

04Why restore Vasubandhu in Unicode?

Vasubandhu is written entirely in ASCII letters, so its restoration is about canonical fidelity rather than recovered diacritics: the temple fixes the name in its scholarly standard spelling, attested in the source tradition, instead of leaving it to drift.

05What is the most important myth about Vasubandhu?

“Three Years in Seclusion” — Vasubandhu was a historical philosopher, not a mythological figure — a monk of Puruṣapura (Peshawar) who, the early Tibetan biographies say, wrote the Abhidharmakośa as a Sarvāstivādin critic and then embraced the Yogācāra Mahāyāna of his half-brother Asaṅga. His 'myths' are the legends his tradition grew around a real life. The full telling lives on the lore page.
