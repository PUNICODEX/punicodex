The Unicode Herald

Vol. 1, No. 1 Q3 2026 Inaugural Issue September 1, 2026

From a Single Question to 542 Temples

Three months after the first domain was registered, PuniCodex restores the original names of myth, place, and sacred tradition across twenty-five pantheons — and invites the world inside.

On 25 May 2026 a single question was written into a registrar search box: why do the gods live in ASCII? The names that had shaped human imagination for millennia — Apóllōn, Hekátē, Śakti, Tōnatiuh — were reduced to plain Latin letters in the digital realm, stripped of stress, length, script, and soul. PuniCodex began as an attempt to give those names back their original Unicode form.

By 1 September that attempt has become 548 flagship temples. Each one is a complete scholarly edition: restored orthography, original script or transliteration, a rules-derived pronunciation, mythology, archaeology, a curated gallery, industry-pattern mappings, a blog, and a scholars tab edited under university sponsorship. The project now spans twenty-five pantheons, from the Olympians to the Aboriginal Dreamtime, and holds 983 lexicon entries across the restored traditions.

This first edition of The Unicode Herald records that transformation. It introduces the new per-pantheon pages and the movie guide, explains how sponsorship and patronage keep the lore free of intrusive advertising, and sets out the goals for the next quarter: hardening the automated sponsorship pipeline, expanding university collaboration, and preparing the Reliquary store and Mythic Duel card game for wider release.
