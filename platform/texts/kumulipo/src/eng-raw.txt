<p><A NAME="page_vii"><FONT SIZE=1 COLOR=GREEN>p. vii</FONT></A></p>  <h1 align="center">INTRODUCTION.</h1>  
<p>THERE are several reasons for the publication of this work, the translation of which pleasantly employed me while imprisoned by the present rulers of Hawaii. It will be to my friends a souvenir of that part of my own life, and possibly it may also be of value to genealogists and scientific men of a few societies to which a copy will be forwarded. The folk-lore or traditions of an aboriginal people have of late years been considered of inestimable value; language itself changes, and there are terms and allusions herein to the natural history of Hawaii, which might be forgotten in future years without some such history as this to preserve them to posterity. Further, it is the special property of the latest ruling family of the Hawaiian Islands, being nothing less than the genealogy in remote times of the late King Kalakaua,--who had it printed in the original Hawaiian language,--and myself.</p>  
<p>This is the very chant which was sung by Puou, the High Priest of our ancient worship, to Captain Cook whom they had surnamed Lono, one of the four chief gods, dwelling high in the heavens, but at times appearing on the earth. This was the cause of the deification of Captain Cook under that name, and of the offerings to him made at the temple or Heiau at Hikiau, Kealakekua, where this song was rendered.</p>  
<p>Captain Cook's appearance was regarded by our people then as a confirmation of their own traditions. For it was prophesied by priests at the time of the death of Ka-I-i-mamao that he, Lono, would return anew from the sea in a Spanish man-of-war or Auwaalalua. To the great navigator they accordingly gave a welcome with the name of Lono.</p>  
<p><A NAME="page_ix"><FONT SIZE=1 COLOR=GREEN>p. ix</FONT></A></p>  
<p>The chanters of this great poem were Hewahewa and Ahukai, and by them it was originally dedicated to Alapai, our ancestress, a woman-chief of the highest rank, then at Koko Oahu. Keeaumoku was lying on his death-bed. The Lonoikamakahiki, of whom this chant sings so eloquently in our native tongue, is none other than Kalaninuiiamamao (Ka-I-i-mamao). His name was also Lonoikamakahiki. He was thus called by his mother, Lonomaikanaka, from the very moment of his birth. It was his grandmother Keakealani who changed his name at the time he was dedicated to the gods and the sacred tabus of the Wela, Hoano, and the Moe; or, translated, Fire, Honor, and Adoration were conferred upon him at the time when his navel string was cut at the Heiau at Nueku, Kahaluu, Kona, Hawaii. The correct name of this chief was Ka-I-i-mamao, but the bards of his day named him in their chants Kalaninuiiamamao; thus he was styled in their Mele or chant called Kekoauli-kookea ka lani. The words "lani nui" were simply inserted by them as it was their intention to hand the young prince's name down to posterity in song, while to explain the object of the parents in naming him Ka-I-i-mamao it signified that when Keawe married Lonoma-I-Kanaka it was an infusion of a new royal blood with that of their own royal line of I, the father of Ahu, the grandfather of Lonomaikanaka. By this it was also intended to show that he, that is Ka-I-i-mamao, was above all other I's, for there were many families, descendants of I, a high and powerful chief, and the last term, "mamao," means "far off" and above all the rest of the I's.</p>  
<p>Before he was deposed by the people of Kau he was called Wakea on account of his wicked actions, and, like Wakea, he married his own daughter. By this name he wandered through the wilds of Kahihikolo at Kalihikai, Kalihiwai, and Hanalei, and ultimately became demented. Through all his wanderings he was followed by his faithful attendant and retainer Kapaihi a Hilina.</p>  
<p>This historical character is distinct from the Lonoikamakahiki, son of Keawenuiaumi, the same one who challenged</p>  
<p><A NAME="page_x"><FONT SIZE=1 COLOR=GREEN>p. x</FONT></A></p>  
<p><FONT SIZE=-2 COLOR=GREEN>[paragraph continues]</FONT> Kakuhihewa at checkers, Konane. He figured at a different period. There were really three persons of the name of Lonoikamakahiki; the first of these was the son of Keawenuiaumi; the second was a celebrated hunchback son of Kapulehuwaihele, and belonged to Makakaualii. The third Lonoikamakahiki is the one for whom this prayer was composed. His parents were Keaweikekahialiiokamoku and Lonomaikaamaka. Ka-I-i-mamao was the father of Kalaniopuu, this last-named being ancestor in the third degree of King Kalakaua, who reigned over the Hawaiian kingdom from 1874 to 1891, and of Liliuokalani, who reigned from 1891 to 1893. He was also ancestor in second and third degree to the wife of Kalakaua, at present the dowager Queen Kapiolani.</p>  
<p>It will be seen, therefore, that as connecting the earlier kings of ancient history with the monarchs latest upon the throne this chant is a contribution to the history of the Hawaiian Islands, and as it is the only record of its kind in existence it seemed to me worthy of preservation in convenient form.</p>  
<p>I have endeavored to give the definition of each name as far as it came within my knowledge of words, but in some cases this could not be done because the true signification has been lost. The ancient Hawaiians were astronomers, and the terms used appertained to the heavens, the stars, terrestrial science, and the gods. Curious students will notice in this chant analogies between its accounts of the creation and that given by modern science or Sacred Scripture. As with other religions, our ancient people recognized an all-powerful evil spirit: Mea was the King of Milu as Satan is of the infernal regions, or hell.</p>  
<p>I hope that to some interested in all that pertains to Hawaii, this may give one-half the pleasure which it gave to me in the translation and preparation of the manuscript.</p>  
<p align="right">LILIUOKALANI, <i>of Hawaii</i>,&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;<br> <i>Patron of the Polynesian Historical Society</i>.</p>

</p>  <h1 align="center">HE PULE HOOLAA ALII.</h1>  
<p align="center">_____________________________________</p>  
<p>(An ancient prayer for the dedication of the high chief Lonoikamakahiki to the gods, soon after his birth, and at which time the honors of Kapu, Wela, Hoano, and Moe were conferred on him by his father, Keaweikekahialiiokamoku, King of Hawaii. After the ceremony his name was changed to Ka I&nbsp;i Mamao.)</p>  
<p align="center">HE KUMULIPO</p>  
<p align="center">(THE CREATION)</p>  
<p align="center">FOR</p>  
<p align="center">KA I&nbsp;I MAMAO.</p>  
<p align="center">FROM HIM TO HIS DAUGHTER, ALAPAI WAHINE.</p>  
<p align="center">_____________________________________</p>  <h3 align="center" align="center">THE FIRST ERA, OR AGE.</h3>  <h4 align="center">FIRST VERSE.</h4>  
<p>At the time that turned the heat of the earth,<br> At the time when the heavens turned and changed,<br> At the time when the light of the sun was subdued<br> To cause light to break forth,<br> At the time of the night of Makalii (winter)<br> Then began the slime which established the earth,<br> The source of deepest darkness.<br> Of the depth of darkness, of the depth of darkness,<br> Of the darkness of the sun, in the depth of night,<br> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It is night,<br> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;So was night born.</p>  
<p><A NAME="page_2"><FONT SIZE=1 COLOR=GREEN>p. 2</FONT></A></p>  <h4 align="center">SECOND VERSE.</h4>  
<p>Kumulipo was born in the night, a male.<br> Poele was born in the night, a female.<br> A coral insect was born, from which was born perforated coral.<br> The earth worm was born, which gathered earth into mounds,<br> From it were born worms full of holes.<br> The starfish was born, whose children were born starry.<br> The phosphorous was born, whose children were born phosphorescent.<br> The Ina was born Ina (sea egg).<br> The Halula was born Halula (sea urchin).<br> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;<i>Shell-fish</i>.<br> The Hawae was born, the Wana-ku was its offspring.<br> The Haukeuke was born, the Uhalula was its offspring.<br> The Pioe was born, the Pipi was its offspring (clam oyster).<br> The Papaua was born, the Olepe was its offspring (pearl and oyster).<br> The Nahawele was born, the Unauna was its offspring (muscle and crab in a shell).<br> The Makaiaulu was born, the Opihi was its offspring.<br> The Leho was born, the Puleholeho was its offspring (cowry).<br> The Naka was born, its offspring was Kupekala (rock oysters).<br> The Makaloa was born, the Pupuawa was its offspring.<br> The Ole was born, the Oleole was its offspring (conch).<br> The Pipipi was born, the Kupee was its offspring (limpets).<br> <br> Kane was born to Waiololi, a female to Waiolola.<br> The Wi was born, the Kiki was its offspring.<br> The Akaha's home was the sea;<br> Guarded by the Ekahakaha that grew in the forest.<br> A night of flight by noises<br> Through a channel; water is life to trees;<br> So the gods may enter, but not man.</p>  
<p><A NAME="page_3"><FONT SIZE=1 COLOR=GREEN>p. 3</FONT></A></p>  <h4 align="center">THIRD VERSE.</h4>  
<p>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;<i>Seaweed and grasses</i><br> Man by Waiololi, woman by Waiolola,<br> The Akiaki was born and lived in the sea;<br> Guarded by the Manienie Akiaki that grew in the forest.<br> A night of flight by noises<br> Through a channel; water is life to trees;<br> So the gods may enter, but not man.</p>  <h4 align="center">FOURTH VERSE.</h4>

<p><A NAME="page_6"><FONT SIZE=1 COLOR=GREEN>p. 6</FONT></A></p>  <h3 align="center" align="center">THE SECOND ERA.</h3>  <h4 align="center">FIRST VERSE.</h4>  
<p>The first child born of Powehiwehi (dusky night)<br> Tossed up land for Pouliuli (darkest night),<br> For Mahiuma or Maapuia,<br> And lived in the land of Pohomiluamea (sloughy hill of Mea);<br> Suppressed the noise of the growth of unripe fruit,<br> For fear Uliuli would cause it to burst, and the stench<br> To disagree and turn sour,<br> For pits of darkness and pits of night.<br> Then the seven waters became calm.<br> Then was born a child (kama), &#8217;twas a Hilu and swam.<br> The Hilu is a fish with standing fins,<br> On which Pouliuli sat.<br> So undecided seemed Powehiwehi,<br> For Pouliuli was husband<br> And Powehiwehi his wife.<br> <i>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fish</i>.<br> And fish was born, the Naia (porpoise) was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Mano (shark) was born, the Moana was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Mau was born, the Maumau was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Nana was born, the Mana was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Nake was born, the Make was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Napa was born, the Nala was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Pala was born, the Kala was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Paka (an eel) was born, the Papa (crab) was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Kalakala was born, the Huluhulu was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Halahala was born, the Palapala was born in the sea and swam. <A NAME="page_7"><FONT SIZE=1 COLOR=GREEN>p. 7</FONT></A><br> The Pea (starfish) was born, the Lupe was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Ao was born, the Awa was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Aku (bonito) was born, the Ahi (same kind) was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Opelu (same as above) was born, the Akule was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Amaama (mullet) was born, the Anae (large kind) was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Ehu was born, the Nehu was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Iao (used for bait) was born, the Aoao was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Ono (large fish) was born, the Omo was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Pahau (striped flatfish) was born, the Lauhau was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Moi was born, the Loiloi was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Mao was born, the Maomao was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Kaku was born, the A'ua'u was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Kupou was born, the Kupoupou was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Weke was born, the Lele was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Palani was born, the Nuku Moni was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Ulua was born, the Hahalua was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Aoaonui was born, the Pakukui was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Maiii was born, the Alaihi was born in the sea and swam.<br> The Oo was born, the Akilolo was born in the sea and swam.</p>  
<p><A NAME="page_8"><FONT SIZE=1 COLOR=GREEN>p. 8</FONT></A></p>  <h4 align="center">SECOND VERSE.</h4>

<p>He was the man and she the woman;<br> The man that was born in the dark age,<br> And the woman was born in the age of bubbles.<br> The sea spread, the land spread,<br> The waters spread, the mountains spread,<br> The Poniu grew tall with advancing time,<br> The Haha grew and had nine leaves,<br> And the Palai (fern) sprout that shot forth leaves of high chiefs<br> Brought forth Poeleele, a man (darkness),<br> Who lived with Pohaha, a woman (bubbles),<br> And brought forth generations of Haha (kalo tops).<br> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The Haha was born.</p>  <h4 align="center">SECOND VERSE.</h4>  
<p>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;<i>Insects</i>.<br> The Haha was born and became parent;<br> His offspring, a Hahalelelele, was born.<br> The Peelua (caterpillar) was born and became parent;<br> Its offspring was a flying Pulelehua (butterfly).<br> The Naonao (an ant) was born and became parent;<br> Its offspring was a Pinao (dragonfly).<br> The &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;<i>Birds</i>.<br> The Hualua was born and became parent;<br> Its offspring was a bird, and flew.<br> The Ulili was born and became parent (snipe);<br> Its offspring was a Kolea, and flew (plover).<br> The A-o was born and became parent;<br> Its offspring was an Au, and flew (a species of plover).<br> The Akekeke was born and became parent (sea-bird);<br> Its offspring was Elepaio, and flew (woodpecker). <A NAME="page_13"><FONT SIZE=1 COLOR=GREEN>p. 13</FONT></A><br> The Alae was born and became parent <p>Established in the dawn of Laa's light<br> The Ape aumoa with faintest strife<br> Envied the sea that washed the land,<br> As it crept up and yet crept down<br> And brought forth creeping families<br> That crept on their backs and crept on their front,<br> With pulses that beat in front and rounding backs,<br> With faces in front and claws to feel<br> Of darkness, of darkness,<br> For Kaneaka Papanopano is born (dawn).<br> So Popanopano the man<br> And Polalowehi his wife, <A NAME="page_16"><FONT SIZE=1 COLOR=GREEN>p. 16</FONT></A><br> Man was born to increase--<br> To increase in the night by the thousands.<br> At this age there is a lull--<br> At this age take your children to the beach.<br> Children play at heaping sands.<br> They are the children born of night.<br> Night was born.</p>  <h4 align="center">SECOND VERSE.</h4>  
<p>Night was born of great delight,<br> Night was rolled for the pleasure of gods,<br> Night gave birth to the split-back turtle.<br> Watch in the night for the land turtle.<br> Night gave birth to the brown lobster,<br> The night of commotion for the <A HREF="errata.htm#0">Alii (?)</A> lobster,<br> The birth night of the lazy monster<br> Was a wet night for the rolling monster.<br> Night gave birth to clinging beings,<br> And Night loudly called for roughness.<br> Night gave birth to wailing<br> A night of drawback to oblivion,<br> Night gave birth to high noses,<br> Night dug deep for jelly fish,<br> Night gave birth to slush,<br> So the night must wait for motion.</p>  <h4 align="center">THIRD VERSE.</h4>

<p>Polaa (sacred night) was born.<br> Then Storm was born, the Tide was born,<br> The Crash was born, and also bursts of bubbles.<br> Confusion was born, also rushing, rumbling, shaking earth.<br> The sea became calm and mountains rose;<br> Water became lakes and formed houses;<br> With great awe the posts rose,<br> And shouts ascended when the spear of Kauikaho was flung.<br> He wrestled with Kanaloa, Kanikahoe.<br> Second night was born in the presence of Wakea.<br> Stormy night was born.<br> Plenty was born.<br> A fowl was born on the back of Wakea.<br> Then Kupololiilialiimuaoloipo died;<br> The Aukaha Opikokahonua died; he was a warrior.<br> The leaves bore and disappeared,<br> Disappeared in the darkest night.</p>

<p>Maui was the man and Hinakealohaila was the wife.<br> Nanamoa was the man and Hinakapaikua the wife.<br> Kulai was the man and Hinahoopaia the wife.<br> Nanakuae was the man, Keauhonua the wife.<br> Kapawa was the man, Kukuluhiokalani the wife.<br> Heleipawa was the man, Kookookumaikalani the wife.<br> Hulumalailena was the man, Hinamaikalani the wife.<br> Aikanaka was the man, Hinaaiakamalama the wife.<br> Pumainua was born, then Hema and Puna last of all.<br> Hema snatched the goblet in the scuffle with Luamahaheau.<br> Kahainui of Hema was born, Hinauluohia the wife.<br> Wahieloa the man, Hoolaukahili the wife.<br> Laka the man, Hikawaolena the wife.<br> Luanuu the man, Kapokuleiula the wife.<br> Kamea the man, Popomaile the wife.<br> Pohukaina the man, Huahuakapolei the wife.<br> Hua the man, Hikiiluna the wife.<br> Paunuikaikeanaina the man, Manokalililani the wife.<br> Huanuiekalalailaikai the man, Kapoea the wife.<br> Paunuikuakaolokea the man, Kapuhookia the wife.<br> Haho the man, Kauwilaianapu the wife.<br> Palena the man, Hikawainui the wife.<br> Hanalaanui was born, Hanalaaiki was born.<br> Hanalaaiki the man, Kapukapu was born.<br> Mauiloa the man, Kauhua was born.<br> Alau the man, Moeikeana was born.<br> Kanunokokuhelii the man, Keikauhale.</p>  <table border="0" width="100%">  <tr>   <td valign="top" align="top" width="327">   
<p>Lonomai   (heard from) was the man.<br>   Wakalana (time of straining) was the man.<br>   Alo (dodge) was the man.<br>   Kaheka (ditches).<br>   Mapuleo.<br>   Paukei.</p>   </td>   <td valign="top" align="top" width="327">   
<p>Kolu   (third) the wife.<br>   Kawai (the water) the wife.<br>   Puia the wife.<br>   Mailou the wife.<br>   Kamaeokalani.<br>   Painalea.</p>   </td>  </tr> </table>

<p>Kawaukaohele was born, also Keleanuinohoanaapiapi,--<br> The woman that lived at Kalamakua,<br> From whence Laielohelohe was born and who married<br> Piilani. Piikea was born and married Umi; to<br> Kumalaenuia Umi, who owned those precipices from whence slaves were held.<br> Kumalaenui of Umi was the husband of Kunuunuipuawalau.<br> Their son, Makua, was the only high chief (wohi kukahi) of the island. Kapohelemai, his wife, whose rank as sacred wohi Alii and Honor. So their heir I, the I of the Kingdom,<br> Whose power and right to execute,<br> And lord of the famed lands of Pakini,<br> Of the sliding Ohia and the weaving of the islands of Hawaii<br> To Ahu--to Ahu of I, of Lono,<br> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Of Lonoikamakahiki.</p>
