STRATA

An evidence-guided journey through the historical layers of the pantheons — from the deep antiquity of Sahul to the mythic geography of the modern map.

01

stratum 01 — the foundation · Human presence in Sahul c. 65,000 years ago; diverse living traditions to the present

The Dreamtime

Archaeological evidence dates human occupation of Sahul to approximately 65,000 years ago. It does not demonstrate one unchanged religious system for that entire period. Aboriginal traditions are diverse, living bodies of knowledge sustained through Country, ceremony, and intergenerational transmission; specific histories belong to their communities.

RegionAustralia

TonguePama–Nyungan and non-Pama–Nyungan isolates

ScriptNo writing — the archive is the land itself: songlines, rock art, ceremony.

Substanceochre bedrock — the deepest layer, the foundation stone

Unlike traditions reconstructed principally through texts, many Aboriginal traditions remain living responsibilities to Country and community. Their extraordinary depth deserves precision: continuity of practice and transmission must not be flattened into a claim of identical beliefs across 65,000 years.

02

stratum 02 of 25 · c. 3200–539 BCE

The First Writing

The first attested pantheon anywhere: Sumerian theonyms appear in cuneiform by the Early Dynastic period; Akkadian, Babylonian and Assyrian hands continue the same gods for two and a half millennia.

RegionSumer and Akkad — Iraq

TongueSumerian (isolate) and Akkadian (Semitic)

ScriptCuneiform — the script that taught the world to write: 𒀭 dingir, the divine determinative, marks every name of a god.

Substancefired river silt — the first tablets

Here the gods first learned to be written down. Enki's me — the divine decrees of civilization — is humanity's first theory of what a god is for: the custody of the arts, one by one.

  • the seven-day week
  • the 60-minute hour
  • the flood narrative the Hebrew Bible inherits
  • epic itself — Gilgameš is the first hero

03

stratum 03 of 25 · c. 3100 BCE – 4th century CE

The Everlasting

Three thousand years of continuous state cult — the Pyramid Texts (c. 2350 BCE) are the world's oldest large religious corpus, still legible to the last Egyptian temples.

RegionThe Nile Valley

TongueEgyptian (Afro-Asiatic)

ScriptHieroglyphs — 𓇋𓏏𓆑 for Thoth, 𓂀 the Eye — a writing system that kept its sacred character for three millennia.

Substancesandstone and gold leaf

The Egyptian answer to death was the most confident ever built: not an end but a change of address. Osiris dies and rules the dead — the first god whose resurrection is the guarantee of every follower's own.

04

stratum 04 of 25 · c. 1500 BCE — present

The Vedic Current

The Rigveda was composed in stages, conventionally c. 1500–1200 BCE, and sustained through elaborate oral transmission with remarkable fidelity over many centuries. It remains a living religious text. The Indus inheritance beneath it is older still, and still unread.

RegionThe Indian subcontinent

TongueSanskrit (Indo-Aryan)

ScriptVedic oral transmission predates its scripts; later Devanagari: अग्नि Agni, ॐ Om.

Substancemantra-etched stone

No tradition has carried its oldest hymns across so much time with so little loss. The Vedic fire god Agni is still worshipped at the dawn of every Hindu wedding — four thousand years after his hymns were composed.

  • a billion living practitioners
  • deva → 'divine'; the numerals the world counts with

The steppe carried a language family from the Pontic grasslands into the Indus world — and the hymns met gods already there.

05

stratum 05 of 25 · c. 1650–1180 BCE

The Anatolian Synthesis

The Hittite archives of Ḫattuša — the largest cuneiform library of the Bronze Age — record a deliberately syncretic state pantheon: Hattic, Hurrian and Mesopotamian gods given ranks and dossiers.

RegionAnatolia — Türkiye

TongueHittite (Anatolian Indo-European)

ScriptCuneiform in Hittite hands; hieroglyphic Luwian on the monuments.

Substancebasalt relief

The Hittites ran the Bronze Age's most deliberate theology: a pantheon as a civil service, with thousand-year-old Sumerian gods retained in the register alongside their own. When their empire fell, the archive baked hard in the fire that destroyed it — and waited three thousand years to be read.

Anatolia was a crossroads before it was an empire: Hattic earth-gods, Hurrian storm-gods, Mesopotamian scribes — one register.

06

stratum 06 of 25 · c. 1400–1200 BCE (tablets)

The Ugaritic Tablets

The Baal Cycle, the Epic of Aqhat and the myth of El's banquet survive on tablets from Ugarit — a single generation before the city burned, preserving the Levantine pantheon whole.

RegionThe Levant — coastal Syria

TongueUgaritic (Northwest Semitic)

ScriptThe Ugaritic alphabet — 𐎁 bēt, 𐎍 lāmed — the first alphabet ordered in the order we still use.

Substancefired clay tablet

Ugarit gave us the alphabet's ordering and the storm-god's biography in the same library. Baal's battle with Prince Sea is the Canaanite answer to the question every coast asks: who holds back the water?

07

stratum 07 of 25 · c. 1200–146 BCE

The Alphabet's Merchants

Continues Canaanite religion westward: Tyre, Sidon, and Carthage, attested in inscriptions, sacrifice lists and hostile Roman testimony from the Tophet to the sack of Carthage.

RegionLevantine coast, then the whole Mediterranean

TonguePhoenician (Northwest Semitic)

ScriptThe Phoenician consonantal alphabet — an ancestor of Greek, Latin, and many later alphabets, alongside the independent writing systems shown elsewhere in this column.

Substancepurple-dyed cedar

The Phoenicians' great export was not cedar or glass but the alphabet itself — twenty-two letters that unshipped the priestly monopoly on reading and let writing travel in merchant hulls.

08

stratum 08 of 25 · c. 1000–600 BCE — present

The Choice

Zarathustra's date is honestly uncertain (mainstream c. 1000–600 BCE; traditional c. 1500 BCE). The Gāthās are the oldest confidently datable religious poetry after the Rigveda, and the faith still keeps its sacred fire.

RegionThe Iranian plateau

TongueAvestan (Iranian)

ScriptAvestan scripture, fixed in a purpose-built alphabet in Sasanian times: 𐬀𐬵𐬎𐬭𐬀 Ahura.

Substancelimestone fire temple

Zarathustra inverted the old order: the gods were not to be bribed but obeyed, and history itself became a battleground of truth against the lie. Every subsequent faith of the single God inherits the question he asked.

What survived

  • the moral engine of the modern world — good against evil, choice as cosmology
  • the magi at Bethlehem; Nowruz on the spring equinox
  • fire temples still burning in Yazd
Open the Choice pantheon → Compare this tradition in Archē →

On the Iranian plateau the old Indo-Iranian gods were re-sorted into a single choice: truth, or the lie.

09

stratum 09 of 25 · Mycenaean roots (c. 1450 BCE) — present

The Canon

Zeus, Poseidon and Dionysos appear in Linear B tablets at Knossos and Pylos — the Greek gods are older than the Greek alphabet. Homer and Hesiod fixed the canon around 750–700 BCE.

RegionThe Aegean, then the Mediterranean world

TongueGreek (Indo-European)

ScriptLinear B → the Greek alphabet: ΑΘΗΝΑ, ΔΙΟΣ — the vowels, the first true alphabet.

SubstanceParian marble

Greece did not invent its gods; it developed an unusually durable argument about them. Hesiod's Theogony offers an early systematic Greek genealogy of the divine world, and Athenian drama gave the gods a public, civic, and literary life.

What survived

  • the planets' names

  • the word 'panic', the word 'music', the word 'chaos'

The Mycenaean tablets remember Zeus three centuries before Homer — Greece inherited its gods and invented the argument about them.

stratum 10 of 25 · c. 753 BCE — 4th century CE

The Adoption

Rome kept its own numina and household gods, then adopted Greece's pantheon wholesale — by interpretatio romana, every foreign god found a Roman desk.

RegionLatium, then the empire

TongueLatin (Indo-European)

ScriptThe Latin alphabet — Rome's most durable province is the alphabet itself.

Substancetravertine and bronze

Rome's genius was administrative, and its theology shows it: it ran a pantheon the way it ran a province — absorb, rename, standardize, endure. The gods of conquered Gaul, Britain and Syria all ended up keeping Rome's books.

stratum 11 of 25 · La Tène (c. 450 BCE) — recorded c. 8th–12th century CE

The Island Memory

The continental Celts left no scripture; the tradition survives through medieval Irish and Welsh manuscripts copying oral matter far older than their inks — the oldest vernacular literature north of the Alps.

RegionGaul to Ireland and Wales

TongueCeltic (Indo-European)

ScriptOgham on the stones (ᚠᚢᚏᚐᚉᚔᚔᚅᚔᚐ); Latin script for the manuscripts.

Substancetorcs of gold and bog-oak

The Celts trusted memory over inscription, and nearly paid the ultimate price for it: Rome destroyed the druids' schools, and the tradition survived only where Rome never ruled — Ireland's monks, writing out of sheer love of the matter.

The Eddic Record

The gods' names run in runes from the earliest Germanic inscriptions; the myths themselves were fixed in Iceland's Eddas only after most of Scandinavia had converted — a rescue mission in manuscript.

RegionScandinavia and the Norse world

TongueNorth Germanic

ScriptThe runic rows — ᚦ ᚱ ᛟ — and the Latin letters the sagas were written in.

Substancebog iron and carved wood

The Norse gods moved through halls, sacred landscapes, and the skalds' verses. Ragnarök gives their surviving narratives an unusually explicit account of divine death and a renewed earth — an ending that is also a beginning.

13

stratum 13 of 25 · attested 13th–14th century CE; continuity far older

The Last Pagans of Europe

Baltic religion is attested almost entirely by hostile chronicles — Peter of Dusburg, the Polish and Teutonic records — because it was still being practised, at scale, when Christian Europe wrote it down to end it. Lithuania was Europe's last great pagan state.

RegionThe Baltic coast — Lithuania and Latvia

TongueBaltic (Indo-European)

ScriptNo native writing; the chronicles' Latin is our window, and the living Baltic languages our survivors.

Substanceamber and sacred oak

While cathedrals rose across Europe, the Balts still crowned their kings beneath sacred oaks, and crusades were preached against them. Their religion's source is the enemy's diary — read it against the grain.

Christian Europe's frontier ran through sacred oak groves — Lithuania was still pagan when Oxford was teaching theology.

stratum 14 of 25 · attested 6th–12th century CE; practice continuous

The Dual Faith

Procopius names the Slavic gods in the 6th century; medieval chronicles (Kiev, Novgorod, Thietmar of Merseburg) record the cults — and dvoeverie, 'dual faith', kept them alive under Christianity for a thousand years.

RegionEastern Europe

TongueSlavic (Indo-European)

ScriptAttested through Cyrillic chronicles — Перунъ Perun in the Primary Chronicle.

Substancecarved idol wood — Zbruch

The Slavic solution to conversion was not resistance but persistence: the gods withdrew into folklore and came back every festival. Perun's oaths became Perun's curses became, simply, thunder.

stratum 15 of 25 · c. 5th century BCE — present

The Turning of the Wheel

Siddhartha Gautama's life is dated by modern scholarship to c. 5th century BCE; within five centuries the dharma had walked from the Ganges to the Silk Road to Japan — the fastest long-distance spread of any faith before Islam.

RegionSouth Asia → all of Asia

TongueSanskrit and Pali sources; every Asian script follows

ScriptSanskrit sutras in Devanagari and Siddhaṃ; the dharma wheel itself as emblem.

Substancegilt bronze

Buddhism's great move was to export not gods but methods — and then, on arrival, to crown bodhisattvas anyway: Avalokiteśvara, Amitābha, the medicine Buddha. Compassion, personified, walked further than any army.

The wheel turned outward: a teaching from the Ganges walked the Silk Road and became Asia's common tongue of the sacred.

The Middle Kingdom's Register

The Shang kings scratched their questions to the high god Di 帝 into oracle bone — the first Chinese writing and the first Chinese theology arrive together. Three thousand years of continuous record follow.

RegionThe Yellow River to all of China

TongueSinitic

ScriptOracle bone → bronze → seal → the characters used today: 天 heaven, 帝 the Lord, 神 spirit.

Substanceoracle bone and bronze

China's gods were administered as carefully as its provinces: canonized, promoted, merged, and filed. The Jade Emperor's celestial ministry is the longest-running celestial civil service on record.

17

stratum 17 of 25 · Dàodéjīng c. 4th century BCE; organized religion c. 2nd century CE — present

The Way and Its Power

The Dàodéjīng is a short text with an endless afterlife; organized Taoism begins with the Way of the Celestial Masters in 142 CE and has kept registers, revelations and immortals ever since.

RegionChina

TongueSinitic (Classical Chinese scripture)

ScriptThe Eight Trigrams 乾坤震巽坎離艮兌 — diagram as scripture.

Substancecinnabar and talisman paper

Taoism alone refused the question. Where other strata ask 'which gods rule?', the Dàodéjīng answers that the Way that can be named is not the eternal Way — and then, gloriously, goes on to name its immortals anyway.

The Altar of the Three

The founding myth — Hwanin, Hwanung, the bear-woman, and Dangun — is recorded in medieval chronicles carrying far older oral matter; the mudang shamans never stopped practising it.

RegionThe Korean peninsula

TongueKoreanic

ScriptHanja and then Hangul — the alphabet the king invented in 1443 and the shamans adopted at once.

Substancewhite paper and mountain shrine stone

Korea's tradition kept its shamanic spine through every dynasty: three gods in one, a bear who fasted to become a woman, and a founder who lived a thousand years — half bear, half god, half of everything at once.

stratum 19 of 25 · Kojiki 712 CE, Nihon Shoki 720 CE — present

Japan's myths were written down late but remember much older practice; Shinto's kami cults were never interrupted — the same shrines, the same festivals, the same imperial rites, continuously.

TongueJaponic

ScriptMan'yōgana → kana; the sun goddess's name in the oldest script we have: 天照 Amaterasu.

Substancecypress and mirror-bright bronze

Amaterasu's grandson descended to rule Japan, and the imperial line has claimed descent ever since — the only living monarchy on Earth with a goddess for a founder. The mirror that lured her from the cave is one of the Three Treasures of the throne.

stratum 20 of 25 · settlement c. 1000 BCE–1200 CE; continuity unbroken

The Triangle of the Sea

The greatest navigation story ever told — settling a triangle of ocean larger than every continent combined — carried its pantheon by memory across open water: Hawaii to Rapa Nui to Aotearoa, all one star-map.

RegionThe Polynesian triangle

TongueAustronesian

ScriptNo writing until contact — the geneaologies were chanted, syllable by syllable, canoe by canoe.

Substancecarved whalebone and volcanic glass

Every other stratum spread by land or by conquest; this one spread by dead reckoning at night. Tangaroa's ocean, Māui's hooks, Pele's volcanoes — a pantheon compact enough to carry in a canoe and strong enough to hold a tenth of the planet's water.

stratum 21 of 25 · c. 1300–1521 CE (Mexica), on Toltec and Teotihuacan roots centuries older

The Mexica state kept its gods in books — the Borgia, Fejérváry-Mayer and Florentine Codices — and in a temple rebuilt five times at the heart of Tenochtitlan, excavated today under Mexico City's cathedral.

The Mexica believed the fifth world-sun would die by earthquake unless it was fed — and fed it with the one currency that moves the sun: human hearts. The theology was precise, the mathematics superb, and the covenant terrible.

stratum 22 of 25 · Inca state c. 1438–1533 CE; Andean roots to Chavín (c. 900 BCE)

Children of the Sun

The Andes stacked three thousand years of cult before the Inca: Chavín, Tiwanaku, Wari. The Inca wove them into a state religion — Inti the sun, Viracocha the creator, the huacas of every ridge and river — and administered it province by province.

Substanceandesite masonry, fitted without mortar

The Inca built without wheels, mortar, or writing — and ran an empire of ten million with knotted string. Their gods were the mountains themselves, and the mountains are still there.

The People of the Land

Spanish chronicles record the Mapuche cosmos — Pillán the thunder spirits of the volcanoes, Ngen the lords of place, the silver branch — while the machi shamans carried the tradition undefeated through war, mission and dictatorship.

TongueMapudungun (isolate)

The Mapuche held the Araucanía for three centuries against the empire that conquered the Inca and the Aztec — and their religion held with them: a covenant with the land itself, where every river and ridge has its Ngen, and the machi climbs her rewe between worlds.

25

stratum 25 of 25 · c. 8th century BCE — present

When the Land Became Myth

The youngest stratum is not a people but a move: Greece personified its own geography — Asia, Europe, Atlantis, the rivers and cities as beings — and the map itself joined the pantheon.

RegionThe Mediterranean map

TongueGreek

The breach — the strata reach the surface

Every layer is still holding.

Twenty-five strata rise from the Dreamtime to the mythic map — and then they breach the surface, here, now. The days of the week are Norse. The alphabet is Phoenician. The flood is Sumerian. Yoga is Vedic, mindfulness is Buddhist, the obelisk is Egyptian, the map is Greek — and at the foundation, living Aboriginal traditions whose depth must be described with care. The modern world is not built on the ruins of these strata. It is built of them — and the building continues every time a name is carried with greater fidelity. This is a reading path, not a claim of direct descent or identical continuity. Layers are ordered by their earliest secure textual, archaeological, or conventional scholarly anchor; oral transmission is distinguished from written attestation. Dates overlap, ranges remain approximate, and contested interpretations stay visible.
