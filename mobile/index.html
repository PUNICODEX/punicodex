2 Suggestions & temples›

The keyboard carries the whole PuniCodex lexicon. Type any god, goddess or mythological name in ordinary letters, anywhere — Messages, Notes, any app — and the suggestion bar offers the scholarly Unicode spelling.

  • Tap a chip to replace what you typed with the full restoration: apollon → Apóllōn, hera → Hēra, zeus → Zeús.
  • Long-press a chip for the scholarly variant sheet — full restoration, circumflex form, macron-only (LSJ convention) and plain ASCII — each with its punycode domain.
  • Temple card — every suggestion links to its flagship temple on punicodex.com: the domain in Unicode, the name's meaning, pantheon and sources. The keyboard is a doorway into the whole ecosystem.
  • Completions improve as you type — the bar re-ranks with every keystroke and follows your cursor, so you can commit a name mid-sentence.
3 Long-press, everywhere›

Every key on both panels hides a curated variant sheet behind a long-press. Press and hold, slide to the character you want, release.

  • Letters — 5–10 scholarly forms per letter, separately curated for lower and upper case. Hold a for á à â ä ã å ā ă ǎ; hold h for ĥ ḥ ḫ ẖ ħ; hold t for þ; hold s for ß and ṣ. The classics (é, ñ, ü…) are all there.
  • Digits — Roman numerals: hold 1 for Ⅰ, Ⅱ, Ⅲ…
  • Symbols — hold $ for ¢ € £ ¥ ₹; " for “ ” « »; ' for ‘ ’ ′; - for – — −; < for ≤ ≪ ⟨; and so on across the whole symbol panel.
  • Punctuation — . offers …; ? offers ¿ and ‽; , offers its typographic relatives.
  • Your own additions — any variant you pin via the Ω directory joins the sheet in orange, ahead of the defaults. Chapter 5.
4 The Ω directory›

Tap Ω (it lights purple) to open a complete Unicode workbench: 7,728 characters in 49 categories — Greek, Coptic and Glagolitic scripts, runes, Egyptian hieroglyphs, cuneiform, Linear B, Gothic, Ogham, Phoenician, Baybayin, math symbols, arrows, technical signs, braille, alchemical sigils, dominoes, enclosed alphanumerics, combining marks and more. Every cell shows the glyph, its name and its U+ code point.

  • Insert — tap any character: it is typed into your field and the directory closes. The keyboard stays live while the directory is open.
  • Categories — chips along the top. The directory remembers the category you were in and reopens there, even days later.
  • All view — the first 1,000 characters, fronted by the categories you pin in the customizer box (up to 6, your order). Chapter 5.
  • Search — focus the search field and type with this keyboard; results match names, keywords and code points.
  • Multi-select — the Multi-select toggle (or long-press Ω) queues characters instead of inserting: build a string, then Copy, Insert or Clear from the buffer bar.
  • Move & resize — drag the PuniCodex Directory header to reposition; − / + resize the grid; ◐ toggles the see-through background so you can read the field behind it. Size and opacity are remembered.
  • Collapse — ▼ (in the suggestion bar) hides the key rows while keeping suggestions live, giving the directory room; ▲ brings the keys back.
  • Close — tap Ω again (it glows purple while the directory is open). The keyboard keeps working the whole time the directory is up.
5 Make it yours›
  • Pin any character to any key — in the Ω directory, long-press the character you want. The keyboard glows orange and a banner shows what is pending. Tap the target key and the character joins that key's long-press sheet (letters pin to both cases). Tap ✕ to cancel.
  • Remove a custom variant — long-press the key, then long-press the orange variant in its sheet and confirm Delete. Built-in defaults are permanent; only your additions can be removed.
  • Customise the All view — in the directory's All starts with box, tap up to 6 categories to pin them (and their order) to the front of All. Untap to release. Your arrangement is saved.
  • Everything persists — pins, deletions, All arrangement, directory size, opacity and last category all survive restarts and updates.
6 Numbers & symbols›

123 swaps to the symbols panel: digits, brackets, currency marks, operators and arrows — every single-character key with its own long-press sheet (Roman numerals on digits, currency on $, typographic quotes, ≤ ≥ ≠ ≈ on the operators). ABC returns to letters; ⇧ on that panel cycles its shifted set; Ω works there too.
