The Doctrine

THE TIER SYSTEM

A simple philological rule: the more phonology the original language lets us recover, the richer the Unicode restoration. Greek names are measured by whether they preserve stress and length — the two features ancient Greek used to distinguish words. Every other tradition is measured by whether its restoration keeps the distinctive letters and diacritics that ASCII erases.

Multiple Valid Restorations

The Greek original carries both stress and length, and history has handed down more than one defensible Unicode spelling. Each variant is a real restoration, not a stylistic alias.

  • Both acute stress and at least one long vowel
  • Multiple historically valid spellings
  • Each variant owns a real domain

Full Restoration

Every feature of the original phonology survives in the Unicode form. For Greek names, that means the original carries both stress and length, with one dominant scholarly spelling. For every other tradition, it means at least one distinctive letter or diacritic is preserved that ASCII would erase — a macron, a tone mark, an underdot, a þ or ð.

  • Greek: stress and length both preserved
  • Other traditions: ≥1 distinctive feature kept
  • The philologically faithful form of the name
Zeús Athénā Óðinn
Tier 2 332 entries
´ or ¯

Partial Restoration

Only one feature survives, or none. For Greek names, the original offers only stress, only length, or only a circumflex — the Unicode form preserves that single feature, the standard convention of LSJ, Cambridge, and Oxford editions. For other traditions, the faithful form happens to need no distinctive letters at all — what survives is the name itself.

  • Greek: one feature preserved, not both
  • Other traditions: no distinctive feature lost to ASCII
  • Still a defensible scholarly orthography
Ártemis Diónysos Baldr

The Science

Two Marks, One System

Ancient Greek was a pitch-accent language. Words carried a melody and distinguished long vowels from short. Our tiers map these lost features to Unicode depth.

´

Stress

The acute (´) marks rising pitch on a short vowel. The circumflex (ˆ) combines stress and length on one syllable.

Ζεύς → Zeús
Ποσειδῶν → Poseidôn
¯

Length

Greek distinguished long from short vowels. η and ω are always long; other long vowels are marked with macrons (¯).

Ἥρα → Hēra
Ἀφροδίτη → Aphrodítē
´ + ¯

Both Together

When Greek carries both features, the transliteration preserves the full scholarly orthography — every recoverable phonological detail.

Ἥφαιστος → Hēphaistos
Προμηθεύς → Promētheus
