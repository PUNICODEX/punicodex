The Doctrine

THE TIER SYSTEM

Greek prosody determines restoration depth. Two phonological features — stress and vowel length — define three tiers of scholarly authenticity. Every archetype in the pantheon is classified according to what survives from the original language.

The Science

Greek Prosody

Ancient Greek was a pitch-accent language. Every word carried a melody — rising and falling tones — and distinguished long vowels from short. Our tier system maps these lost features to Unicode restoration depth.

´

Stress

The acute (´) marks rising pitch on a short vowel. The circumflex (ˆ) combines stress and length on one syllable. The grave (`) is a neutralized acute.

Ζεύς → Zeús acute on short
Ποσειδῶν → Poseidôn circumflex
¯

Length

Greek distinguished long from short vowels. η (ē) is always long; ω (ō) is always long. Long α, ι, υ exist but are harder to detect. We mark them with macrons (¯).

Ἥρα → Hēra η = long e
Ἀφροδίτη → Aphrodītē ῑ + η
´ + ¯

Both Together

When the Greek original carries both features, the transliteration preserves the full scholarly orthography. This is the philological ideal — every recoverable phonological detail retained.

Ἥφαιστος → Hēphaistos length + stress
Προμηθεύς → Promētheus length + stress

The Classification

Three Tiers of Authenticity

Tier 1 24 archetypes
´ + ¯

Acute Stress + Long Vowel

Full Restoration

The Greek original carries both an acute stress mark (or circumflex) and at least one long vowel. The transliteration preserves the full scholarly orthography.

These names have only one valid Unicode restoration. Their ASCII fallbacks are modern English spellings — not ancient canonical forms.

Tier 2 26 archetypes
´ or ¯

Stress Only  or  Length Only

Partial Restoration

The Greek original carries only one feature — either acute stress or a long vowel, but not both. The transliteration preserves whichever feature remains.

These are still authentic scholarly orthographies, but they represent a partial restoration of the original phonological system.

Dual-Tier 4 archetypes
´ + ¯ ×2

Both Features × Multiple Valid Forms

The Exceptional Four

These four archetypes have multiple historically valid Unicode spellings because their Greek originals allow alternate stress positions, alternate long-vowel interpretations, or dialectal variation.

Their ASCII fallbacks are also historically legitimate. You own multiple domain variants. This is not a bug — it is a linguistic feature of these names.

Methodology

Scholarly Sources

Every restoration is grounded in established philological reference. We do not invent accents — we recover them.

LSJ

Liddell-Scott-Jones

The standard Greek-English lexicon. Our macron conventions follow LSJ practice.

Beekes

Beekes Etymological Dictionary

Indo-European etymology and reconstructed forms for Greek names.

Cambridge

Standard reference for accentuation rules and vowel quantity.

Oxford

Faulkner's Egyptian Dictionary

For Egyptian names and transliteration conventions.

Smyth Greek Grammar

The American standard for Greek accent and quantity.
